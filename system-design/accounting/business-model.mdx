## The pain, stated the way Kens stated it

> *Many customers and tenants have their own system as separate - accounting system, POS system, payroll and etc. If we can bind accounting and POS together, maybe those kind of customer can switch to us.*

**That is the whole thesis, and it is a displacement thesis, not a feature thesis.**

The customer we are looking at is not "a restaurant that wants accounting". It is **a business running three or four separate subscriptions that do not talk to each other**, paying for the privilege of re-typing the same numbers by hand every month. The pain is not a missing feature - it is **the friction between the systems**.

**And the friction is where the money is.** Anyone can sell a chart of accounts. Nobody else can say: *your POS already knows what happened, and it is already writing to us.*

### The three customers this creates

| The customer | What they run today | What they want | How we reach them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The F&B operator** | our POS + a spreadsheet + a payroll package | to stop re-typing the month | the POS add-on - the wedge revenue |
| **The fragmented-stack owner** | FeedMe or AutoCount + a separate accounting package + payroll | to replace three subscriptions with one | the books-first switch (§3) |
| **The accountant** | a practice managing 20 clients across 4 accounting systems | one login, many books | the accountant workspace (later) |

**The accountant is the sticky party, and the operator is the buyer.** That distinction drives everything in §4.

## The packaging - what we sell, and when

### 2.1 Two shapes on one billing spine

The website API is the single billing and entitlement system (`website_api/app/models/models.py`): `WebsiteCustomer` (`:70`), `Subscription` (`:95`), `SubscriptionPlan` (`:6`), `Feature` (`:228`), `PlanFeature` (`:245`), `FeaturePrice` (`:258`), `CustomerFeatureOverride` (`:299`).

**Shape 1 - the add-on. This is how the wedge revenue arrives - and the wedge revenue is the thesis.**

A new `Feature` row - `accounting` - with `FeaturePrice` rows per currency. This is exactly the shape the two AI add-ons already use (`ai_agent` at MYR 80 / USD 20 monthly, MYR 800 / USD 200 annual; `ai_agent_actions` at MYR 240 / USD 50 monthly, MYR 2400 / USD 500 annual). **A POS tenant buys the add-on and the accounting service grants it.**

**Why this shape first, and it is not a compromise:** the entitlement is a `plan_features` row and the plan assignment is **data, not code** - the same rule the delivery module already follows (`delivery_service.py:50`). The accounting service asks the website API for its entitlements exactly as the POS does (`fetch_plan_features`, `backend/app/services/plan_features.py:71`, over the internal key at `core/config.py:148`).

**But an earlier draft of this document claimed this shape needed "no new checkout code, no new plan rows, no new subscription logic". That was wrong, and the live code says so plainly.** The *entitlement* half of the shape is already built and needs nothing. The *purchase* half does not exist:

- **Nothing in the codebase sells a `FeaturePrice`.** The only routes that read one are admin routes (`website_api/app/routes/admin.py:1045`, `:1081`, `:1121`, `:1189`); the only route that grants a feature to a customer writes a `CustomerFeatureOverride` as an admin (`admin.py:987`, `:1011`). **The two AI add-ons are granted by an admin, not bought by a customer** - so the add-on as a *purchase* is new behaviour for the whole platform, not just for accounting.
- **Every Stripe checkout prices a plan or an invoice, never a feature.** `website_api/app/routes/subscription.py:464` (subscription), `:834` (upgrade), `:1483` (proration), `website_api/app/routes/billing.py:436` (invoice payment).
- **So the add-on needs four pieces of new work, all of it Phase 0:** a checkout that accepts a `FeaturePrice` and resolves its price for the right currency and billing cycle; a webhook that grants the entitlement on `payment_succeeded`; the invoice record; and the cancellation/dunning path. Without them, "a POS tenant buys the add-on" is a sentence in a design document rather than something a customer can do.

**What does NOT need to change is the billing catalogue itself.** `FeaturePrice` rows per currency are data, and the house rule (the live database is the truth, the seed only starts a fresh install) already covers them.

**Shape 2 - the standalone product. Later in the sequence, and it is the more valuable sale.**

New `subscription_plans` rows for the accounting product, distinguished by a new **`product` discriminator** (`'pos'` / `'accounting'`) so two catalogues live under one table without mixing. A tenant with no POS subscribes to an accounting plan and never touches a POS plan.

**The discriminator is a schema change, and so are two of its consequences.** `SubscriptionPlan` has no `product` column today (`website_api/app/models/models.py:6`); adding one is Phase 0 work, not Phase 6 work, because the accounting catalogue has to be publishable from the start. `is_published` already exists on the plan (`models.py:25`), so publishing and unpublishing an accounting plan needs no new column.

**The existing checkout cannot be reused for this shape as it stands.** `create-checkout` hard-codes `'currency': 'usd'` and names the product `"IDEAZES POS"` (`website_api/app/routes/subscription.py:839`, `:841`) - a standalone Malaysian accounting plan sold through it would be charged in dollars. The currency-resolving path is the other one (`subscription.py:464`, resolving at `:471` via `resolve_price_safe`, `website_api/app/services/plan_pricing.py:115`), and a standalone checkout resolves the plan's price the same way - **the price comes from the plan's own price rows, never from a hard-coded literal.**

**The signup cannot be reused either.** Every website customer is auto-provisioned a POS account (`website_api/app/routes/auth.py:143-161` -> `provision_pos_account`), again on subscription creation (`website_api/app/routes/subscription.py:577`) and again in the webhook (`website_api/app/routes/webhooks.py:683`). **Today it is impossible to be a customer without a POS tenant** - which is the opposite of the tenant model this proposal sells. The provisioning becomes conditional on the product bought, and `WebsiteCustomer.restaurant_name` - `nullable=False` (`models.py:77`) - stops being the right question for an accountant buying books for a company.

**The house rule holds throughout:** the database is the truth and the seed is only what a fresh install starts with - `website_api/app/core/ai_addon_prices.py` says so in as many words, with a `--check` migration that reports the drift. **Every price in this document is a proposal for a seed, not a hard-coded value.**

### 2.2 The sequence

| Phase | What is sellable | Who buys it | What it proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0-1** | the books, entered by hand or by API, with the statements | an accountant or an owner who wants the books | the engine is correct - the trial balance balances |
| **2** | **the add-on: our POS writes the books** | **our existing POS tenants** | **the wedge: zero re-keying, and it is demonstrable in one screen** |
| **3-5** | purchases, the bank, receivables, the year-end close | the same tenants, now on a real accounting package | the books are complete enough to run the business on |
| **6** | **the books-first switch**: bring a third-party POS's sales in | **the fragmented-stack owner** | **the displacement: the tenant switches without switching their POS** |
| **7** | the accountant workspace, the payroll posting, the bank feed | the accountant | the suite - one login, many books |

**Two first revenues, and the difference between them matters.** The live-code audit found that the add-on had **no purchase path at all** - no customer-facing checkout for a `FeaturePrice`, no payment webhook that grants a feature, no invoice for one. So Phase 0 now builds the purchase machinery, which means **the standalone accounting plan is sellable from Phase 1**: a tenant with no POS subscribes and pays. That is the first revenue, and it is money from a customer the POS could never have had.

**But the gate that decides the business is still Phase 2.** The add-on - our POS writing an existing POS tenant's books with nobody typing - is the **proof that the binding is worth paying for**, and it is the only thing that validates the displacement thesis before Phase 6 spends a lot of build on it. **The critical discipline: Phase 2 must sell before Phase 6 is built.** If the add-on does not sell to tenants we already have, the displacement story to tenants we do not have is a hypothesis with a large build cost behind it. **The phase gate is a revenue gate, not a completion gate** - and Phase 1 selling is a hedge, Phase 2 selling is the thesis.

### 2.3 The pricing - a proposal

**These are proposals, anchored to two live facts:** the AI add-ons' price pattern (MYR and USD rows, monthly and annual, annual at ten months' price), and the live `subscription_plans` rows, which are currently **Starter USD 0, Basic USD 10, Pro USD 20, Enterprise USD 50** - note that the seed file says 99 / 199 / 399 / 799 in MYR while the **live database has been changed**, which is exactly the drift the `--check` migration is there to catch.

**The add-on (sold to POS tenants):**

| Currency | Monthly | Annual |
|---|---|---|
| MYR | 149 | 1,490 |
| USD | 35 | 350 |

**The standalone plans (later):**

| Plan | MYR / month | USD / month | What it includes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Accounting Starter** | 99 | 25 | one outlet, the books, the statements, manual entry only |
| **Accounting Basic** | 199 | 50 | one legal entity, the POS connector, the bank, receivables |
| **Accounting Pro** | 349 | 90 | multiple legal entities, the third-party POS connector, the Xero / Bukku push, the bring-across tools |

**The reasoning, briefly.**

- **The add-on is priced above the standalone Starter** because the bundle is worth more than the book: the tenant gets the POS integration for free with it. **The standalone Starter has no integration** - it is the book alone - so it must be cheap enough to be bought without one.
- **MYR and USD rows exist because the price list is per-currency** (`FeaturePrice`), and because the multi-currency rule forbids summing across them. A Malaysian tenant pays MYR; a Cambodian or Singaporean tenant pays USD.
- **Annual at ten months** matches the existing AI add-ons. Accounting is a sticky product and an annual commitment is the right shape.
- **The pricing is deliberately below Bukku's and Xero's entry point**, because we are not selling an accountant's software - we are selling *the books, already filled in*. The incumbent charges for the software; we charge for the outcome.

**What is deliberately NOT in the price:** a per-transaction or per-seat fee. The whole value claim is that the tenant does nothing. A price that scales with the tenant's activity punishes exactly the thing we are selling.

### 2.4 What the add-on is worth, in the tenant's own numbers

**The pricing has to be smaller than the re-keying it replaces.** A tenant with 200 covers a day and a month-end that takes two days of someone's time has a re-keying cost of **RM 1,000 and up** in labour, plus the errors. Against that:

- the add-on at MYR 149 is **under 15% of the labour it removes**;
- the errors it removes are the expensive ones - a discount posted as revenue, a tax line mis-split, a void never reversed;
- and the tenant gets a profit and loss statement they did not have before.

**That comparison is the sales pitch, and it is a factual one, not a hype one.** The number to have ready is the tenant's own re-keying hours, which their accountant will already know.

## The displacement thesis - the part that is actually new

### 3.1 The incumbent stack

`malaysia-e-invoice.mdx` records the market's shape: the typical F&B pattern is **FeedMe POS + AutoCount or Bukku + a separate MyInvois bridge**, and the POS itself rarely speaks MyInvois. Every one of those is a subscription, and every hand-off between them is a place where a number gets re-typed.

**The tenant's cost is not the software. It is the seams.**

### 3.2 The wedge: books first, POS later

**The one thing the old design could not do was serve a tenant who kept their POS.** Every accounting feature was behind our POS. So the only tenant we could reach was one already committed to our point of sale - which meant the fragmented-stack owner was unreachable, because switching their POS is a big, risky, visible decision.

**The standalone architecture changes the order of the decision:**

| Step | What moves | Why the tenant accepts it |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The books** | their accounting moves to us, their POS stays | they keep the floor exactly as it is; nothing operational changes; the re-keying stops |
| **2. The POS** | their POS moves to us, their books stay here | the books are already here and already correct - the POS is now a configuration change, not a switch |
| **3. Payroll** | their payroll moves to us | the posting contract is already there |

**Step 1 is the wedge** and it is the reason to build the standalone architecture *first*, even though the standalone plan can be sold from Phase 1 and the add-on only lands in Phase 2. The add-on sells to tenants we have; the standalone architecture is what makes the tenants we do not have reachable at all.

**Step 2 is what makes the switch permanent.** Once the books depend on our POS, the tenant has nothing left to migrate back to, and the incumbent stack loses a system it cannot get back. That is the compounding effect of binding the two.

### 3.3 Why the incumbent cannot simply copy this

The honest answer: **Xero and Bukku could build a POS connector.** So could AutoCount.

What they cannot do, quickly:

- **They do not own the transaction.** A connector is a summary; the frozen cost, the discount's provenance and the outlet-level dimension only exist in a system that was built to run the floor.
- **They do not have our POS tenants.** The distribution is ours: the add-on is sold to a base we already have.
- **They would be displacing themselves.** AutoCount's POS business is the thing they would be cannibalising.

**But the real answer is timing and cost, not impossibility.** Building a POS-grade accounting core is years of work for them to arrive where we arrive by owning the till. **The window is real and it is not forever**, and the honest version of this document says so.

## The unit economics

### 4.1 The costs

**The good news first: the ledger consumes no tokens.**

Unlike the AI features, a double-entry ledger is **deterministic**. Posting rules, trial balances and statements are arithmetic. **There is no inference cost per sale, no model call, and no accuracy cliff on the books.** For a product sold per tenant per month, that is the difference between a 90% margin and a 60% one.

| Cost | What it really is | Scale |
|---|---|---|
| **Database** | Postgres, one cluster, many tenants | the dominant infra cost; roughly flat at our scale |
| **Object storage** | receipts, attachments, imported files | negligible - a few tens of cents per tenant per month |
| **Compute** | the API, the workers, the statement engine | small; the statements are indexed reads, not heavy queries |
| **e-Invoice submission** | the MyInvois API | **free at our side** - the taxpayer submits directly with their own credential; there is no per-document charge to us. This is a structural advantage over an intermediary model |
| **Bank feed** | a statement feed, if we use one | **this one can cost money** - a paid feed is per account or per transaction. It is why the bank is Phase 7 and not Phase 1, and why the manual statement import exists at all |
| **Support** | human hours | **the real cost, and the one that scales badly** - see §4.3 |
| **Encryption and key management** | the source credentials | small, but it is a hard requirement, not an option |

**The cost that is easy to underestimate is support.** An accounting product's questions are hard: "why is my March different from the accountant's?", "which period did that land in?", "this is wrong, fix it". **Every one of those is a conversation with a tenant who cares about their money.** The switch (§5 of `connectors-and-switching.mdx`) is where the support cost concentrates.

### 4.2 The revenue

**The economics are the ordinary SaaS economics, and they are good because the cost is flat and the price is not.**

- **The add-on at MYR 149 with a cost of a few ringgit** is a high-margin product - but it is capped by the POS base. **It is a proof, not a business.**
- **The standalone plans are the business.** A tenant at MYR 199/month who stays for three years is worth more than the whole add-on base, and the cost of serving them does not grow.
- **The accountant workspace is the multiplier.** One accountant on twenty books is **twenty tenants on one support conversation**, and the workspace is the only place the fragmented-stack tenant's real dependency lives.

### 4.3 Where the economics break

**Three honest failure modes:**

1. **Support scales linearly while revenue does not.** A tenant with 12 outlets and a monthly close generates more questions than a tenant with one. **The mitigation is the product:** the trial balance that names the out-of-balance amount, the side-by-side parallel run, the sync log, the dead letter with a retry button. Every one of those turns a support conversation into a screen. **If we build the product as a support-avoidance machine, the margin holds. If we build it as a black box, it does not.**
2. **The switch is expensive per tenant.** A fragmented-stack switch can take a week of the tenant's time and ours. **This is the strongest argument for the books-first wedge and the parallel run** - the parallel run is not a feature, it is the support-cost reduction.
3. **A failed switch is a churn event.** A tenant who trusts us with their books and gets them wrong does not complain - they leave. **The opening-balance import must balance before it is allowed to post** (`connectors-and-switching.mdx` §5.3), because a silently unbalanced opening balance is the single worst thing this product could do.

## The go-to-market

### 5.1 Sequence 1 - the add-on, into the base we already have

**The cheapest revenue in the business, and the one that should start.**

Every POS tenant is a candidate today. The pitch is a **demonstration, not a pitch**: connect their own POS to a trial accounting tenant, let the last month's sales post themselves, and show them a profit and loss statement that nobody typed. **The demo does the selling.**

- **The owner is the buyer.** The owner sees profit for the first time without an accountant's help.
- **The accountant is the validator.** The demo must survive the tenant's accountant - which is why the trial balance and the side-by-side comparison are in the product from the first phase.
- **The friction to remove first:** the signup path currently auto-provisions a POS account for every website customer (`website_api/app/routes/auth.py:137-161` → `provision_pos_account`). **A POS tenant buying the add-on must not be provisioned twice** - the accounting purchase attaches to the existing POS tenant. That is a small, concrete change with a real revenue impact, because it is the difference between a one-click purchase and a support ticket.

### 5.2 Sequence 2 - the books-first switch, into the fragmented stack

**This is the whole thesis, and it is a harder sale with a longer cycle.**

The pitch is not "our accounting is better". It is: **your POS already knows everything; our books will be filled in; you keep the till you have.** The proof is the side-by-side parallel run, not a demo.

- **The cycle is long** - the owner, the accountant, and the incumbent vendor's renewal date all matter.
- **The incumbent vendor will discount.** A POS vendor losing your accounting business discounts hard at renewal. **So the timing is the renewal date**, and the switch must be ready before that date, not after it.
- **The accountant is the way in.** An accountant who has seen our parallel run once is worth more than ten owner demos, because an accountant recommends us to the next client. **The accountant workspace (Phase 7) is therefore not a "nice to have later"** - it is the channel. This is `accounting-service.mdx`'s open question 5, and this document's answer is: **move it earlier if the books-first switch is the priority.**

### 5.3 Sequence 3 - the accountant

The accountant workspace - one login, many books - is a **distribution channel disguised as a feature**. An accountant on twenty books brings twenty tenants, and every tenant they bring is a tenant who came through a recommendation rather than a cold sale.

**But the accountant is also the strictest reviewer we will have.** They will check our statements against their own, line by line, and they will find the differences. **That is a feature, not a threat** - the accountant is the reason our trial balance and our parallel run are honest, and an honest product that survives their scrutiny is worth more than a smooth product that does not.

### 5.4 The market, honestly

**We have not measured this market, and this document will not pretend to.**

What we know factually:

- **The live POS base is small** - 10 tenants, 11 outlets, 365 orders. That is a young product, and it means the add-on revenue starts from a base of ten.
- **The market's shape is documented** in `malaysia-e-invoice.mdx` - a typical F&B operator runs a POS, an accounting package and a MyInvois bridge, and the seam between them is manual.
- **The number that matters is the count of Malaysian F&B establishments running a fragmented stack**, and **we have not measured it**. A real figure needs the number of registered food-service premises, their POS penetration, and their accounting-package penetration - which is a research task, not something to guess at in a design document.

**So the honest framing of the market question is: this is a wedge, not a market, until it is measured.** The first money is ten standalone plans, and the money that decides the business is ten tenants' worth of add-on. The displacement thesis is a hypothesis with a cheap test: **ask twenty POS tenants how many systems they run and whether they would replace their accounting package.** If the answer is "one" for most and "four" for a third, the wedge is real. **That is a week of work and it should happen before Phase 6 is built.**

## The honest case against this

**Every claim above has a counter-argument, and the strong ones are here rather than buried.**

### 6.1 The accounting SaaS market is hard and we would be a small entrant

Xero and Bukku have accountants, bank feeds, payroll, years of data and hundreds of engineers. **We would be a small entrant in a market where the incumbents are strong and the buyers are conservative.**

**The counter-counter:** we are not competing with them. Our competitor is **AutoCount's POS-and-accounting bundle** and **a stack of disconnected subscriptions** - not Xero. Xero is the thing our tenant's accountant *already uses*, which makes us a feeder, not a rival. That is a real defence, but it is a defence of the **wedge**, not of a general accounting business. **If we ever try to win the general SMB market head-on, this document's argument no longer applies.**

### 6.2 We may be building a big thing to sell a small thing

**This is the sharpest risk in Option C, and it should not be softened.** We build a standalone service with its own database, its own tenancy, its own API and its own web app - and the money that decides whether it was worth it is an add-on to tenants we already have, sold at MYR 149.

**How it stays sane:**

- **The phases are revenue gates, not completion gates.** Phase 2 must sell before Phase 6 is built. If the add-on does not sell to the base we have, **we stop** - and the sunk cost is one service, one ledger and one connector, which is reusable. **The standalone plan selling from Phase 1 does not weaken this gate - it is the hedge, and the hedge is not the thesis.**
- **The standalone architecture is not wasted if the accounting product fails.** The ledger, the statements, the trial balance and the accountant-facing UI are all things a **POS-first** product still wants. **This is the strongest hedge in the plan: if we retreat to "accounting as a POS feature", we keep the engine and throw away only the tenancy and the API surface.**
- **The repo stays one repo.** The accounting service lives in `POS_AI` alongside `backend/` and `website_api/` - so retreat is a deletion, not an extraction.

### 6.3 The POS tenants might not want to pay for accounting

They may say the POS already tells them enough, or that their accountant already has the books. **This is the most likely failure of the wedge revenue.**

**The mitigation, and it is a product decision rather than a sales one:** the value is the **frozen cost, the discount source and the outlet dimension** - the three facts an accountant's spreadsheet never has. **If the demo shows only revenue, it will fail.** If the demo shows the margin, computed from the recipe cost at the moment of the sale, it is a different product from a spreadsheet. **This is why `ledger-and-money.mdx` §4.4 insists the COGS journal reads `frozen_cost` and never the live recipe price** - the demo depends on it.

### 6.4 We could get the tenancy wrong

The legal-entity decision (`accounting-service.mdx`) is correct for the books and awkward for the user: **a group with three companies gets three logins.** If that turns out to be the deal-breaker, the fix is the accountant workspace - one login, many books - which is Phase 7 and therefore late. **If the fragmented-stack tenant is the priority, the workspace should move earlier.**

### 6.5 Support could sink it

A per-tenant support cost that scales with the tenant's complexity, against a price that does not. **The mitigation is the product** (§4.3), and it is not optional - the trial balance, the parallel run, the sync log and the dead letter are all support-cost reductions wearing a feature's clothes.
