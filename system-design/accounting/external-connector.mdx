## The shape of it

**The connector is optional. Every tenant can ignore it and still have complete books.**

That sentence is the design. The books in `accounting-module.mdx` are the product;
the connector is a convenience for the tenant whose accountant already works in
Xero or Bukku and will not look at our statements.

**One direction, one source of truth.** Our ledger is the truth. The connector
**pushes** journals, invoices and bills to the tenant's external system. We do not
read their ledger back and we do not let them edit ours through the connector.
A tenant who edits a journal in Xero does not change our books - and we say so
plainly in the connection screen, because a silent two-way expectation is how a
finance tool loses a customer's trust.

**Why push rather than "be" Xero:** the tenant's accountant has a filing habit, a
template, a colleague and a workflow we cannot replace. The correct integration is
the one that puts our numbers where they already work, in a form their system
understands, and then gets out of the way.

## What each provider gives us

### Xero

Read on 26 Nov 2026 from <https://developer.xero.com/documentation/api/accounting/overview>.

| Fact | What it means for the connector |
|---|---|
| The Accounting API is a family of endpoints - Accounts, Contacts, Invoices, Credit Notes, Items, Currencies, TaxRates, Journals, Manual Journals, Bank Transactions, Purchase Orders, Organisation, Payments, Prepayments, Overpayments, Tracking Categories, Budgets, History and Notes, Attachments | we map our entities onto the ones we need and ignore the rest |
| **`GET Journals` is read-only** - the documentation says to use **Manual Journals** to create journals | we create journals through `ManualJournals`, not `Journals`. This is the single most important fact for the design |
| **`GET Journals` returns at most 100 per response** (the high-volume threshold) | any read we do is paged; any write we do is chunked |
| The **Journals endpoint** requires a use-case approval and the **Advanced** tier | the connector must degrade gracefully - a tenant on a lower tier loses the journal push and keeps the invoice push |
| **Organisation** gives the base currency and the fiscal year | we read it at connect time and show the tenant their external base currency next to ours, so a mismatch is visible before the first push |
| **TaxRates** are per-organisation, with a `Type` (TAX / NO tax) and a `Status` | our `acc_tax_codes` map to their tax rates by id |
| **Accounts** carry a `Type` (BANK, CURRLIAB, DEPRECIATN, DIRECTCOSTS, EXPENSE, FIXED, INVENTORY, LIABILITY, NONCURRENT, OTHERINCOME, OVERHEADS, PREPAYMENT, REVENUE, SALES, TERMLIAB, CURRLIAB) and a `Code` | a mapping table, not a conversion - see §3 |
| **OAuth 2** with a tenant id per authorisation, plus **Webhooks** | one authorisation per external tenant; our push does not need webhooks in v1 (we do not read back) |
| **Rate limits** and a concurrency cap on the API | our worker is rate-limited per connection and retries with backoff |

**What we push to Xero:** one **Manual Journal** per our journal (with `LineAmount` and a `TaxType` where relevant), one **Invoice** per our AR invoice, one **Contact** per our debtor, and the **Account** and **TaxRate** setups they are mapped to.

### Bukku

Read on 26 Nov 2026 from <https://developers.bukku.my/>.

| Fact | What it means for the connector |
|---|---|
| Auth is a **Bearer token** plus a **`Company-Subdomain`** header - the subdomain selects the company | our connection row holds the subdomain alongside the encrypted token, and every request carries both |
| The rate limit is **600 requests per minute** | our worker stays well inside it and treats it as a hard ceiling, not a target |
| The endpoint families are **Contacts, Products, Sales, Purchase, Bank, Stock, Accounting - Journal Entry, Accounting - Account** | the Bukku side of the connector is smaller than the Xero side - no tax rate object, the tax lives on the line |
| **Journal Entry** carries `journal_items[].account_id` - a debit/credit amount and an account per item | a near-direct match to our `acc_journal_lines` |
| **Account** carries `type` (the ten accounting types) and `system_type` (`accounts_receivable`, `accounts_payable`, `inventory`) | our chart of accounts maps almost one-to-one - §3 |
| Some endpoints have **eligibility rules**: an invoice's `receivable_account_id` must be an `accounts_receivable` account, a bill's `payable_account_id` must be `accounts_payable`, `default_income_account_id` must be income or other_income, `default_expense_account_id` must be expenses or cost_of_sales, `sale_account_id` must be income or other_income, `purchase_account_id` must be expenses or cost_of_sales, `inventory_account_id` must be an `inventory` account, and a **stock adjustment's account must be expenses or cost_of_sales** | our system accounts are exactly the three that satisfy these - §3.2 - so the connector can check eligibility before the first push and say which account is wrong |
| **Bank deposit** carries `account_id` and `fee_account_id`; a **transfer** needs two different balance-sheet accounts | our bill payments and our settlement movements map to these |
| Bukku is **MyInvois-ready** | relevant to the e-Invoice rail, not to this connector |
| API access is requested from Bukku; there are **staging and production** servers | the connection screen must let a tenant connect to **staging first** - a tenant who pushes to production on the first try is a support ticket |

**What we push to Bukku:** one **Journal Entry** per our journal, one **Sales transaction** per our invoice, one **Contact** per our debtor, and the **Account** setup they are mapped to.

## The mapping

### 3.1 The account map

Our `acc_accounts` has a `type` from the same ten-type list Bukku uses, and a
`system_type` for the three system accounts. So the map is **by type, not by
name** - the tenant renames `4000 Food sales` to `Sales - F&B` and the mapping
does not break.

The mapping table is `acc_connection_mappings`: one row per our account, holding
the external account's id and its type. The connection screen is a two-column
picker - our account on the left, theirs on the right - and it **refuses to save a
pairing whose types disagree**. A `REVENUE` account cannot be mapped to an
`EXPENSE` account, because that would silently misstate their books and there is no
way for the tenant to notice.

**Our accounts are pushed to them once, at connect time**, using their own account
types. The tenant can pre-create accounts in Xero or Bukku and map to those
instead - both paths are supported, and the second is the normal one for a tenant
whose accountant already set the chart up.

### 3.2 The three system accounts and why they are not optional

Bukku's eligibility rules above mean that an invoice cannot be raised without an
`accounts_receivable` account, a bill without an `accounts_payable` account, and
stock cannot be adjusted without an `inventory` account and an expense account.
Our seed chart (§2.3 of the main document) marks exactly these three as
`is_system`, and they are the accounts the connector requires.

**So the connector's setup screen checks them first**, before anything else, and
tells the tenant plainly: *your receivable account is not mapped - invoices cannot
be pushed yet.* The push queue does not silently skip them.

### 3.3 The entity map

| Ours | Xero | Bukku |
|---|---|---|
| `acc_debtors` | Contact | Contact |
| `acc_invoices` | Invoice | Sales transaction |
| `acc_bills` | no equivalent push in v1 | Purchase transaction |
| `acc_journals` | Manual Journal | Journal Entry |
| `acc_accounts` | Account | Account |
| `acc_tax_codes` | TaxRate | the tax on the line |
| `acc_bank_transactions` | Bank Transaction | Bank transaction |

**The journal push is the important one.** Every posted journal becomes one
external journal with one line per our line, carrying the account mapping and the
tax code. A journal we void, we push as a reversing entry - the same audit rule as
our own books (§3.2 of the main document).

**What is NOT pushed:** the tenant's chart of accounts is not overwritten, their
accounts are not deleted, our period locks are not their period locks, and
**nothing is ever pushed to an external system that the tenant has not explicitly
enabled** - each push type (journals, invoices, bills, contacts) is a separate
switch in the connection screen, on by default only for the type the tenant
enabled.

## The design of the push

### 4.1 The queue, not the request

**A push never happens inside a customer request.** The POS order screen must not
wait on Xero's API - that is how a payment screen ends up timing out because a
third party is slow.

So: the journal is written to our ledger (that is the truth, and it is immediate),
and the connector's worker picks up the outbound work from a queue table. The
customer's order is never blocked, and a Xero outage does not stop the restaurant
from taking money.

### 4.2 Idempotency - the rule that stops double-posting

Every outbound item carries a **stable key**: `connection_id` + `local_id` +
`push_type`. Before pushing, the worker checks whether that key was already
pushed successfully. If it was, it skips. **A journal is pushed once, ever.**

The mapping table also stores the external id we were given, so a later push
updates the same external object instead of creating a second one. Without this,
a retried push would double a tenant's revenue in their accountant's system - the
one failure mode that ends the integration permanently.

### 4.3 The rate limits and the backoff

- **Xero** - the worker reads the response headers and honours the API's own
  limits; the concurrency is capped per connection.
- **Bukku** - the ceiling is 600 requests per minute, and the worker targets well
  under it.
- **Both** - a `429` or a `5xx` is retried with exponential backoff and jitter.
  The attempt count is recorded; after a small number of attempts the item goes to
  a **failed** state with the error, and it is visible in the sync log.
- **Both** - the worker is per-tenant and serial per connection. Two workers never
  push into the same external tenant at the same time, so the ordering of journals
  matches the ordering in our books.

### 4.4 The sync log

The connection screen has a **sync log**: every push attempt, with the time, the
entity, the result, and the external id it got. A tenant who says "my March
revenue is wrong in Xero" can be answered in minutes, from the log, instead of by
guessing.

The log also holds the **failures**, and they are actionable: *Bill 00123 was
rejected - account 6100 Gas bills is mapped to an account of type EXPENSE but the
Bukku endpoint requires income for a sale*. Bukku's eligibility rules are exactly
the kind of thing that is invisible until it is a message the tenant can read.

## The credentials

**The tokens are secrets and they are encrypted at rest**, in `acc_connections`:
`access_token_enc`, `refresh_token_enc`. Never in plaintext, never in the
frontend, never in a log line, never in an error message.

The pattern is the one the e-Invoice module already uses for the tenant's ERP
credentials (`malaysia-e-invoice.mdx` §"The tables" - the `client_id` /
`client_secret` columns are encrypted, and the tenant's own token is stored on the
tenant). Two things follow from it:

- **The tenant's own connection, on the tenant's own row.** Nothing is shared
  between tenants, and there is no central pool of accounting credentials.
- **The decrypt happens inside the connector service**, at the moment of the
  request, and the plaintext token lives in memory for the length of that request
  and no longer.

**The tenant sees the connection state, never the token.** The screen shows
"Connected to Xero - organisation name - last pushed 4 minutes ago", with a
**disconnect** button. Disconnect revokes and deletes the tokens and the mappings;
it does not delete what was already pushed (that is the tenant's external system
and the external system's own undo).

**The access token is refreshed by the worker**, not by the frontend, and a failed
refresh marks the connection `needs_reauth` and tells the tenant - rather than
failing silently for a week.

## The screens

| Screen | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Accounting - Connections** | the list of connections (one per provider per tenant), the connect button, the disconnect button, the sync log |
| **Connect wizard - Xero** | the OAuth consent, the organisation picker, the base currency check, the account mapping, the test push, the enable switches |
| **Connect wizard - Bukku** | the token and subdomain, the company check, the account mapping, the test push, the enable switches |
| **Mapping table** | our account on the left, theirs on the right, with the type mismatch refused and the three system accounts flagged when they are unmapped |
| **Sync log** | every attempt, every result, every external id |

The wizard ends with a **test push** - one journal, one invoice - that the tenant
sees in their own Xero or Bukku before the first real one goes. A tenant who has
seen the test arrive should not be surprised by the live data.

**The gateway model is the POS's own.** Xero is OAuth - the tenant authorises in
their browser and we hold the token; Bukku is a token and a subdomain the tenant
gives us. Neither route can see another tenant's connection.

## The phased plan

### Phase 6.1 - the connection and the mapping

The tables, the credential encryption, the Xero OAuth flow, the Bukku token flow,
the organisation/company check, the account mapping screen with its type check and
the three system-account flags.

**Does NOT:** push anything. The test push at the end of the wizard is manual and
one journal only.

### Phase 6.2 - the journal push

The queue, the idempotency key, the worker, the rate limiting, the backoff, the
sync log. Our journals go out as Xero Manual Journals and Bukku Journal Entries.

**Does NOT:** push invoices, bills or contacts yet. One entity type, proved
end-to-end, before the queue carries three.

### Phase 6.3 - invoices, bills and contacts

The debtor contacts, the AR invoices, the AP bills, each behind its own enable
switch.

**Does NOT:** change what is pushed automatically. The switches stay explicit.

### Phase 6.4 - the resilience

The reauth flow, the failed-item retry, the connection health, the "what is not
pushed yet" report, and the alert when a connection has been failing for a while.

## What this design does NOT claim

- **It does not claim two-way sync.** Ours is the source of truth. A journal
  edited in Xero does not come back. §"The shape of it".
- **It does not claim the external system is a certified book.** Pushing a
  balanced journal to a tenant's accountant does not make the tenant's filing
  correct - that is the accountant's and the tenant's responsibility.
- **It does not claim every tenant plan can use it.** It is the
  `Accounting Connector` plan feature row, separate from `Accounting`.
- **It does not claim the token can be recovered.** It is encrypted at rest and
  deleted on disconnect; a lost token means a reauth, by design.
- **It does not claim the pushed history is complete.** Only what is pushed goes
  out - a tenant who connects in November pushes from November.
- **It does not claim a Xero tenant who edits the chart stays in sync.** Our
  mapping is ours; a change in their chart surfaces as a mapping error in the sync
  log, not as a silent mis-mapping.

## The open questions

1. **Does the tenant want their bills pushed too?** A bill in Xero is a Bill
   object, which Bukku does not expose in the same shape. Pushing bills to Xero
   but not to Bukku is an asymmetry we should decide deliberately.
2. **What is our own equivalent of their tax settings?** Xero's TaxRates and
   Bukku's line-level tax do not match. Our `acc_tax_codes` is the neutral
   middle; the connector maps it either way.
3. **Does the push include the frozen cost?** Our COGS journals carry the recipe
   cost frozen at the sale. Pushing them makes their gross profit match ours -
   but their own stock system may compute a different cost. Their accountant needs
   to know which one the number came from.
4. **How far back does the first push go?** The PROPOSAL is from the connect date
   forward, with an explicit re-push of a chosen period. A full historical push
   is a separate job.
5. **What happens to the connection when a tenant leaves?** The connection is
   deleted with the tenant. The data already pushed lives in their system.
6. **Should a failed push ever block a period close?** The PROPOSAL is no - our
   books close on our own facts, and the connector reports what it could not push.
   The alternative is safer for the external book and worse for the tenant's
   workflow.
