## The shape of it

**The POS already knows every number an accountant needs. It just cannot add them up.**

A tenant's own outlet sold 200 covers last month. The POS holds the gross, the discount, the SST, the payment method of every single one of them, the recipe cost of every dish, the purchase orders that filled the fridge and the stock movement that recorded the waste. What it cannot do is say **RM 41,280 revenue, RM 1,914 cost of goods, RM 39,366 gross profit** - because there is no chart of accounts, no journal, no fiscal year, and no statement of any kind in the product today.

**This document is the engine that adds it up.** A **double-entry ledger**:

- a **chart of accounts** the tenant owns (seeded from a preset, editable from day one),
- **journals** written automatically when money moves (an order paid, a purchase received, a stock bin wasted),
- **fiscal years and periods** that can be locked so last quarter cannot be rewritten,
- **the statements** - profit and loss, balance sheet, trial balance, general ledger, tax summary.

**Two things it is not.** It is **not** a second e-Invoice system (`malaysia-e-invoice.mdx` owns that rail - §6.4). And it is **not** us trying to out-build Xero or AutoCount - the **feature set is modelled on Bukku and Xero**, because the third-party case (`connectors-and-switching.mdx`) only works if our model and theirs are the same shape.

**Three findings that shape the whole design.**

**Finding 1 - the money in the POS is a float, and a ledger cannot be a float.**
`Order.subtotal`, `discount`, `tax`, `total` and `Payment.amount` are all `Float` (`models.py:284-295`, `:379`). A float cannot hold 0.1 + 0.2, and a ledger that balances to the cent is the entire point. Meanwhile the purchase side is **already integer cents** (`PurchaseOrder.total_amount`, `PurchaseOrderItem.unit_price` and `subtotal` - `supplier.py:67`, `:93`, `:95`) and the supplier price list is **DECIMAL(10,4)** (`supplier.py:45`). So the codebase already carries three money representations. §4.1 settles this.

**Finding 2 - the platform already has a proven tenant rail, and the ledger must ride it.**
Every reporting endpoint answers the same four questions before it answers the fifth (`reports.py:18-24`): *who may call it* (`require_staff_permission`), *whose data* (`require_outlet_tenant` - the outlet must belong to the tenant of the session, **never** to a `customer_id` in the query string), *did the tenant buy it* (`require_plan_feature`), *does the UI agree*.

**Under the standalone framing this rail is copied, not shared.** The accounting service has its own database and its own sessions, so it cannot call the POS's `require_outlet_tenant`. **But it adopts the rule verbatim, with the same names and the same refusal behaviour** - because the alternative is inventing a second, weaker tenant rule for the most sensitive data in the product. The accounting service's equivalent is stated in §3.5.

**Finding 3 - the outbox already exists, and it is the answer to "how does a sale get into the ledger".**
`domain_events` (`models/domain_event.py:89`), `event_subscriptions` (`:159`) and `event_deliveries` (`:239`), with a dispatcher (`services/domain_events.py:704`) that retries with backoff, writes **dead letters** (`:870`) and can requeue them (`:899`). **A journal is posted from an event, not from a request.** The sale path already has exactly the machinery this needs - the connector spec in `connectors-and-switching.mdx` §3 is a channel on this outbox, not a new queue.

## The design - the shape of the books

### 2.1 Who owns the books

**One set of books per accounting tenant. The accounting tenant is a legal entity, and the outlet is a dimension inside it.**

This is the one place where the rewrite changed the model, and the change is deliberate:

- In the POS-only version, the books belonged to the POS `Customer` row (`models.py:26`) - the restaurant group - with the outlet as a filter.
- **Now the books belong to `acc_tenants`, which is a legal entity**, owned by a platform customer in the website API. The outlet is a **dimension** inside it, not its owner.

The reason is the filing unit. In Malaysia a business is registered, and the registration is what files SST and signs the accounts. A group with three companies has **three sets of books** and one balance sheet per company. Modelling the books per restaurant group would make it impossible to produce a correct SST return for a group whose companies file separately - a common shape here.

**The consequence that needs watching:** a tenant with three companies gets three balance sheets and three logins, not one. That is the correct accounting answer and the worse product answer. `accounting-service.mdx` §"Open questions" asks it explicitly, and the answer is: the books are per entity, but the **accountant workspace** (`accounting-service.mdx` Phase 7) is per platform customer - one login, many books. The awkwardness is solved at the workspace layer, not by lying about the tenant.

**So every ledger row carries the accounting tenant id, and optionally the outlet id** - the branch the money moved in. The tenant owns the books; the outlet is the filter. The financial year belongs to the tenant.

### 2.2 The components

| Component | What it holds | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Accounting tenant** | the legal entity: name, registration number, SST registration, base currency, financial year start | `acc_tenants` |
| **Chart of accounts** | the tenant's accounts, their type, their mapping to a source | `acc_accounts` |
| **Journal** | the postings - one header, many lines, debits equal credits | `acc_journals` + `acc_journal_lines` |
| **Fiscal calendar** | the years and the periods, each open / locked / closed | `acc_fiscal_years` + `acc_periods` |
| **Sales ledger (AR)** | invoices raised from orders, what is owed, what is paid | `acc_invoices` + `acc_debtors` |
| **Purchase ledger (AP)** | bills raised from received purchase orders, what is owed to suppliers | `acc_bills` |
| **Bank** | the cash and card accounts, the transactions, the reconciliation | `acc_bank_accounts` + `acc_bank_transactions` |
| **Tax** | the SST / VAT / service tax rates and the codes they post to | `acc_tax_codes` |
| **Source** | a connected system - our POS, another POS, Xero, Bukku, payroll, a bank | `acc_tenant_sources` + `acc_source_credentials` |
| **Statement engine** | P&L, balance sheet, trial balance, general ledger, tax summary | reads the journal; writes nothing |

### 2.3 The account types - chosen to map 1:1 to Bukku and Xero

The type list is taken from the **Bukku** account model, which is the same shape Xero uses:

`current_assets`, `non_current_assets`, `other_assets`, `current_liabilities`, `non_current_liabilities`, `equity`, `income`, `other_income`, `expenses`, `cost_of_sales`

Plus Bukku's `system_type` - `accounts_receivable`, `accounts_payable`, `inventory` - for the three accounts the system itself must know about. Those three are what make an auto-posting rule possible ("this order paid on credit goes to the receivable account"), and they are why the seed chart is marked `is_system` and cannot be deleted.

**The presets.** The engine is horizontal, but the seed chart is vertical. A new tenant picks a preset at first use:

| Preset | The chart it seeds |
|---|---|
| **F&B** | the restaurant chart below - food and beverage split, service charge, wastage, a bar and a kitchen |
| **Retail** | sales, discounts, cost of goods, stock adjustments, no service charge |
| **Services** | revenue by engagement, no inventory, a contract liability for prepayments |

### 2.4 The rail this engine must not cross: the e-Invoice

**The e-Invoice is a tax document for LHDN. The books are the tenant's internal record. They are not the same thing, and this engine does not replace the e-Invoice rail.**

`malaysia-e-invoice.mdx` already draws the line: the tax invoice is issued on request at the counter, the monthly consolidated e-Invoice is the artefact, and the whole flow is a *document* pipeline (UBL 2.1 JSON, a taxpayer id, a certificate).

What this engine produces instead: **journals, accounts, periods and statements.** The overlap is exactly one field - the tax amount - and the rule is:

> The **tax amount** comes from the POS pricing service (which already computes it correctly on the amount the guest pays - `order_pricing.py:61`), and the same amount is posted to the tax payable account. The **e-Invoice** rail reads that amount for its document. Neither rail computes tax on its own, and neither one may quietly restate the other.

**Under the standalone framing, the direction of that rail changes** - the accounting service becomes the ERP client rather than the POS - and the accounting service therefore holds the tax figure. The rule above is unchanged by that move, and the detailed cross-document change is named in `accounting-service.mdx` §"The cross-document change this creates".

## The design - the data

### 3.1 The tables

All PROPOSAL. Every one carries `acc_tenant_id` (the accounting tenant - the legal entity) and, where the money moved in a branch, `outlet_id`. All money columns are **integer minor units** (cents) - see §4.1.

The service-level tables (`acc_tenants`, `acc_tenant_sources`, `acc_source_credentials`, `acc_tenant_users`, `acc_api_clients`) are specified in `accounting-service.mdx` §"The data model". This section covers the **ledger** tables.

| Table | The columns that matter |
|---|---|
| `acc_accounts` | `id`, `acc_tenant_id`, `outlet_id` (NULL = all outlets), `code`, `name`, `type` (the ten types), `system_type` (the three), `parent_id`, `tax_code`, `currency`, `is_system`, `is_active`, `created_at` |
| `acc_journals` | `id`, `acc_tenant_id`, `outlet_id`, `journal_date`, `memo`, `source_type` (order / purchase / stock / manual / system / opening), `source_id`, `period_id`, `status` (draft / posted / void), `is_reversal`, `reverses_journal_id`, `created_by`, `created_at`, `posted_at` |

| `acc_periods` | `id`, `fiscal_year_id`, `acc_tenant_id`, `name` (`2026-01`), `starts_on`, `ends_on`, `status` (open / locked / closed), `closed_at`, `closed_by` |
| `acc_debtors` | `id`, `acc_tenant_id`, `name`, `phone`, `email`, `tax_id`, `credit_limit_minor`, `created_at` |

| `acc_bills` | `id`, `acc_tenant_id`, `outlet_id`, `bill_number`, `supplier_id`, `purchase_order_id`, `bill_date`, `due_date`, `subtotal_minor`, `tax_minor`, `total_minor`, `paid_minor`, `status` |
| `acc_bank_accounts` | `id`, `acc_tenant_id`, `outlet_id`, `account_id`, `name`, `currency`, `opening_balance_minor` |
| `acc_bank_transactions` | `id`, `acc_tenant_id`, `bank_account_id`, `txn_date`, `amount_minor`, `direction` (in / out), `description`, `counterparty`, `journal_id`, `reconciled`, `statement_ref` |
| `acc_tax_codes` | `id`, `acc_tenant_id`, `code`, `name`, `rate_bp` (basis points - an integer, not a float), `tax_type` (sst / vat / service_tax / zero), `gl_account_id` |
| `acc_period_locks` | `id`, `period_id`, `locked_by`, `locked_at`, `reopened_by`, `reopened_at`, `reason` |

**The last row is new in this version.** Under a single tenant the lock could live on the period row. Under a standalone service where an accountant may re-open a period and a tenant may dispute it, **the lock events are their own rows** - who locked it, when, who re-opened it and why. A locked period that can be silently re-opened is not a lock.

### 3.2 The audit rule

**A posted journal is never edited and never deleted.** A mistake is corrected by posting a **reversal** that carries `reverses_journal_id` pointing at the original, and then a fresh journal. The original stays, so the tenant's accountant can see what happened and when it was corrected.

This is the same rule the POS already follows with the receipt reprint log and the void report (`reports.py:412`): money that moved leaves a mark. A ledger without that rule is a spreadsheet with extra steps.

### 3.3 The fiscal year, the periods and the lock

- A tenant's **fiscal year** is created at first use and can be named freely (`FY2026`, `Jan-Dec 2026`). It matches the calendar year by default; a tenant who files on a different year sets it once.
- A **period** is a month inside the year. Months are created automatically when the year is.
- **Locking a period** is the point of the whole feature. When a period is locked:
  - a journal dated inside it is **refused** - the API answers `409` with the period name and the date that would have been posted;
  - a **posting dated today** that belongs to last month is **refused** the same way;
  - a **reversal** of a journal inside a locked period is refused - the correction belongs in the current open period, which is how a real accountant does it;
  - the locked period's balances are **frozen** - the statement of that month can never change again, which is what makes the number the tenant's accountant signed;
  - **re-opening** is possible by an owner, and it writes a row in `acc_period_locks` and **is visible on the statement**.

Closing a year additionally carries the **retained earnings** into the next year's opening balances and posts the **closing entry**. That is the only entry the system writes on its own without a source document.

### 3.4 The money at the boundary

**Every `*_minor` column is an integer number of cents. Never a float. Never a decimal string.** `RM 41,280.50` is stored as `4128050` with `currency = 'MYR'`.

Why, given the POS already uses floats:

- A ledger that balances to the cent is the entire point. Float arithmetic does not guarantee it.
- The purchase side is **already integer cents** (`supplier.py:67`, `:93`). The ledger reads from those rows directly; making the sales side cents too means the two halves of the business use one arithmetic.
- The one rounding that must happen is **at the boundary**, and it must happen **once**. When a POS order is posted, the float order total is converted to cents **once**, half-up, and **that rounded value is the ledger's truth**. The ledger never re-reads the float. A float `total` of `123.456789` becomes `12346` cents - and the order screen shows the same `123.46` the guest paid, because the rounding difference is under a cent and the ledger absorbs it in the rounding account.

**A rounding account is a first-class account** (`Rounding differences`, `other_income`), so that the pennies which arise from the float boundary have a home and the trial balance still balances to the cent.

### 3.5 The tenant rail - the accounting service's own

The accounting service cannot call the POS's `require_outlet_tenant` - different database, different sessions. So it states its own rail, with the same four questions and the same refusals.

1. **Who may call it** - the session's role against the tenant's user list (`owner` / `accountant` / `staff`). The service checks a permission, not a plan.
2. **Whose data** - **the tenant is never taken from the request.** The tenant comes from the credential or the session, and the tenant of every resource is verified against it. A caller that asks for another tenant's journal is refused.
3. **Did the tenant buy it** - the entitlement call to the website API, exactly as the POS does it (`fetch_plan_features`, `services/plan_features.py:71`, over the internal key at `core/config.py:148`). **The service never decides its own entitlements.**
4. **Does the UI agree** - the web app asks the same entitlements endpoint the API uses.

**The one rule carried over word for word:** the `customer_id`-style parameter is a **claim, never an authority**. The POS states it in `core/tenant.py` - the claim is read at `:56`, required at `:77`, and a key that names no tenant is refused with `KEY_CANNOT_PICK_TENANT` (`:41`). The accounting service adopts the same names and the same refusal, because a ledger is a more attractive target than an order.

## The design - the rules

### 4.1 The chart of accounts is the tenant's

Seeded once per accounting tenant at first use from the preset, then it is theirs. They rename `4000 Food sales` to whatever their accountant calls it. They add `6100 Gas bills`. They can restrict an account to one outlet (`outlet_id`) - a tenant whose kitchen and its bar file separately may want a `5100 Bar supplies` account that only the bar posts to.

Three accounts are **system** accounts: `Accounts receivable`, `Accounts payable` and `Inventory`. The auto-posting rules (§4.5) need them, so they cannot be deleted and they cannot be moved to another type.

### 4.2 Currency: one book, several currencies, never one sum

The accounting tenant's books have a **base currency**. A transaction in another currency is stored in its own currency **and** in the base currency at the rate of the day, in the same journal - two `currency` values on the lines.

**The rule from `multi-currency-money.mdx` holds without exception: amounts in different currencies are never summed.** The P&L is reported per currency, or in the base currency at a stated rate with the rate visible.

**The new part, and it is a real problem:** under the standalone framing, a platform customer can hold books in **different base currencies** - a Malaysian entity and a Cambodian entity, say. `accounting-service.mdx` §"Open questions" question 3 is this one. The proposal: **one base currency per accounting tenant, which follows from one legal entity per tenant** - a company has one functional currency. Two companies, two base currencies, two books. The **consolidated view across a platform customer with two currencies is not a single number, and the product must refuse to show one.** That is the multi-currency rule applied one level up, and it is the rule that makes the accountant workspace honest.

### 4.3 When a journal is written

A journal is written when **money moves**, not when a screen is opened. The triggers, all PROPOSAL:

| Trigger | Source of truth | What is posted |
|---|---|---|
| An order is **paid** | `Order.payment_status` becomes `paid` | the sale (§4.4) |
| An order is **voided** | `Order.status` becomes `cancelled` | a reversal of the sale journal |
| An order is **refunded** | `Payment` / `OrderPayment` shows a refund | a reversal of the payment journal |
| A purchase order is **received** | `PurchaseOrder.status` becomes `received` | the stock in, the bill out (§5.2) |
| Stock is **wasted or adjusted** | `stock_movements` with `waste` / `adjustment` | the expense out, the stock down |
| A **bill is paid** | `acc_bills.paid_minor` changes | the payable down, the bank down |
| An **invoice is paid** | `acc_invoices.paid_minor` changes | the receivable down, the bank up |
| A **bank transaction is imported** | a statement import | the bank against a suspense account |
| A **period is closed** | the year-end close | retained earnings, and nothing else |

**One rule on timing:** the POS knows the moment of the sale, but a restaurant with `prepay_online` and `postpay` (`models.py:272`) has two different cash moments. A journal is dated when the **payment** is taken, and an unpaid order posts nothing until it is paid - unless it is a credit sale to a corporate debtor (§5.3), which posts on the invoice.

### 4.4 The auto-posting rules - the heart of the engine

Every rule below is PROPOSAL, and every rule is **per accounting tenant, per outlet, configurable** - the tenant can point any of these at a different account. The defaults are what §2.3 describes.

**The sale.** One order paid RM 100.00 (subtotal RM 100.00, discount RM 10.00, SST RM 5.40, paid by card):

| Line | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `1010 Bank - settlement` | RM 95.40 | |
| 2 | `4100 Discount given` | RM 10.00 | |
| 3 | `4000 Food sales` | | RM 100.00 |
| 4 | `2100 SST payable` | | RM 5.40 |

Debits RM 105.40, credits RM 105.40. The tax is on the amount the guest **pays** (RM 90.00) - which is exactly what `order_pricing.py:61` already does, so the book and the receipt say the same number.

**The discount is its own account, not a smaller sale.** `Order.discount_source` (`models.py:292`) says whether it came from a voucher, a promotion or the manager's own hand - and those are three different things to an accountant (a voucher breaks a liability, a promotion is marketing spend, a manual discount is contra revenue). We post it to `4100 Discount given` and record the source on the line, so the tenant can split the three later without re-reading the orders.

**The payment method picks the debit account.** Cash goes to `1000 Cash on hand`; card, DuitNow QR, Touch 'n Go, GrabPay and Boost go to `1010 Bank - settlement` until the money actually lands - which is why that account exists and why it is a settlement account and not a bank account.

**The cost of goods.** When the order is ready, the POS already writes the stock movement (`order.py:236`, `movement_type='usage'`) and it already froze the line cost (`OrderItem.frozen_cost`, `models.py:338`). The journal reads the frozen cost - **never the live recipe price**, because the margin of an order sold in October must not be rewritten by October's price:

| Line | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `5000 Cost of goods sold` | RM 21.30 | |
| 2 | `1200 Inventory` | | RM 21.30 |

**The frozen cost is the same number the Reports screen uses.** The margin rule in `reports.py:96-130` - the recipe when the tenant has Inventory, the estimate otherwise, and `cost_known: false` (never zero) when neither - must be the **one** rule. The accounting margin and the operational margin must not disagree; if they do, one of them is wrong and the tenant cannot tell which.

**A line with no known cost posts nothing to COGS**, and the tenant is told. It does not post zero cost - zero is a lie, and `frozen_cost` is already NULL for "unknown" rather than 0.0 for that reason.

**The reversal.** An order voided after it was paid posts the mirror image of the sale journal, with `reverses_journal_id` set. The stock movement that came with it is reversed the same way. The void report (`reports.py:412`) and the books tell the same story.

### 4.5 The manual journal

A tenant's accountant will need to post something no source produced: an accrual, an adjustment, a depreciation, a correction. So there is a **manual journal** screen - but it is a **journal**, not a shortcut around one: the same debit/credit lines, the same period lock, the same audit rule, and it posts into the same charts. The only difference is that its `source_type` is `manual` and it has a memo instead of an order.

## The design - the statements

### 5.1 What the statements are

| Statement | The question it answers | Built from |
|---|---|---|
| **Profit and loss** | what did the business earn, and what did it cost? | journal lines in the period, grouped by account type |
| **Balance sheet** | what does the tenant own, owe, and have left? | journal lines up to the date, grouped by balance-sheet type |
| **Trial balance** | does the whole thing add up? | every account's debit and credit total, and the grand total that must be equal |
| **General ledger** | what happened to this one account? | the lines of one account, in date order |
| **Tax summary** | what SST did we collect, and what may we claim? | the tax-coded lines by `acc_tax_codes` |
| **Cash and bank summary** | where is the money? | the bank accounts' balances, reconciled and not |
| **Sales by category** | which menu lines earn, and which only fill seats? | the same revenue accounts, with the `frozen_cost` margin |

**Every statement is per outlet and consolidated**, in one screen: pick the outlet or pick "all outlets".

**The trial balance is the honesty check.** If the debits and the credits differ by one cent, the statements are not shown as final - the screen says so and names the out-of-balance amount. A platform that shows a P&L from an unbalanced ledger is teaching the tenant to trust a number that is wrong.

### 5.2 Purchases - the bill

When a `PurchaseOrder` reaches `received` (`supplier.py:60`), the books post it:

| Line | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `1200 Inventory` (or `5100 Purchases`) | the ingredient's value | |
| 2 | `2000 Accounts payable` | | the supplier's total |

The bill is then paid by a bank transaction: `2000 Accounts payable` down, `1010 Bank - settlement` down. The purchase order's money is **already integer cents** (`supplier.py:67`, `:93`), so this path needs no conversion at all - which is exactly why §3.4 chose cents.

### 5.3 Receivables - the debtor the POS does not have

The POS has no debtor entity today (§1). A corporate table that eats every month and pays at the end needs one, or the books cannot show what is owed.

`acc_debtors` is that entity: name, phone, tax id, credit limit. An order can be raised against a debtor instead of being paid at the counter - the invoice posts `1100 Accounts receivable` instead of the bank, and the payment later clears it. **This is a new POS behaviour, not only a new screen**, so it is late in the phases (`accounting-service.mdx` Phase 4) and it is the one item in this document that touches the order flow.

### 5.4 The screens

The eleven screens are listed in `accounting-service.mdx` §"What the accounting tenant sees". **The design of every one of them follows the header standard Kens set for the whole app** (the 40x40 purple-to-indigo tile with a white 22px icon, the title beside it, the 14px grey description under it - the Order screen is the reference), and the responsive rules the other screens already follow.

**One difference from the POS screens, and it is deliberate:** the accounting screens live in their **own web app**, not in the POS frontend. The accountant's workflow - a journal, a trial balance, a year-end close - is not the cashier's, and mixing them makes both worse.

## The role gating

### 6.1 The plan - a feature row, and it is DATA

The entitlement is **not code**. It is a row in `plan_features` in the website API (`website_api/app/models/models.py:245`), read through `fetch_plan_features(customer_id)` (`backend/app/services/plan_features.py:71`) and stamped into the session at login (`backend/app/middleware/auth.py:839`).

So the accounting service is gated by a feature row - **`accounting`** - plus **`accounting_connector`** for the Xero / Bukku connection. Moving either between plans is a **data change** - exactly the pattern the delivery module already uses (`delivery_service.py:50` - "the assignment is DATA, so moving the module to another plan is a `plan_features` change and not a code change").

**The change under the standalone framing:** the website API's `subscription_plans` needs a **`product` discriminator** so the accounting catalogue is separate from the POS catalogue under one table. That is specified in `accounting-service.mdx` §"Billing - two shapes, one spine". The rule that the assignment is data - never code - does not change.

### 6.2 The roles

| Permission | owner | accountant | staff |
|---|---|---|---|
| `accounting:read` - the statements and the journals | yes | yes | **no** |
| `accounting:write` - manual journals, invoices, bills, reconciliation | yes | yes | **no** |
| `accounting:close` - lock a period, close a year | yes | **no** | no |

**Two deliberate lines.** The **floor does not see the books** - a staff member's job is the order, not the profit of the restaurant; that is the same decision the `staff` role already makes about the reports. And **closing a year is the owner's alone** - the same shape as the AI Manager's `ai:approve`, which the manager holds and the floor does not (`auth.py:45`, the comment on `ai:approve`). A year-end close is a statement to the tax authority and to the bank; one person signs it.

**The new role is `accountant`.** A standalone accounting service has a person the POS never had: an external accountant who needs the books and the journals but must not close the year or change the chart. The role exists for exactly that person, and it is why the accountant workspace (`accounting-service.mdx` Phase 7) can be one login across many books.

## What this design does NOT claim

- **It does not claim the tenant's books are statutory records.** The service produces a proper double-entry ledger, but the tenant's accountant and the tax authority decide what is filed. Nothing here is a certified accounting system.
- **It does not claim to replace the e-Invoice rail.** §2.4. The tax document is `malaysia-e-invoice.mdx`'s job.
- **It does not claim to make the tenant's reporting work in another currency.** §4.2 - amounts in different currencies are never summed, and the service says so rather than guessing a rate.
- **It does not claim the historical data is converted.** A tenant who switches on in November gets **opening balances**, not a reconstructed past. See `connectors-and-switching.mdx` §5.
- **It does not claim to be an audit.** The audit rule (§3.2) makes the books honest and traceable; it does not make them audited.
- **It does not claim the connector is two-way.** Ours is the source of truth; the connector pushes.
- **It does not claim a bank feed.** Bank transactions are entered or imported; a live feed is later.
- **It does not claim to hold payroll.** Payroll postings are accepted; payroll is a later module.

## The open questions

1. **Cash basis or accrual?** Most small restaurants report on a cash basis - the money is the money. Accrual (and the debtor ledger) implies a choice. Our PROPOSAL: cash basis by default, accrual available per accounting tenant.
2. **Is the fiscal year the calendar year?** The PROPOSAL is yes by default and settable. A tenant who files on a fiscal year ending in June sets it once.
3. **Two base currencies under one platform customer?** The PROPOSAL is one per accounting tenant - one legal entity, one functional currency - and the consolidated view across entities with different currencies **shows no single number**. This is a change from the POS-only version, where the tenant was the group.
4. **Who signs the year?** `accounting:close` is the owner today (§6.2). Should it be grantable to an accountant who is not the owner - the owner of a group with a bookkeeper outside the business?
5. **How much history do we import?** The PROPOSAL is opening balances only. A tenant who wants March through October reconstructed needs the float order totals - which means importing the orders and re-running the rules, and the frozen costs for old orders may be NULL for orders placed before the freeze existed.
6. **Does a credit sale change the order flow's shape?** It introduces the debtor at the counter. That touches the payment panel and the receipt - the two screens Kens and the team look at most. The alternative (a credit sale only from the Accounting app, creating an invoice that is not an order) is less coherent but much smaller.
7. **Does the bank reconciliation need to be in this project?** Posting bills and payments is one thing; reconciling a bank statement against them is more work.
8. **Should the rounding account exist per currency?** A rounding difference in MYR and one in USD cannot share one account without summing across currencies.
