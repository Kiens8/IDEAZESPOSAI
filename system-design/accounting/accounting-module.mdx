## The shape of it

**The POS already knows every number an accountant needs. It just cannot add them up.**

A tenant's own outlet sold 200 covers last month. The POS holds the gross, the
discount, the SST, the payment method of every single one of them, the recipe cost
of every dish, the purchase orders that filled the fridge and the stock movement
that recorded the waste. What it cannot do is say **RM 41,280 revenue, RM 1,914
cost of goods, RM 39,366 gross profit** - because there is no chart of accounts,
no journal, no fiscal year, and no statement of any kind in the product today.

**This is a plan to add one.** A **per-tenant set of books** inside the platform:

- a **chart of accounts** the tenant owns (seeded with a restaurant chart, editable),
- **double-entry journals** written automatically when money moves (an order paid, a
  purchase received, a stock bin wasted),
- **fiscal years and periods** that can be locked so last quarter cannot be rewritten,
- **the statements** - profit and loss, balance sheet, trial balance, general ledger,
  tax summary - and a screen the tenant's accountant can open.

**Two things it is not.** It is **not** a second e-Invoice system (`malaysia-e-invoice.mdx`
owns that rail - §2.4). And it is **not** us becoming an accounting software vendor
that competes with AutoCount in Malaysia or Xero in the world - the **feature set is
modelled on Bukku and Xero**, and §8 is the connector that lets a tenant who already
keeps their books in Xero or Bukku push ours into theirs.

## Why this is a POS problem and not an accounting problem

Two facts make the books a natural part of this product rather than a bolt-on:

1. **The transaction is the clock.** A restaurant sells 200 covers a day, and every
   one of them is a fact the POS already holds in its own tables. An accountant
   working from a PDF of the sales loses the discount source, the payment method and
   the line cost - the three things that decide whether the number is right.
2. **The tenant already lives in this product.** They chose it because it runs the
   floor. Asking them to re-key the same month into a second system is the failure
   mode of every accounting add-on ever bolted onto a POS.

**And one fact makes it hard, which is why the market looks the way it does.** In
Malaysia the incumbent pattern (recorded in `malaysia-e-invoice.mdx` §"How the market
does it") is **POS -> accounting -> a separate e-Invoice submission**: AutoCount is
the Malaysian accounting/POS incumbent, and the supplier issues the receipt while the
accounting system files the month with LHDN. **The gap we can own** is the middle
step - the books themselves - because the POS is the only place that has the
transaction *and* the cost *and* the tax treatment *at the moment of the sale*.

**Two findings that shape the whole design.**

**Finding 1 - the money in the POS is a float, and a ledger cannot be a float.**
`Order.subtotal`, `discount`, `tax`, `total` and `Payment.amount` are all `Float`
(`models.py:284-295`, `models.py:379`). A float cannot hold 0.1 + 0.2, and a ledger
that balances to the cent is the entire point. Meanwhile the purchase side is
already **integer cents** (`PurchaseOrder.total_amount`, `PurchaseOrderItem.unit_price`
and `subtotal` - `supplier.py:67`, `:93`, `:95`) and the supplier price list is
**DECIMAL(10,4)** (`supplier.py:45`). So the codebase already carries three money
representations. §4.1 settles this.

**Finding 2 - the platform already has a proven tenant rail, and the books must ride it.**
Every reporting endpoint answers the same four questions before it answers the fifth
(`reports.py:18-24`): *who may call it* (`require_staff_permission`), *whose data*
(`require_outlet_tenant` - the outlet must belong to the tenant of the session, **never
to a `customer_id` in the query string**), *did the tenant buy it*
(`require_plan_feature`), *does the UI agree*. The accounting module answers the same
four questions the same way. Nothing new is invented.

## The design - the shape of the books

### 2.1 Who owns the books

**One set of books per tenant. The outlet is a dimension inside them, not a second set of books.**

The tenant is the `Customer` row (`models.py:26`) - the restaurant group - which
carries `website_customer_id` (`models.py:40`), the link to the website API where
the plan lives. An `Outlet` (`models.py:86`) is a branch of that group. A tenant with
three branches wants three sets of P&L columns and **one** balance sheet, the way a
real group reports - not three disconnected books and not one book that cannot say
which branch earned it.

So every accounting row carries `customer_id` (the tenant) **and** `outlet_id`
(the branch the money moved in). The tenant is the owner; the outlet is the filter.
The financial year belongs to the tenant.

### 2.2 The components

| Component | What it holds | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Chart of accounts** | the tenant's accounts, their type, their mapping to the POS | `acc_accounts` |
| **Journal** | the postings - one header, many lines, debits equal credits | `acc_journals` + `acc_journal_lines` |
| **Fiscal calendar** | the years and the periods, each open / locked / closed | `acc_fiscal_years` + `acc_periods` |
| **Sales ledger (AR)** | invoices raised from orders, what is owed, what is paid | `acc_invoices` + `acc_debtors` |
| **Purchase ledger (AP)** | bills raised from received purchase orders, what is owed to suppliers | `acc_bills` |
| **Bank** | the cash and card accounts, the transactions, the reconciliation | `acc_bank_accounts` + `acc_bank_transactions` |
| **Tax** | the SST / VAT / service tax rates and the codes they post to | `acc_tax_codes` (seeded from `OutletSettings`) |
| **Connector** (optional) | the tenant's Xero or Bukku account and the mapping to it | `acc_connections` + `acc_connection_mappings` |
| **Statement engine** | P&L, balance sheet, trial balance, general ledger, tax summary | reads the journal; writes nothing |

**The chart of accounts is the tenant's, not ours.** We seed it with a restaurant
chart at first use; from that day the tenant edits it. Deleting an account that has
postings is refused - the postings would lose their meaning. This is the same shape
as the Xero and Bukku models, and it is what makes the connector in §8 possible.

### 2.3 The account types - chosen to map 1:1 to Bukku and Xero

The type list is taken from the **Bukku** account model, which is the same shape
Xero uses and covers every accounting standard we care about:

`current_assets`, `non_current_assets`, `other_assets`, `current_liabilities`,
`non_current_liabilities`, `equity`, `income`, `other_income`, `expenses`,
`cost_of_sales`

Plus Bukku's `system_type` - `accounts_receivable`, `accounts_payable`, `inventory` -
for the three accounts the system itself must know about. Those three are what make
an auto-posting rule possible ("this order paid on credit goes to the receivable
account"), and they are why the seed chart is marked `is_system` and cannot be
deleted.

**The seeded restaurant chart** (PROPOSAL - the tenant renames it freely):

| Code | Account | Type | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1000 | Cash on hand | current_assets | the till |
| 1010 | Bank - settlement | current_assets | card / QR / e-wallet money in transit |
| 1100 | Accounts receivable | current_assets | system type - what a corporate table owes |
| 1200 | Inventory | current_assets | system type - ingredients on hand |
| 2000 | Accounts payable | current_liabilities | system type - what we owe suppliers |
| 2100 | SST payable | current_liabilities | the tax we collected and must remit |
| 2200 | Service tax payable | current_liabilities | the 10% service charge tax |
| 3000 | Owner's capital | equity | the money the owner put in |
| 3100 | Retained earnings | equity | last year's profit, carried |

| 4010 | Beverage sales | income | the bar |

### 2.4 The rail this module must not cross: the e-Invoice

**The e-Invoice is a tax document for LHDN. The books are the tenant's internal
record. They are not the same thing, and this module does not replace the
e-Invoice rail.**

`malaysia-e-invoice.mdx` already draws the line: the tax invoice is issued on
request at the counter, the monthly consolidated e-Invoice is the artefact, and the
whole flow is a *document* pipeline (UBL 2.1 JSON, a taxpayer id, a certificate).

What this module produces instead: **journals, accounts, periods and statements.**
The overlap is exactly one field - the tax amount - and the rule is:

> The **tax amount** comes from the POS pricing service (which already computes it
> correctly on the amount the guest pays - `order_pricing.py:61`), and the same
> amount is posted to the tax payable account. The **e-Invoice** rail reads that
> amount for its document. Neither rail computes tax on its own, and neither one
> may quietly restate the other.

The tenant's SST return and their accountant's books both fall out of the same
journal. A separate tax computation would be a third answer to the same question,
and three answers is one too many.

### 3.1 The tables

All PROPOSAL. Every one carries `customer_id` (the tenant) and, where the money

**The token columns are encrypted at rest.** The e-Invoice module already holds ERP
credentials encrypted (`malaysia-e-invoice.mdx` §"The tables" - `client_id` /
`client_secret` encrypted, the tenant's own). The connector holds an accounting
provider's token, which is the same class of secret - it can move money in the
tenant's books.

### 3.2 The audit rule

**A posted journal is never edited and never deleted.** A mistake is corrected by
posting a **reversal** that carries `reverses_journal_id` pointing at the original,
and then a fresh journal. The original stays, so the tenant's accountant can see
what happened and when it was corrected.

This is the same rule the POS already follows with the receipt reprint log and the
void report (`reports.py:412`): money that moved leaves a mark. A ledger without
that rule is a spreadsheet with extra steps.

### 3.3 The fiscal year, the periods and the lock

- A tenant's **fiscal year** is created at first use and can be named freely
  (`FY2026`, `Jan-Dec 2026`). It matches the calendar year by default; a tenant who
  files on a different year sets it once.
- A **period** is a month inside the year. Months are created automatically when
  the year is.
- **Locking a period** is the point of the whole feature. When a period is locked:
  - a journal dated inside it is **refused** - the API answers `409` with the
    period name and the date that would have been posted;
  - a **posting dated today** that belongs to last month is **refused** the same way;
  - a **reversal** of a journal inside a locked period is refused - the correction
    belongs in the current open period, which is how a real accountant does it;
  - the locked period's balances are **frozen** - the statement of that month can
    never change again, which is what makes the number the tenant's accountant
    signed.

Closing a year additionally carries the **retained earnings** into the next year's
opening balances and posts the **closing entry**. That is the only entry the system
writes on its own without a source document.

## The design - the money

### 4.1 Money is integer minor units, everywhere, in the books

**Every `*_minor` column is an integer number of cents. Never a float. Never a
decimal string.** `RM 41,280.50` is stored as `4128050` with `currency = 'MYR'`.

Why, given the POS already uses floats:

- A ledger that balances to the cent is the entire point. Float arithmetic does not
  guarantee it.
- The purchase side is **already integer cents** (`supplier.py:67`, `:93`). The books
  read from those rows directly; making the sales side cents too means the two
  halves of the business use one arithmetic.
- The one rounding that must happen is **at the boundary**, and it must happen
  **once**. When a POS order is posted, the float order total is converted to cents
  **once**, half-up, and **that rounded value is the ledger's truth**. The ledger
  never re-reads the float. A float `total` of `123.456789` becomes `12346` cents -
  and the order screen shows the same `123.46` the guest paid, because the rounding
  difference is under a cent and the ledger absorbs it in the rounding account.

**A rounding account is a first-class account** (`Rounding differences`,
`other_income`), so that the pennies that arise from the float boundary have a home
and the trial balance still balances to the cent.

### 4.2 The chart of accounts is the tenant's

Seeded once per tenant at first use from §2.3, then it is theirs. They rename
`4000 Food sales` to whatever their accountant calls it. They add `6100 Gas
bills`. They can restrict an account to one outlet (`outlet_id`) - a tenant whose
kitchen and its bar file separately may want a `5100 Bar supplies` account that only
the bar posts to.

Three accounts are **system** accounts: `Accounts receivable`, `Accounts payable`
and `Inventory`. The auto-posting rules (§4.5) need them, so they cannot be deleted
and they cannot be moved to another type.

### 4.3 Currency: one book, several currencies, never one sum

The tenant's books have a **base currency** (the outlet's `currency_code`,
`models.py:420`). A transaction in another currency is stored in its own currency
**and** in the base currency at the rate of the day, in the same journal - two
`currency` values on the lines.

**The rule from `multi-currency-money.mdx` holds without exception: amounts in
different currencies are never summed.** The P&L is reported per currency, or in
the base currency at a stated rate with the rate visible. The same rule already
governs the admin dashboard, so the tenant sees one behaviour across the product.

### 4.4 When a journal is written

A journal is written when **money moves**, not when a screen is opened. The
triggers, all PROPOSAL:

| Trigger | Source of truth | What is posted |
|---|---|---|
| An order is **paid** | `Order.payment_status` becomes `paid` | the sale (§4.5) |
| An order is **voided** | `Order.status` becomes `cancelled` | a reversal of the sale journal |
| An order is **refunded** | `Payment` / `OrderPayment` shows a refund | a reversal of the payment journal |
| A purchase order is **received** | `PurchaseOrder.status` becomes `received` | the stock in, the bill out (§5.2) |
| Stock is **wasted or adjusted** | `stock_movements` with `waste` / `adjustment` | the expense out, the stock down |
| A **bill is paid** | `acc_bills.paid_minor` changes | the payable down, the bank down |
| An **invoice is paid** | `acc_invoices.paid_minor` changes | the receivable down, the bank up |
| A **bank transaction is imported** | a statement import | the bank against a suspense account |
| A **period is closed** | the year-end close | retained earnings, and nothing else |

**One rule on timing:** the POS knows the moment of the sale, but a restaurant with
`prepay_online` and `postpay` (`models.py:272`) has two different cash moments. A
journal is dated when the **payment** is taken, and an unpaid order posts nothing
until it is paid - unless it is a credit sale to a corporate debtor (§5.3), which
posts on the invoice.

### 4.5 The auto-posting rules - the heart of the module

Every rule below is PROPOSAL, and every rule is **per tenant, per outlet,
configurable** - the tenant can point any of these at a different account. The
defaults are what §2.3 describes.

**The sale.** One order paid RM 100.00 (subtotal RM 100.00, discount RM 10.00,
SST RM 5.40, paid by card):

| Line | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `1010 Bank - settlement` | RM 95.40 | |
| 2 | `4100 Discount given` | RM 10.00 | |
| 3 | `4000 Food sales` | | RM 100.00 |
| 4 | `2100 SST payable` | | RM 5.40 |

Debits RM 105.40, credits RM 105.40. The tax is on the amount the guest **pays**
(RM 90.00) - which is exactly what `order_pricing.py:61` already does, so the
book and the receipt say the same number.

**The discount is its own account, not a smaller sale.** `Order.discount_source`
(`models.py:292`) says whether it came from a voucher, a promotion or the manager's
own hand - and those are three different things to an accountant (a voucher breaks a
liability, a promotion is marketing spend, a manual discount is contra revenue). We
post it to `4100 Discount given` and record the source on the line, so the tenant
can split the three later without re-reading the orders.

**The payment method picks the debit account.** Cash goes to `1000 Cash on hand`;
card, DuitNow QR, Touch 'n Go, GrabPay and Boost go to `1010 Bank - settlement`
until the money actually lands - which is why that account exists and why it is a
settlement account and not a bank account.

**The cost of goods.** When the order is ready, the POS already writes the stock
movement (`order.py:236`, `movement_type='usage'`) and it already froze the line
cost (`OrderItem.frozen_cost`, `models.py:338`). The journal reads the frozen
cost - **never the live recipe price**, because the margin of an order sold in
October must not be rewritten by October's price:

| Line | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `5000 Cost of goods sold` | RM 21.30 | |
| 2 | `1200 Inventory` | | RM 21.30 |

**The frozen cost is the same number the Reports screen uses.** The margin rule in
`reports.py:96-130` - the recipe when the tenant has Inventory, the estimate
otherwise, and `cost_known: false` (never zero) when neither - must be the **one**
rule. The accounting margin and the operational margin must not disagree; if they
do, one of them is wrong and the tenant cannot tell which.

**A line with no known cost posts nothing to COGS**, and the tenant is told. It
does not post zero cost - zero is a lie, and `frozen_cost` is already NULL for
"unknown" rather than 0.0 for that reason.

**The reversal.** An order voided after it was paid posts the mirror image of the
sale journal, with `reverses_journal_id` set. The stock movement that came with it
is reversed the same way. The void report (`reports.py:412`) and the books tell the
same story.

### 4.6 The manual journal

A tenant's accountant will need to post something the POS did not generate: an
accrual, an adjustment, a depreciation, a correction. So there is a **manual
journal** screen - but it is a **journal**, not a shortcut around one: it has the
same debit/credit lines, the same period lock, the same audit rule, and it posts
into the same charts. The only difference is that its `source_type` is `manual`
and it has a memo instead of an order.

## The design - the statements and the screens

### 5.1 What the statements are

| Statement | The question it answers | Built from |
|---|---|---|
| **Profit and loss** | what did the business earn, and what did it cost? | journal lines in the period, grouped by account type |
| **Balance sheet** | what does the tenant own, owe, and have left? | journal lines up to the date, grouped by balance-sheet type |
| **Trial balance** | does the whole thing add up? | every account's debit and credit total, and the grand total that must be equal |
| **General ledger** | what happened to this one account? | the lines of one account, in date order |
| **Tax summary** | what SST did we collect, and what may we claim? | the tax-coded lines by `acc_tax_codes` |
| **Cash and bank summary** | where is the money? | the bank accounts' balances, reconciled and not |
| **Sales by category** | which menu lines earn, and which only fill seats? | the same revenue accounts, with the `frozen_cost` margin |

**Every statement is per outlet and consolidated**, in one screen: pick the outlet
or pick "all outlets". The consolidation is the tenant's whole group; the outlet
columns are its branches.

**The trial balance is the honesty check.** If the debits and the credits differ by
one cent, the statements are not shown as final - the screen says so and names the
out-of-balance amount. A platform that shows a P&L from an unbalanced ledger is
teaching the tenant to trust a number that is wrong.

### 5.2 Purchases - the bill

When a `PurchaseOrder` reaches `received` (`supplier.py:60`), the books post it:

| Line | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `1200 Inventory` (or `5100 Purchases`) | the ingredient's value | |
| 2 | `2000 Accounts payable` | | the supplier's total |

The bill is then paid by a bank transaction: `2000 Accounts payable` down,
`1010 Bank - settlement` down. The purchase order's money is **already integer
cents** (`supplier.py:67`, `:93`), so this path needs no conversion at all - which
is exactly why §4.1 chose cents.

### 5.3 Receivables - the debtor the POS does not have

The POS has no debtor entity today (§3). A corporate table that eats every month
and pays at the end needs one, or the books cannot show what is owed.

`acc_debtors` is that entity: name, phone, tax id, credit limit. An order can be
raised against a debtor instead of being paid at the counter - the invoice posts
`1100 Accounts receivable` instead of the bank, and the payment later clears it.
**This is a new POS behaviour, not only a new screen**, so it is Phase 4 (§9) and
it is the one item in this document that touches the order flow.

## The plan and the role gating

### 6.1 The plan - two feature rows, and they are DATA

The plan assignment is **not code**. It is a row in `plan_features` in the website
API (`website_api/app/models/models.py:245`), and the POS reads it through
`fetch_plan_features(customer_id)` (`backend/app/services/plan_features.py:71`) and
stamps the list into the session at login (`backend/app/middleware/auth.py:839`).

So the accounting module is gated by **two new feature rows**:

| Feature row | Unlocks |
|---|---|
| `Accounting` | the books, the journals, the statements, the Accounting section in the navigation |
| `Accounting Connector` | the Xero / Bukku connection and its mapping screens (§8) |

Moving either between plans is a **data change** - exactly the pattern the delivery
module already uses (`delivery_service.py:50` - "the assignment is DATA, so moving
the module to another plan is a `plan_features` change and not a code change").

The POS enforces the row with `require_plan_feature("Accounting")` - the same
dependency the Reports screen uses (`reports.py:23`). The frontend asks the same
entitlements endpoint it asks today (`entitlements.py:97` `/features`,
`entitlements.py:120` `/plan-limits`), so the navigation and the API cannot
disagree.

### 6.2 The roles

The accounting permissions are added to `ROLE_PERMISSIONS`
(`backend/app/middleware/auth.py:45`) as a new namespace:

| Permission | admin | manager | staff |
|---|---|---|---|
| `accounting:read` - the statements and the journals | yes | yes | **no** |
| `accounting:write` - manual journals, invoices, bills, reconciliation | yes | yes | **no** |
| `accounting:close` - lock a period, close a year | yes | **no** | no |

**Two deliberate lines.** The **floor does not see the books** - a staff member's
job is the order, not the profit of the restaurant; that is the same decision the
`staff` role already makes about the reports. And **closing a year is the owner's
alone** - the same shape as the AI Manager's `ai:approve`, which the manager holds
and the floor does not (`auth.py:45`, the comment on `ai:approve`). A year-end close
is a statement to the tax authority and to the bank; one person signs it.

### 6.3 The tenant rail - unchanged, and the same four questions

Every accounting endpoint answers the four questions the Reports screen answers
(`reports.py:18-24`), in the same order:

1. **Who may call it** - `require_staff_permission("accounting:read")` or
   `accounting:write`, namespaced `staff:` so the frontend key satisfies it through
   its `staff:*` grant while the customer key and the external key - which carry no
   `staff:*` grant at all - are refused with 403 (`auth.py:765-790`).
2. **Whose data** - `require_tenant_claim(request)` (`app/core/tenant.py:77`) then
   `require_outlet_tenant(db, outlet_id, tenant_id)` (`app/core/tenant.py:188`).
   **The tenant is never taken from a query string.** A caller who asks for
   another tenant's `outlet_id` is refused, exactly as today.
3. **Did the tenant buy it** - `require_plan_feature("Accounting")`.
4. **Does the UI agree** - the navigation asks the same entitlements endpoint the
   API uses.

**There is no new tenant rule in this module, and that is the point.** The books
are the tenant's most sensitive data in the product - the profit, the payroll
provider's invoices, the bank balance - and they ride the rail that was already
built and proved, unchanged.

## The phased plan

Each phase says what it does **not** do, because the boundary is the design.

### Phase 0 - the foundation (the tables, the chart, the years)

The accounting tables, the seeded chart of accounts, the fiscal years and periods,
the account settings, the money conversion at the boundary, the permissions, the
plan rows.

**Does NOT:** post any journal, show any statement, or change any order. The books
exist and are empty. Phase 0 is where the migration is written and the rounding
rule is proved against the existing float totals.

### Phase 1 - the sale

The sale journal, the discount line, the tax line, the payment-method routing, the
reversal on a void, the cost of goods from `frozen_cost`, and the period lock.

**Does NOT:** handle purchases, receivables, the bank, or a year-end close. One
journal family only.

### Phase 2 - the purchases and the stock

The bill from a received purchase order, the waste and adjustment journals, the
payment of a bill, the purchase ledger screen.

**Does NOT:** touch the sale side, which Phase 1 already froze - if Phase 2
reveals a mistake in the sale rules, the sale rules change and the affected periods
are re-opened by an admin, deliberately and visibly.

### Phase 3 - the statements

P&L, balance sheet, trial balance, general ledger, tax summary, the cash summary -
per outlet and consolidated, printable and exportable, with the trial-balance check
that refuses to present an unbalanced ledger as final.

**Does NOT:** make any number up. Every figure is a sum of posted journal lines.

### Phase 4 - the receivables

The debtor entity, the credit sale at the counter, the AR ledger, the invoice
numbering, the overdue view. **This is the only phase that changes the order
flow**, so it is the last of the core.

**Does NOT:** change the walk-in path, the prepayment path, or anything about how
a normal customer pays.

### Phase 5 - the fiscal year

The year-end close, the retained earnings carry-forward, the opening balances for
a tenant who joins mid-life with money already in the bank, and the export of a
closed year to their accountant.

**Does NOT:** re-open a closed year on its own. An admin may re-open a locked
period - it is recorded, and the re-opening is visible in the audit.

### Phase 6 - the connector

The Xero and Bukku connection, the account mapping, the push of journals and
invoices, the sync log. This is `external-connector.mdx` and it is **optional for
every tenant** - the books in Phase 5 are complete without it.

**Does NOT:** make the external system the source of truth. Ours is; the connector
pushes.

## What this design does NOT claim

- **It does not claim the tenant's books are statutory records.** The platform
  produces a proper double-entry ledger, but the tenant's accountant and the tax
  authority decide what is filed. Nothing here is a certified accounting system.
- **It does not claim to replace the e-Invoice rail.** §2.4. The tax document is
  `malaysia-e-invoice.mdx`'s job.
- **It does not claim to make the tenant's reporting work in another currency.**
  §4.3 - amounts in different currencies are never summed, and the platform says so
  rather than guessing a rate.
- **It does not claim the historical data is converted.** A tenant who switches on
  in November gets **opening balances**, not a reconstructed past. The POS's own
  reports remain the history.
- **It does not claim to be an audit.** The audit rule (§3.2) makes the books
  honest and traceable; it does not make them audited.
- **It does not claim the connector is two-way.** §8 - ours is the source of truth.
- **It does not claim a bank feed.** Bank transactions are entered or imported; a
  live feed is a separate project.

## The open questions

1. **Cash basis or accrual?** Most small restaurants report on a cash basis - the
   money is the money. Accrual (and the debtor ledger in Phase 4) implies a
   choice. Our PROPOSAL: cash basis by default, accrual available per tenant.
2. **Is the fiscal year the calendar year?** The PROPOSAL is yes by default and
   settable. A tenant who files on a fiscal year ending in June sets it once.
3. **Do we support more than one base currency per tenant?** The PROPOSAL is no -
   one base, many transaction currencies. A group with, say, a Malaysian and a
   Cambodian entity under one tenant may need two sets of books. That would break
   §2.1's "one set of books per tenant".
4. **Who signs the year?** `accounting:close` is the admin today (§6.2). Should a
   tenant be able to grant it to a specific accountant who is not the admin - the
   owner of a group with a bookkeeper outside the business?
5. **Does the connector need to be able to pull back?** Today the design is
   push-only. If a tenant's accountant edits a journal in Xero, we do not see it.
   Two-way sync is a different product.
6. **How much history is migrated?** The PROPOSAL is opening balances only (§
   "What this design does NOT claim"). A tenant who wants March through October
   reconstructed needs the float order totals - which is possible, but it means
   importing the orders and re-running the rules, and the frozen costs for old
   orders may be NULL for orders placed before the freeze existed.
7. **Does a credit sale change the order flow's shape?** Phase 4 introduces the
   debtor at the counter. That touches the payment panel and the receipt - the two
   screens Kens and the team look at most. The alternative (a credit sale only
   from the Accounting section, creating an invoice that is not an order) is less
   coherent but much smaller.
8. **Per-outlet or per-tenant books for the balances?** The PROPOSAL is one set of
   books with the outlet as a dimension. A tenant whose branches are separate
   legal entities wants separate balance sheets - that is question 3 again, seen
   from the legal side.
9. **Does the bank reconciliation need to be in this project?** Phase 2 posts
   bills and payments; reconciling a bank statement against them is more work and
   could be a Phase 7.
