## What this document is

**IDEAZES is a customer of its own accounting product, and the first tenant of it.**

That sentence has two halves, and they are different jobs.

1. **We are a customer of the product.** Our own business runs on the accounting service. That means an **internal accounting tenant** - books, chart of accounts, fiscal periods, a legal entity - belonging to the IDEAZES operating entity. It is granted, not bought, and it is the first tenant that exists, which makes it the reference tenant, the test tenant, and the demo tenant.
2. **Our own revenue flows through the same machine.** When a customer subscribes to a POS plan and pays, that transaction is **our revenue**, and it belongs in our books. Today it does not go anywhere: the invoice is written (`website_api/app/models/models.py:162`), the money lands, and the only record of it is the admin dashboard's aggregate (`website_api/app/routes/admin.py:243-245`). **Our books are the accounting product's own first customer, and the connector that carries our subscription revenue is the connector that proves the connector design works.**

**This document specifies the internal tenant, the billing connector, the revenue recognition it needs, and the rules that keep our books separate from every customer's data.** It does not change the tenant model, the ledger, the purchase path or the POS binding. It adds a tenant, a source, and a set of recognition rules - and it forces three gaps in the live billing system to be named, because a revenue connector that cannot answer "how much tax" and "what did the gateway keep" produces books that are confidently wrong.

**The three facts that shape everything below:**

- **Our subscription invoices carry no tax and no fee.** Verified: there is **no tax column** and **no fee column** on `SubscriptionPlan` or `Invoice` (`models.py:6`, `:162`), and no occurrence of "fee" anywhere in `website_api/`. Our revenue is therefore **gross of SST** and **gross of gateway fees**, and a book that records only what the invoice says is a book that overstates revenue twice.
- **An invoice does not carry the service period it paid for.** The period lives on the subscription (`models.py:95`, `current_period_start` / `current_period_end`), and **the renewal job advances it at the moment it issues the invoice** (`website_api/scripts/send_notifications.py:617-626`). A connector that reads the subscription's period later reads the *wrong* period for every invoice but the newest.
- **The live billing system can sum across currencies in the customer's billing summary.** `website_api/app/routes/billing.py:553-563` adds every invoice amount and returns a single figure labelled `"currency": "MYR"`. The admin dashboard does not - it groups by currency (`routes/admin.py:243-245`, via `services/money.py:25`). **Our books must follow the admin dashboard's rule and not the summary's.**

## 1. The internal tenant

### 1.1 What it is

**An accounting tenant, belonging to a legal entity, that we grant to ourselves.**

It is **not** a special mode, a demo tenant, or a read-only view of the POS. It is a tenant of the product with its own books, its own chart of accounts, its own fiscal calendar and its own audit trail - reached through the same API, with the same isolation rules, by the same code paths as every paying customer.

| What | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **The platform customer** | a `website_customers` row for the IDEAZES operating entity | the accounting tenant hangs off a platform customer, exactly as a customer's does (`accounting-service.mdx` §"The service boundary") |
| **The accounting tenant** | one accounting tenant, one legal entity | one legal entity per tenant, so one set of books |
| **The base currency** | **MYR** | the entity is Malaysian; the base currency follows the functional currency (`ledger-and-money.mdx` §4.2) |
| **The entitlement** | **a granted feature, not a purchase** | see §2 |
| **The access** | a named founder allowlist, audited | our revenue and our margins are not customer-facing (§6) |

**The legal entity is a decision for Kens, not for this document.** It must be the entity that actually sells the subscriptions - because our revenue lands in *that* entity's books - and it must be the entity that files the SST return. A group with an operating company and a holding company has two books, not one, and this document does not paper over it.

### 1.2 What it is for

**Four jobs, and only the first is glamorous.**

1. **It is the reference tenant.** Every question about the accounting service is answered on our own books first: does the trial balance balance, does a period lock hold, does a connector retry cleanly, does a restore bring the books back. **We answer those questions on our own data before a customer does.**
2. **It is the revenue connector's first user.** Our subscription revenue is the first fact stream that has to land in a book by itself, with nobody typing (§3).
3. **It is our own accounts.** We have to keep books for the company anyway. Producing them with the product we sell is the cheapest way to get them, and it means the product is exercised by a real business's real volume rather than by a test fixture.
4. **It is the demo.** Anonymised, our books are the most honest demo there is - a real restaurant group's numbers, in our own product, with the connector that filled them.

### 1.3 What it is not

- **Not a way to give away the product.** The internal tenant is granted, and granting it is an **audit-recorded row**, not a bypass.
- **Not a self-sale.** We do not invoice ourselves, we do not charge ourselves, and **our own use of our own product creates no revenue and no intercompany balance** (§2.3). "We are our own customer" is true operationally and false in the accounts, and the design keeps them apart.
- **Not a place to put customer data.** Our books contain our revenue; our books never contain a customer's books (§6).

## 2. The entitlement - a grant, not a purchase

### 2.1 The mechanism that already exists

**We grant the accounting product to ourselves exactly the way an admin grants an add-on to a customer today.**

The live code has one mechanism for "this customer has this feature without buying it": the `CustomerFeatureOverride` row (`website_api/app/models/models.py:299`), written by `POST /api/admin/customer-features` (`website_api/app/routes/admin.py:969`). It carries the feature, an `enabled` flag, a `reason`, and who wrote it - **an audit trail in the row itself.**

The entitlement read that Phase 0b turns into a union already counts an enabled override as one of its three sources (`purchase-path.mdx` §"Decision 5"). **So the internal tenant's entitlement needs no new mechanism and no new code path**: one row, for the `accounting` feature, for our platform customer id, with `reason = 'IDEAZES internal tenant'`.

**One addition the union needs, and it is small.** The override names a **feature**, and the union is **product-scoped**. So the feature row needs the `product` discriminator that Phase 0b adds anyway (`purchase-path.mdx` §"Decision 5" - `features.product`), and the override must point at the feature whose `product == 'accounting'`. A grant of the POS's own features to our own tenant is a different thing and is not what this document is about.

### 2.2 The row

```
features:
    name = 'accounting'   product = 'accounting'   is_active = 1

customer_feature_overrides:
    customer_id = <the IDEAZES platform customer id>
    feature_id  = <the accounting feature>
    enabled     = 1
    reason      = 'IDEAZES internal tenant'
    created_by  = <the founder admin id>
```

**This is seeded data, not a script that runs on every boot.** The house rule holds: the database is the truth and the seed is only what a fresh install starts with (`website_api/app/core/ai_addon_prices.py` says so in as many words, with a `--check` migration that reports drift). **The override is created once, by an admin action, and it is visible in the admin UI like every other override** - so our own entitlement is auditable by the same screen that audits a customer's.

### 2.3 Why granting beats buying - and what it forecloses

**We do not create a subscription for ourselves, so no invoice, no payment, no revenue, no self-deal.**

This is the correct accounting answer, not just the convenient one. If we invoiced ourselves for our own use of our own product, we would have to recognise revenue we did not earn and carry an intercompany balance that has to be eliminated on consolidation - all to say something that is already true: the software was free, and it was ours.

**The rule, stated once:** *the internal tenant's use of the accounting product is granted, never billed.* If we ever want the books to show what our own use costs us, that is an **internal transfer at cost, posted as a manual journal with a memo**, and it is eliminated against the platform's own cost - not recognised as revenue. `ledger-and-money.mdx` §4.5 is the mechanism: a manual journal, same debit/credit lines, same period lock, same audit rule, with a memo instead of an order.

## 3. The billing connector - our revenue, into our books

### 3.1 The shape of it

**A source, registered against the internal tenant, that carries our subscription revenue into our books.**

In the connector vocabulary of `connectors-and-switching.mdx`, this is a new provider - `billing_ours` - with the same three obligations as every other connector: **an explicit source, an explicit idempotency key, and a visible failure.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Provider** | `billing_ours` |
| **Source of truth** | the website API's invoice stream - the billing database |
| **Writes into** | the internal tenant, and no other tenant |
| **Direction** | in |
| **Facts it carries** | invoice id, plan, product, billing cycle, amount, currency, service period, status, paid date |
| **What it must never carry** | customer identity (§3.4) |

**This is not the POS connector.** `pos_ours` reads a tenant's sales from the POS's snapshot API and writes into *that tenant's* books. `billing_ours` reads *our* revenue from the billing system and writes into *our* books. They share the connector interface and nothing else.

### 3.2 The read surface - and the three facts about it

**There is no service-facing read of invoices today, and the accounting service needs one.**

Verified, in the live code:

| Route | Auth | Who it serves | Usable by the accounting service? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `GET /api/billing/invoices` (`routes/billing.py:49`) | the logged-in **customer's** JWT (`get_current_user`) | one customer, their own invoices | **no** - it is scoped to one customer's token, and the accounting service is not a customer |
| `GET /api/billing/summary` (`routes/billing.py:547`) | the logged-in customer | one customer, a sum | **no** - same reason, and it sums across currencies (§0, third fact) |
| `GET /api/admin/invoices` (`routes/admin.py:667`) | an **admin** token (`get_current_admin`) | **every** invoice in the platform | **no** - an admin token is a human credential, and the accounting service is not an admin |
| `GET /api/admin/dashboard` (`routes/admin.py:209`) | an admin token | aggregates | **no**, and the aggregates are not journal facts |

**So the design needs one new route on the website API - read-only, scoped by a service key, and carrying no customer identity:**

```
GET /api/internal/billing/ledger-events?since=<cursor>
```

returning, per invoice: the invoice id, the plan's `product`, the billing cycle, the amount, the currency, the **service period the invoice covers**, the status, and the paid date. **No customer name, no email, no notes, no payment reference.** Our books need amounts; they do not need to know who paid.

**The scope discipline is the house's own** (`website_api/app/core/api_key_scope.py`): the accounting service gets **its own server key**, with its own scope, used only for this feed - and it is the key that authorises reading *our* revenue stream, not a customer's. A tenant key bound to one tenant may not read this feed at all.

**The route lives in `website_api/`, not in `backend/`.** That matters, because it keeps the central promise of `program-structure.mdx` §1 intact: **Phase 0 and Phase 1 change the POS backend zero times.** The billing spine is already being changed in Phase 0b; adding a read-only feed to it changes the spine, not the POS.

### 3.3 The period problem - and why it is a schema change

**This is the subtle one, and it is the reason the feed is not just "read the invoices".**

The service period a customer paid for lives on the subscription (`models.py:95`: `current_period_start`, `current_period_end`), **and the renewal job advances it at the moment it issues the next invoice** (`website_api/scripts/send_notifications.py:617-626`):

```
new_period_start = sub.current_period_end
...
sub.current_period_start = new_period_start
sub.current_period_end   = new_period_end
```

**So the subscription's period is not the period of the invoice you are reading.** After the next renewal, the subscription points at a later period, and a connector that reads `subscription.current_period_end` when it sees an old invoice will recognise that invoice's revenue **in the wrong period** - and for an annual plan, a year wrong.

**Two ways out, and the ledger needs the first:**

1. **The feed carries the period it actually covers.** The cleanest form is a **period snapshot on the invoice** - `period_start` and `period_end` columns on `invoices`, written when the invoice is created, from the same values the renewal job already computes. **This is the design's choice**, because the period is a fact about the invoice and an invoice should carry the period it paid for. It is a small addition to the billing schema and it is **Phase 0b work**, alongside the `product` discriminator and `feature_subscriptions`.
2. **The connector recomputes the period** from `invoice.created_at` plus the billing cycle. **This works and it is the fallback** - but it is a *guess* for a proration invoice and for an upgrade invoice, where the amount covers a partial period, and it is wrong for a promotion that changes the amount without changing the cycle.

**Rule: if the feed cannot state the period the invoice covers, the connector does not post the invoice.** It parks it in a dead letter with a named reason, and the operator sees a number that is missing rather than a number that is wrong. That is the whole connector discipline from `connectors-and-switching.mdx` applied to our own revenue.

### 3.4 What the feed must not carry

**Our books need amounts, not identities.** A revenue book records *what was sold and what was paid*, and the counterparty on our side is the world, not a named person.

So the feed carries **no customer name, no email, no notes, no payment reference**, and the connector's mapping writes **no counterparty detail** into the books. The audit trail for our revenue is the invoice id and the amount - and the invoice id resolves inside the billing system, where it belongs.

**This is not only privacy hygiene; it is the rule that stops our books becoming a customer database.** The moment our books hold customer names, the internal tenant becomes a shadow copy of the customer list, and every retention, access and export rule in the product has to be applied to it twice.

### 3.5 The idempotency key

**The invoice id plus the status transition.**

The billing system already treats an invoice as uniquely identified by `stripe_invoice_id` or `stripe_checkout_session_id`, and deliberately refuses to compare on a missing value (`website_api/app/routes/webhooks.py:163`, `find_invoice`). We use the same instinct:

```
idempotency key = (invoice_id, status, paid_at)
```

**Because an invoice's status can change after the fact.** The admin route can set any status on any invoice (`routes/admin.py:832-885` - `inv.status = request.status`), including `refunded` and `forgiven`, and `renewal-system.mdx:167` sets `forgiven` on an unpaid invoice when the grace runs out. **So a paid invoice can become a refunded invoice days later**, and the connector must see that as a **new event** and post a reversal - not as a duplicate and skip it.

The same shape handles a late payment: a `pending` invoice that becomes `paid` is a new event, and the revenue lands on the date it was actually paid (`paid_at`), not on the invoice's creation date.

## 4. The revenue recognition

**This is where the deferred revenue rules live, and they are not dogfood-specific.** Every prepaid annual plan needs them, whoever buys it - so this is Phase 1 ledger work, and the internal tenant is simply its first user.

### 4.1 Cash basis and accrual basis

`ledger-and-money.mdx` §4.1 and the open question at the end of that document record the choice: **cash basis by default, accrual available per accounting tenant.** The internal tenant runs **accrual**, because it has to - a prepaid annual subscription is a liability until the service is delivered, and Malaysian statutory reporting does not let us call it revenue on the day the money arrives.

**The rule, in one line:** *cash is recorded when it is received; revenue is recorded as the service is delivered.*

### 4.2 The journal rules

Every rule below is a proposal; the accounts are the F&B preset's, renamed for our own chart as any tenant may rename them (`ledger-and-money.mdx` §4.1).

**A monthly plan, paid on the 1st, RM 100.00:**

| Line | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `1010 Bank - settlement` | RM 100.00 | |
| 2 | `2400 Deferred revenue - subscriptions` | | RM 100.00 |

Then, over the service month, the release:

| Line | Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `2400 Deferred revenue - subscriptions` | RM 100.00 | |
| 2 | `4000 Subscription revenue` | | RM 100.00 |

**An annual plan, paid up front, RM 1,200.00 for twelve months:**

- On payment: **Dr `1010 Bank - settlement` RM 1,200.00 / Cr `2400 Deferred revenue - subscriptions` RM 1,200.00.** Nothing is revenue.
- Month by month: **RM 100.00 released** to `4000 Subscription revenue`, straight-line over the twelve months of the service period.
- At the end of the period the liability is zero, and the year's revenue is RM 1,200.00 - matched to the year the service was delivered.

**The release is straight-line over the service period, and it is driven by the period the feed carries** (§3.3). Not by the invoice's creation date, and not by the subscription's current period.

**A promotion** (`promo_price`, `promo_cycles_left` on the subscription, `models.py:95`): **the amount invoiced is already the promoted amount**, so recognition is unchanged in shape - the invoiced amount, ratably over the period it covers. **The promotion is not a separate discount account here**, because our books are not the customer's books: a discount given on *our* price is a reduction of *our* revenue, and the invoice already carries the reduced figure. (`ledger-and-money.mdx` §4.4 posts a discount given on *sales* to its own account, because in a restaurant the voucher is a liability and the promotion is marketing spend. **Those are different facts about different money.** Our side is one fact: what we were paid.)

**A failed payment** (`handle_payment_failed`, `routes/webhooks.py:1004-1030` - marks the invoice failed and does **not** revoke access): **no revenue, no cash, no journal.** The subscription's access is a separate matter from the revenue, and the billing system already keeps them separate.

**A forgiven invoice** (`renewal-system.mdx:167` - the grace ran out, the invoice is forgiven, the subscription expires): **no revenue.** Nothing was collected. The subscription's expiry is not our revenue reversing; it never became revenue.

**A cancelled invoice** (the voided proration, `routes/subscription.py:1295`): **no revenue.** It was never collectable.

**A refunded invoice** - an admin set the status to `refunded` on an invoice that was paid (`routes/admin.py:832-885`): **a reversing journal.** The original revenue is reversed for the amount recognised so far, the deferred liability for the unearned part is reversed, and the cash is credited. **The reversal is a new journal, not an edit of the old one** - the audit rule (`ledger-and-money.mdx`) means a posted journal is never rewritten.

**A zero-amount invoice** (trial or free plan, marked paid by `mark_zero_invoices_paid`, `scripts/send_notifications.py:491`): **no journal.** RM 0 is not revenue, and a zero line is refused by the ledger's balance rule anyway.

**A proration invoice** (an upgrade: `UPG-...`, `routes/subscription.py:1303`, amount computed by `services/upgrade_proration.py`): **recognised immediately, in the period it covers.** A proration is a true-up of a period already partly elapsed - the customer paid for the remainder of a period they have already had - so it is revenue **now**, not spread over a period that has already passed. The notes (`build_notes`, `upgrade_proration.py:102`) carry the arithmetic, and the feed must carry the period the proration covers so the connector can date it.

**A $0 trial invoice** is the same as the zero-amount case. **An invoice for a plan we do not sell** - not possible today, but the rule stands: **the connector recognises only what it can name.** An invoice whose plan has no `product`, or whose period it cannot state, is a dead letter with a named reason.

### 4.3 The tax

**Our invoices carry no tax, and our books must.**

Verified: **no tax column** on `SubscriptionPlan` or `Invoice` (`models.py:6`, `:162`), and no tax logic in the pricing path (`services/plan_pricing.py`). So an invoice is **gross of SST**, and our revenue account is currently a gross figure.

**This is a real gap with a real consequence.** The SST return for our subscription sales needs the output tax separated from the price charged. A book that records only the gross cannot produce that return, and an accountant reading our books will ask which number is the revenue.

**Two ways, and the design names both:**

1. **Put the tax in the price and record it on the invoice** - the invoice carries `tax_amount`, the customer sees the tax line (as Malaysian consumers expect), and the connector posts revenue net and SST payable gross. **This is the correct long-term answer**, and it changes what the customer sees, so it is a product decision for Kens, not a connector decision.
2. **Accrue the tax in our books** from a rate table and the invoiced gross, without changing the invoice. **This is the interim answer**: the books carry the tax, the invoice does not, and the two disagree until the price is fixed.

**Either way the books need a tax account and a rate** - `2100 SST payable` exists in the F&B preset, and `tax_settings` / `tax_codes` are in the accounting service's own schema (`program-structure.mdx` §3). **The rate is a business fact and is not hard-coded anywhere.** It must be confirmed for our service category before the first accrual, and the rate's effective date recorded with it - a rate that changes mid-year changes the accrual for the year.

**The interim answer is not free, and the cost is named:** with option 2, **our books' revenue and our SST return are computed from a tax figure the customer was never charged and never saw.** That is a reconciliation gap with the tax authority, not just with ourselves. **The design recommends option 1 before the accounting product has a paying customer**, and flags it as a decision Kens owns.

### 4.4 The gateway fee

**Same shape, and the honest answer is a monthly adjustment until the settlement is a feed.**

There is **no fee anywhere in `website_api/`** - no column, no field, no calculation. Our invoice is the gross; the gateway keeps its percentage before the money lands. So:

- **Our books are gross of fees at the transaction level**, and every invoice-level figure is overstated by the fee.
- **The net is a monthly fact**, because the fee is only knowable from the gateway's **settlement** - the payout that reconciles a month's charges to a month's money.

**The design: a monthly fee journal, from the settlement, with the settlement as the source document.** The fee is posted as an expense against the settlement's period, with the gateway settlement reference in the memo, and the reconciliation shows gross revenue, gateway fees, and net. **A settlement import is a connector of the same family** - a file or an API pull, idempotent per settlement period, dead-lettered if it does not parse.

**Until that exists, our books are gross and we say so.** A books page that says "gross of gateway fees, net not yet reconciled" is honest; a books page that says "revenue" and means gross is not.

### 4.5 The currency

**Two currencies, and no honest way to add them.**

Our revenue is in **MYR** (every HitPay method settles in MYR, always - `services/plan_pricing.py:48-53`) and in **USD** (a Stripe subscription keeps the plan's own currency). The house rule holds without exception: **amounts in different currencies are never summed** (`multi-currency-money.mdx`; `services/money.py:12` - "an exchange rate is a lie that gets older every minute").

**So the internal tenant's books are MYR-based, and USD invoices are held in USD** with a base-currency amount on the line at a stated rate - and the **rate source must be named and dated**, because there is **no exchange-rate table anywhere in the live code** (verified: no `exchange_rate` in `website_api/`). That is a **Phase 1 gap in the accounting service** - it owns its own rate table, with the rate, its date and its source recorded on the line, and a rate that gets older is visibly older.

**And the base currency is MYR** - which means every USD revenue figure in our books is a translation, and the translation rate is a fact with a date on it, not a preference.

### 4.6 The account set for our own books

The F&B preset (`ledger-and-money.mdx` §2.3) is a restaurant's chart. Ours is a software company's, and the tenant may rename and extend it - so this is the shape we expect to start from:

| Code | Account | Type | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `1010` | Bank - settlement | current_assets | where subscription money lands before the gateway settles |
| `1100` | Accounts receivable | current_assets | the system type - a customer who has been invoiced and not paid |
| `2100` | SST payable | current_liabilities | the tax we collected and must remit (§4.3) |
| `2400` | Deferred revenue - subscriptions | current_liabilities | **the money for a service not yet delivered** |
| `4000` | Subscription revenue | income | released as the service is delivered |
| `4010` | Subscription revenue - promotions | income | only if we ever need the promoted volume separated |
| `6100` | Gateway fees | expenses | the gateway's cut (§4.4) |
| `6200` | Hosting and infrastructure | expenses | what the accounting service costs us |
| `6300` | Salaries and contractors | expenses | the company's own costs |

**The deferred revenue account is the one that matters.** It is the account that proves the accrual rule works, and it is the account an auditor looks at first.

## 5. The connector's failure modes

**A connector that silently loses revenue is worse than no connector**, because our books would then be confidently wrong - and ours are the books we would trust.

| Failure | What it looks like | What the connector does |
|---|---|---|
| **The feed is behind** | invoices exist, the books do not show them | the cursor is per invoice id; a gap is detected by the id sequence, not by a timestamp |
| **The period is unknown** | the invoice's period cannot be stated | **dead letter with a named reason.** No journal. A missing number beats a wrong number |
| **The plan has no product** | the invoice cannot be attributed | dead letter with a named reason |
| **The amount is in a currency we do not hold** | a third currency appears | dead letter. The base currency is MYR; a third currency is a new decision, not a conversion |
| **A status flips after the fact** | paid becomes refunded | **a new event** (§3.5), so a reversing journal |
| **The settlement does not reconcile** | gross minus fees is not the money that arrived | the monthly reconciliation names the difference, and the difference is an alert, not a rounding note |

**The reconciliation is the daily check, and it is the acceptance test (§7).**

## 6. The separation of our books from everyone else's

### 6.1 The rules

**The internal tenant is a tenant like any other, with three differences, and each difference is a restriction rather than a privilege.**

1. **It is marked internal, and the mark is used only to restrict.** A flag on the accounting tenant - `is_internal` - that **removes** it from customer-facing surfaces. **It never grants access to anything.** This is the important direction: a flag that only ever subtracts.
2. **It is excluded from customer-facing surfaces.** Specifically: the tenant switcher in the accountant workspace, the tenant list in any customer-visible screen, the demo and sample data, the screenshot sets, the marketing site, and the support tooling's default views. **A customer must never be able to browse to our books.**
3. **Its access is a named allowlist, and every access is audited.** Our revenue and our margins are ours. The founder allowlist is the equivalent of `ROLE_PERMISSIONS` on the POS side (`backend/app/middleware/auth.py:45`), and the audit row records who read what, when - the same `audit_log` the ledger already writes (`program-structure.mdx` §3).

### 6.2 What the flag must never do

**It must not change the isolation path.** The internal tenant resolves its tenant from a signed credential, exactly like every other tenant, and **no route reads a tenant id from a request body** (`program-structure.mdx` §6.3). If our tenant could be reached by naming it in a URL, then our books would be one authorisation bug away from a customer's screen - and our books contain our revenue, our margins and our cost base.

**The internal tenant is in the same Postgres as every customer, in the same tables, under the same rules.** It is not in a separate schema and it does not have a separate code path, because **a separate code path is a separate set of bugs** - and the bugs would be in the path that touches the most sensitive data we have.

### 6.3 What lands in the backup and the drill

**The internal tenant's books are in scope of the Postgres backup and the monthly restore drill, like every other tenant's** (`operations.mdx` §1) - and the drill's row-count comparison must include the internal tenant's journals, because **a restore that loses our revenue silently is the one failure we would not notice until tax time.**

---

## 7. The reconciliation - the acceptance test

**Two independent computations of the same number. If they disagree, one of them is wrong, and the drill finds out which.**

### 7.1 Cash in

**Our books' cash receipts, per currency, per period, must equal the billing system's paid invoices, per currency, per period.**

The billing system's own figure is recomputable from the invoices: the same `group_by_currency(invoices, statuses={"paid"})` the admin dashboard uses (`routes/admin.py:243-245`, `services/money.py:25`). **The check is per currency and per period, and it is exact to the minor unit** - because both sides are the same invoice set, so any difference is a connector bug, not a rounding question.

**One asymmetry to expect and to name:** the dashboard's `monthly_revenue` is **all-time** paid invoices grouped by currency - the field name says monthly, the query does not filter (`routes/admin.py:243`). **So the dashboard is not the period figure the drill compares against.** The drill compares against a **period-scoped recomputation** of the same helper, and the dashboard's aggregate is a sanity check on the whole. **This is worth knowing before someone reconciles against the wrong number.**

### 7.2 Revenue recognised

**Recognised revenue is not the invoice count, and the difference is the deferred revenue balance.** The check that matters:

> **Deferred revenue at the end of a period, recomputed independently from the invoice set and the periods they cover, must equal the `2400 Deferred revenue - subscriptions` balance in the books.**

That is the real test of the recognition rules: an annual prepayment sitting in the liability account for exactly as long as it should, and not one month longer.

### 7.3 The reconciliation screen

**The difference is named, not rounded away.** `ledger-and-money.mdx` and `connectors-and-switching.mdx` both say the same thing about the POS reconciliation: *the difference is the alert, and the threshold is set so a rounding difference does not page anyone at 2am.* The internal tenant's reconciliation follows the same rule, and it is the same screen - a tenant's books, reconciled against their source.

### 7.4 What it proves

- **The connector works** - our revenue lands in a book without a human typing.
- **The ledger works** - the trial balance balances with real volume, real promotions, real refunds.
- **The accrual engine works** - before we sell it to anyone, our own books have run a full year of annual prepayments through it.
- **The restore works** - the drill brings back our books and they reconcile again.

**A tenant's first month is not the place to find out the deferred revenue release is wrong. Our own books are.**

## What this document does NOT claim

- **It does not claim our books are statutory records.** We produce a proper double-entry ledger. What is filed is our accountant's and the law's business.
- **It does not claim the tax answer is settled.** §4.3 names two ways to do it and recommends one; **the decision is Kens's**, and the rate is a fact about our service category that must be confirmed, not assumed.
- **It does not claim the fee is captured.** It is not, anywhere, in the live code. §4.4 is the honest interim answer: gross books, monthly adjustment from the settlement.
- **It does not claim the customer summary is a good model.** `routes/billing.py:553-563` sums across currencies and labels the result MYR. **We do not copy that.** Our books follow the dashboard's per-currency rule.
- **It does not claim we invoice ourselves.** §2.3: the internal tenant is granted, never billed, and our own use creates no revenue.
- **It does not claim the internal tenant is a normal tenant.** It is a normal tenant with three **restrictions** on top, and none of them widens access.
- **It does not claim the period problem is solved by reading carefully.** §3.3: it is a schema change on `invoices`, and without it the connector refuses to post rather than guessing.
- **It does not claim dogfooding is a substitute for a customer.** It is a faster, cheaper, more honest way to find our own bugs. It is not evidence that a cold-sale accountant will buy.

## The open questions

1. **Which legal entity owns the internal tenant?** The entity that sells the subscriptions, because that is where the revenue lands - and the entity that files the SST return. A group with two entities has two books and this document does not merge them.
2. **Do we put SST into the plan price (recommended) or accrue it in the books (interim)?** §4.3. The first changes what the customer sees; the second leaves our books and our SST return computed from a tax the customer never paid.
3. **What is the applicable SST rate for our service category, and when did it take effect?** It is a business fact, it is not hard-coded anywhere, and it changes the accrual for the year it changes.
4. **Do we add `period_start` / `period_end` to `invoices`, or does the connector recompute the period?** §3.3 recommends the columns. The connector's fallback works for ordinary renewals and is wrong for prorations and promotions - and the cost of being wrong is a year of misallocated revenue.
5. **Is the gateway fee a monthly manual journal, or do we build the settlement connector now?** §4.4. The manual journal is honest and small; the connector is correct and is work.
6. **Do we run our own restaurant on the POS?** If yes, it becomes the Phase 2 connector's reference tenant for free, and the Phase 2 connector gets tested against a real tenancy before a customer sees it.
7. **What is the base currency for the internal tenant's books?** This document says MYR, following the Malaysian entity. A group reporting in USD changes the translation question, and the rate table with it.
8. **Should the internal tenant's books be visible to the accountant workspace at all?** §6.1 excludes it. If the answer is ever yes, it is an access decision with an audit trail - not a flag flip.
