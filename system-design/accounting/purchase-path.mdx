## What this document is

Phase 0b in the phase plan is one line: **the account and the purchase.** It is the line that was missing, and it is missing because the plan was written as if the accounting service were a module inside a POS that already had customers. It is not. It is a service that has to acquire its own.

The live-code audit behind this document found that **the add-on has no purchase path at all.** `FeaturePrice` is an admin-managed catalogue, not a customer-facing purchase mechanism: the only routes that touch it are admin routes, the only `checkout.Session.create` calls price a plan or an invoice, and every `Invoice` row is created inside the subscription and webhook flows. The two AI add-ons are not bought by customers either - an admin grants them.

**So the purchase path is new code, and it is Phase 0 work.** Phase 2's own proof - "a POS tenant buys the add-on and sees their own month's books appear" - cannot happen until it exists.

This document decides six things, and each one is a decision the live code forces:

| The decision | Why it cannot be deferred |
|---|---|
| **1. The persistence model for a feature-only purchase** | `Subscription.plan_id` is `nullable=False` and a POS tenant who buys the add-on is **already blocked from a second subscription row** by a live guard |
| **2. The signup path** | every website customer is auto-provisioned a POS tenant at signup, so **there is no way to be a customer without one** |
| **3. The checkout** | the existing `create-checkout` hard-codes `usd` and names the product "IDEAZES POS" |
| **4. The grant** | the Stripe webhook creates a subscription against **plan 1** when the metadata has no `plan_id` |
| **5. The entitlement read** | the route that answers "what is this customer entitled to" reads **one** subscription and merges only plan features and overrides |
| **6. The lifecycle** | renewal, cancellation and dunning all iterate `subscriptions`; a purchase outside that table is never renewed and never dunned |

## The purchase path end to end

**Shape 1 - the add-on.** A POS tenant buys the accounting add-on on top of the plan they already have.

```
POST /api/features/{feature_id}/checkout     (new)
  -> resolve the price from feature_prices for the RESOLVED currency
  -> Stripe session (card) or HitPay request (DuitNow / Touch 'n Go)
  -> checkout.session.completed / hitpay webhook
       -> invoices row          (subscription_id NULL, feature_subscription_id set)
       -> feature_subscriptions row (active)
       -> the entitlement union now says: entitled
  -> the accounting service, on its next entitlement read, creates the accounting tenant
```

**Shape 2 - the standalone plan.** A customer with no POS subscribes to an accounting plan.

```
POST /api/subscriptions/                     (existing route, product-aware branch)
  -> plan.product == 'accounting'
  -> NO POS tenant is provisioned
  -> Stripe session / HitPay request
  -> checkout.session.completed
       -> subscriptions row (plan_id -> the accounting plan)
       -> invoices row
  -> the accounting service, on its next entitlement read, creates the accounting tenant
```

**The one thing both shapes share:** the customer is a platform customer in the website API, and the accounting tenant is created by the accounting service - never by the website API writing into it.

## Decision 1 - the persistence model for a feature-only purchase

**The live facts that close the options.**

- `Subscription.plan_id` is `Column(Integer, ForeignKey("subscription_plans.id"), nullable=False)` (`website_api/app/models/models.py:101`). A customer who bought only the accounting add-on has **no plan**.
- `Subscription.customer_id` is `nullable=False` and points at `website_customers` (`models.py:99`). The table is keyed by *a subscription to a plan*, and it carries plan semantics throughout: `billing_cycle`, `next_billing_cycle`, `next_plan_id`, `max_outlets`/`max_staff` through the plan, the promotion snapshot (`promo_price`, `promo_currency`).
- **A POS tenant cannot get a second `Subscription` row today.** The subscribe route refuses it: `existing = db.query(Subscription).filter(Subscription.customer_id == current_user.id, Subscription.status.in_(["active", "trial"])).first()` -> `raise HTTPException(400, "Already has active subscription")` (`website_api/app/routes/subscription.py:226-233`). That guard is load-bearing - it is what stops a customer being charged twice.
- There is no feature-subscription table. `CustomerFeatureOverride` (`models.py:299`) is the closest thing and it is **not** it: `enabled`, `reason`, `created_by` - a manual admin switch with no money, no price, no period and no invoice behind it.
- `Invoice.subscription_id` is already nullable (`models.py:168`), so an invoice that is not about a plan is representable.

**The options.**

| Option | Why it loses |
|---|---|
| **Make `Subscription.plan_id` nullable** and add a `feature_id` | the whole renewal, proration, upgrade and cancel machinery then has to carry two shapes, and `plan_id` is read by the entitlement route, the checkout webhooks, the renewal cron and the upgrade path |
| **Sell the add-on as a plan in the POS catalogue** (a plan whose `plan_features` includes `accounting`) | a POS tenant on Plan A who buys the add-on cannot be *moved* to that plan - it would replace their plan - and a second `Subscription` row is blocked by the guard above. It would also price the add-on from `plan_prices` rather than `feature_prices`, which is the wrong catalogue |
| **A new `feature_subscriptions` table beside `subscriptions`** | **chosen** |

**The decision: a new table.** The add-on is a *feature on top of a subscription*, not a subscription. It has its own price catalogue (`feature_prices`, per currency, with its own `is_active`), its own period, its own cancellation, and it does not have outlets, staff limits, a promotion or an upgrade path. Giving it a table of its own means **the plan machinery is not touched at all** - which is the property that makes Phase 0b safe to build before Phase 1 has any ledger in it.

**The proposed table.**

```
feature_subscriptions
  id                      primary key
  customer_id             FK website_customers.id, NOT NULL
  feature_id              FK features.id,          NOT NULL   -- the 'accounting' feature row
  feature_price_id        FK feature_prices.id,     NOT NULL   -- the price row actually sold
  status                  NOT NULL   -- active | cancelled | expired | past_due
  billing_cycle           NOT NULL   -- monthly | annual
  currency                NOT NULL   -- the currency the customer was charged in
  price_amount            NOT NULL   -- a SNAPSHOT of what was sold, in minor units
  current_period_start    NOT NULL
  current_period_end      NOT NULL
  cancelled_at            nullable
  stripe_subscription_id  nullable
  stripe_session_id       nullable, UNIQUE  -- the idempotency key
  created_at / updated_at
```

**Three rules on that table.**

1. **`price_amount` is a snapshot, and it is in minor units.** `feature_prices.price_monthly` is a `Float` (`models.py:270`) and an admin can edit that row at any time. The sale must not move when the catalogue does - so the amount that was charged is copied onto the purchase, in the integer minor units the money rules use (`ledger-and-money.mdx` §2).
2. **`feature_price_id` is the row, `price_amount` is the fact.** The row says which catalogue entry was bought; the snapshot says what it cost. If the catalogue later changes price, the invoice for that month still says what the customer agreed to pay.
3. **One active purchase per customer per feature.** A second active row for the same `(customer_id, feature_id)` is a double sale and is refused by the checkout, not by the webhook - the checkout is where the customer can still be told.

**And the invoice links to it.** `invoices` gains one nullable column:

```
invoices.feature_subscription_id   FK feature_subscriptions.id, nullable
```

A feature invoice has `subscription_id = NULL` and `feature_subscription_id` set. **This is a change to a table every billing path touches**, so it is Phase 0 schema work - the same migration that adds `subscription_plans.product` and `features.product`.

**The one thing this decision does NOT do:** it does not add a plan row for the add-on, and it does not change what `Subscription` means. A POS tenant who buys the add-on still has **one** `subscriptions` row (their POS plan) and **one** `feature_subscriptions` row (the add-on).

## Decision 2 - the signup path

**The live facts.**

- `UserCreate.restaurant_name: str` is required (`website_api/app/routes/auth.py:31`) and `WebsiteCustomer.restaurant_name` is `nullable=False` (`models.py:77`).
- `provision_pos_account` is called at **three** sites: the signup (`auth.py:143-161`), the subscription creation (`subscription.py:577`) and the webhook (`webhooks.py:683`). Its own docstring is "Create (or repair) the POS account of a website customer" (`website_api/app/services/pos_provisioning.py:40`) - it POSTs to the POS's `/register-customer` and `/register`.
- `WebsiteCustomer.customer_id` - the link to the POS tenant - is nullable (`models.py:92`). **That null is the signal that a customer has no POS tenant**, and it is the field the accounting-only path leaves empty.
- `restaurant_name` is read at runtime in about 25 places in `website_api/app` and `backend/app`: the email templates (`app/emails/email_service.py`, `app/services/subscription_email.py:128`), the invoice and receipt rendering (`app/invoices/routes.py:79`, `app/routes/billing.py:159`), the admin customer list (`app/routes/admin.py:589`, `:681`, `:776`) and the HitPay/Stripe customer name (`subscription.py:150`, `:728`; `webhooks.py:686`).

**The decision: an accounting-only signup, and the name problem solved without a table rebuild.**

The signup takes an **account type**, and the account type decides whether a POS tenant is created:

| Account type | What it asks | What it provisions |
|---|---|---|
| `pos` (today) | restaurant name, phone, country | a POS tenant + a staff admin, exactly as now |
| `accounting` (new) | **legal entity name**, country | **no POS tenant at all** - `WebsiteCustomer.customer_id` stays `NULL` |

**The name, decided deliberately.** Making `restaurant_name` nullable means a SQLite table rebuild plus touching every one of those runtime reads - and the failure mode is an email that says *"Hi None!"*, because `email_service.py` interpolates the name straight into the template. The alternative is to **add a column and keep the display name working**:

```
website_customers.legal_entity_name   String(200), nullable   -- the accounting-only onboarding identity
website_customers.account_type        String(20),  NOT NULL, default 'pos'
```

The accounting-only signup writes the legal entity name into **both** `legal_entity_name` and `restaurant_name`. The duplication is deliberate and temporary: `restaurant_name` remains the **display name** on every email, invoice and receipt - which is what those 25 reads want - while `legal_entity_name` is the **onboarding identity** the accounting service reads for the legal entity beneath the platform customer. A later migration can make `restaurant_name` nullable and introduce one `display_name` helper; **that is not Phase 0b's business, and the accounting service reads `legal_entity_name`, not `restaurant_name`.**

**The product-aware provisioning.** `provision_pos_account` stays exactly as it is - it is good code and the POS needs it. What changes is **when it is called**:

- the signup calls it **only** when `account_type == 'pos'`;
- the subscription route calls it **only** when the plan's `product == 'pos'`;
- the webhook calls it **only** when the plan's `product == 'pos'`.

**The accounting tenant is not provisioned by the website API.** The website API would have to call across to a second service to create rows in the accounting service's own database - a new cross-service write path with its own failure modes, and it would put the books' lifecycle behind the billing system's availability. Instead:

**the accounting service creates its own tenant, lazily, from the entitlement it already reads.** When the accounting service sees a platform customer it has no accounting tenant for, and that customer is entitled to accounting, it creates the accounting tenant and its books, keyed by the platform customer id - idempotent by construction, because the key is the platform customer.

That is the whole provisioning contract, and it has one moving part: the entitlement read.

## Decision 3 - the checkout

**The live facts.**

- `POST /api/subscriptions/create-checkout` hard-codes `'currency': 'usd'` and names the product `f"{plan.name} - IDEAZES POS"`, and prices from the plan's own float column with a **truncating** `int(plan.price_monthly * 100)` (`website_api/app/routes/subscription.py:839`, `:841`, `:847`).
- The other checkout - the one in the subscribe route - resolves the currency properly: `resolved_currency` from `resolve_price_safe` (`subscription.py:471`, `website_api/app/services/plan_pricing.py:115`) and `unit_amount = int(round(resolved_amount * 100))` (`subscription.py:479`). **It rounds; `create-checkout` truncates** - `int(8.99 * 100)` is `898`, not `899`.
- `resolve_price` **raises** `NoPriceForCurrency` when a plan has no price row in the currency, and `resolve_price_safe` returns `(None, None)` instead of failing the flow (`plan_pricing.py:115-123`). There is no fallback into another currency, on purpose.
- The currency is not a free choice: `CURRENCY_BY_METHOD` maps DuitNow and Touch 'n Go to **MYR, always** (`plan_pricing.py:34-37`), because the wallets are Malaysian.
- The country decides which method is allowed, and the server decides, not the browser: `is_method_allowed(country, method)` with `COUNTRY_METHODS` for MY and KH and `DEFAULT_METHODS = [STRIPE, PAY_LATER]` for anything else (`website_api/app/services/payment_methods.py:57-71`). A Cambodian customer sending `hitpay_duitnow` is refused, not silently downgraded.
- `create-checkout` is **card only** (`payment_method_types=['card']`, `subscription.py:838`). The HitPay path is a different shape entirely: an invoice plus a payment request (`_hitpay_payment_for_invoice`, `subscription.py:126-170`), and the return URL rides along so the customer comes back to the portal.

**The decision: one new checkout route for the add-on, and a product-aware branch on the existing subscribe route for the standalone plan.**

```
POST /api/features/{feature_id}/checkout
```

**It must not reuse `create-checkout`.** That route is wrong for the add-on in three ways at once - it prices a plan, it charges dollars, and it says "IDEAZES POS" - and it is also wrong for the standalone plan, because a Malaysian accounting plan sold through it is charged in dollars.

**What the new route does, in order:**

1. **Resolve the price from `feature_prices`, in the currency the customer will actually be charged in.** The same rule as plans: the customer may choose USD or MYR where a price row exists; DuitNow and Touch 'n Go are **MYR, always**. If the feature has **no** price row in that currency, the route **fails loudly** - it does not fall back into another currency, exactly like `NoPriceForCurrency`. **No price, no sale.**
2. **Charge in integer minor units with rounding**: `int(round(amount * 100))` - the rounding form at `subscription.py:479`, not the truncating form at `:847`.
3. **Offer the methods the customer's country allows**, decided server-side by `is_method_allowed`. Stripe card, plus DuitNow and Touch 'n Go for Malaysia.
4. **Write the metadata the grant needs**: `customer_id`, `feature_id`, `feature_price_id`, `billing_cycle`, `currency`, `amount_minor`, and `kind: 'feature_purchase'`. The amount is in the metadata so the webhook **compares rather than trusts**: if the gateway's amount and currency disagree with what was resolved, the grant refuses and the difference is an alert, not a silent overwrite.
5. **Be idempotent on the customer's side.** One open purchase attempt per customer per feature: a second attempt while one is open returns the existing session instead of creating a second one - the rule the subscribe route already follows.

**The standalone plan branch** is a smaller change: in the subscribe route, a plan whose `product == 'accounting'` skips the POS provisioning call and skips the "IDEAZES POS" product name, and the currency resolution it already does at `:471` is reused unchanged. **The currency-resolving checkout is the good one and it is already written.**

**One more decision: the add-on's currency is MYR for a Malaysian tenant and the catalogue says so.** `feature_prices` is already per currency with a uniqueness constraint on `(feature_id, currency_code)` (`models.py:290-292`) - the shape this needs. The two AI rows show the pattern: USD 20/200 and MYR 80/800 for `ai_agent`. **The accounting add-on gets the same shape**, and the price is a proposal, not a value in this document.

## Decision 4 - the grant

**And one grant that is not a purchase: our own.**

IDEAZES is a tenant of its own accounting product. **The internal tenant's entitlement is granted, not bought**, and the mechanism is the one the live code already has - a `CustomerFeatureOverride` row (`website_api/app/models/models.py:299`), written by `POST /api/admin/customer-features` (`website_api/app/routes/admin.py:969`), which carries the feature, an `enabled` flag, a `reason` and who wrote it.

**That row is already one of the three sources in the entitlement union of Decision 5**, so the internal tenant needs **no new grant mechanism and no new code path** - one seeded row for our platform customer id, against the feature whose `product == 'accounting'`, with a reason that says what it is. **And it is auditable by the same admin screen that audits a customer's grant.**

**We do not create a subscription for ourselves, so no invoice, no payment, no revenue, and no intercompany balance.** The full reasoning, and what would happen if we invoiced ourselves, is in `internal-tenant.mdx` §2.


**The live facts.**

- `checkout.session.completed` is handled by `handle_checkout_completed` (`website_api/app/routes/webhooks.py:353`). The session's **mode** decides what the event means: `payment` settles an existing invoice, `subscription` with an `invoice_id` in the metadata settles an existing invoice and creates the recurring subscription, and anything else is a **new subscription**.
- **The landmine.** In both places where the webhook builds a subscription, the plan id is `plan_id=int(plan_id) if plan_id else 1` (`webhooks.py:480` and `:542`). A checkout session whose metadata carries no `plan_id` does not fail - **it silently attaches the customer to plan 1.** For a feature purchase there is no `plan_id` at all, so the feature branch must be recognised **before** that line is ever reached, and the `or 1` fallback must not be reachable from it.
- The invoice is deduplicated by `find_invoice` (`webhooks.py:163`), which matches on `stripe_invoice_id` **or** `stripe_checkout_session_id` and deliberately refuses to build a comparison on a missing value - because `== None` in SQL matches every row with no value. There is also `_is_the_same_payment` (`webhooks.py:123`), a windowed same-amount check for a payment that may already be recorded.
- HitPay has its own idempotency: the `hitpay_webhook_events` table with a unique event id, so the second delivery of the same event is ignored and answered 200 (`website_api/app/models/models.py`, after `Invoice`).
- `handle_invoice_paid` marks the invoice paid (`webhooks.py:921`) and explicitly **does not** extend the period - period extension happens at renewal, not on payment.
- `handle_payment_failed` marks the invoice `failed` and **does not revoke access** (`webhooks.py:1004-1030`).

**The decision: one branch, keyed on the metadata, ahead of the plan branch.**

```
handle_checkout_completed:
    if metadata.kind == 'feature_purchase':   -> handle_feature_purchase(...)
    elif mode == 'payment' or (mode == 'subscription' and metadata.invoice_id):
                                                -> _settle_existing_invoice(...)   # unchanged
    else:                                      -> the plan branch                 # unchanged
```

`handle_feature_purchase` does five things, in this order:

1. **Resolve the customer from the metadata id**, and re-derive the purchase row from `feature_price_id` - never from a caller-supplied amount.
2. **Compare.** The gateway's amount and currency must equal the resolved ones. A mismatch does **not** create a purchase and does **not** grant: it is logged and alerted, because a mismatch is either a bug or an attack, and neither should become a granted entitlement.
3. **Create the `feature_subscriptions` row** (`status='active'`, the period computed from the billing cycle - a month or a year, from the same `relativedelta` arithmetic the plan path uses at `webhooks.py:399-402`).
4. **Create the `invoices` row** with `subscription_id = NULL`, `feature_subscription_id` set, the invoice number in the house format `INV-{YYYYMMDD}-{token_hex(4).upper()}`, `currency` set, `status='paid'` and `paid_at` set.
5. **Notify.** The accounting service learns about it the same way the POS learns about a plan change: `notify_pos_plan_changed` is a fire-and-forget POST to `/internal/plan-changed` with the internal key and a 3-second timeout, and **it never breaks the billing flow** (`website_api/app/services/plan_notify.py:25-59`). The accounting service gets the same shape.

**The idempotency has three layers, and all three are needed.**

- **The session id** is `UNIQUE` on `feature_subscriptions`, so a second `checkout.session.completed` for the same session finds the row and stops.
- **The invoice** is found by `find_invoice` on `stripe_invoice_id` or `stripe_checkout_session_id` before anything is written - the house pattern, including its refusal to compare on a missing value.
- **The HitPay event id** is unique in `hitpay_webhook_events`, so the second delivery of the same event is ignored.

**The access rule on a failed payment is the existing one, and it is worth stating plainly:** `handle_payment_failed` marks the invoice failed and **does not revoke access**. The accounting tenant keeps its books through a failed payment, inside the grace period the access table already defines. That is correct - a tenant's books do not disappear because a card expired - and it is also why the dunning path in Decision 6 exists.

## Decision 5 - the entitlement read

**This is the read that does NOT need to change, and the read that does.**

**The one that does not.** The accounting service asks the website API what a customer is entitled to exactly as the POS does - by the platform customer's id, over the internal key, against the admin route that merges the plan's features with the customer's overrides (`website_api/app/routes/admin.py:1216`). The POS does it at `backend/app/services/plan_features.py:71` over `X-API-Key`. **That route is keyed by the platform customer, not by a POS tenant, so it already works for a customer who has no POS.** The tenant of the books is still derived from the books, never from the caller.

**The one that does.** `GET /customer/{customer_id}/features` computes its answer from **one** subscription and **two** sources:

```python
sub = db.query(Subscription).filter(Subscription.customer_id == web_id).first()   # admin.py:1244
...
enabled = f.id in plan_features if not f.id in override_map else override_map[f.id]  # admin.py:1268
```

Two problems, both created by this design:

- **One subscription.** `.first()` on an unordered query. A customer with a POS plan **and** an accounting plan has two `subscriptions` rows and the read picks one of them arbitrarily. The order is not even stable.
- **Two sources.** The add-on is a **third** source - an active `feature_subscriptions` row - and it is in neither.

**The decision: the route becomes product-scoped, and its answer becomes a union of three sources.**

```
GET /customer/{customer_id}/features?product=pos
GET /customer/{customer_id}/features?product=accounting
```

- **`product=pos`** - the existing behaviour, unchanged: the features of the customer's POS plan, plus the overrides. The POS calls this and sees exactly what it sees today.
- **`product=accounting`** - the accounting entitlement, and the answer is **entitled** when any of these is true:
  - the customer has a `subscriptions` row on a plan whose `product == 'accounting'`, in a live status; or
  - the customer has an **active** `feature_subscriptions` row for a feature whose `product == 'accounting'`; or
  - an **enabled** `CustomerFeatureOverride` grants it.

**This needs two more discriminators, and they are the same idea as `SubscriptionPlan.product`:**

```
subscription_plans.product   String(20), NOT NULL, default 'pos'   -- 'pos' | 'accounting'
features.product             String(20), NOT NULL, default 'pos'   -- 'pos' | 'accounting'
```

`subscription_plans.product` is already part of the plan - it is what lets the two catalogues live under one table without mixing, and what lets the accounting plan publish and unpublish on its own through the `is_published` column that already exists (`models.py:25`). `features.product` is new and smaller: it says which product a feature belongs to, so the accounting feature row is not POS UI. The live `features` table has 46 rows and **none of them is `accounting`** - it is created by this migration.

**The `.first()` goes.** The product-scoped read filters by the product rather than taking the first row, so a customer with both products gets a correct answer for each instead of one arbitrary answer for both.

**And the second read has the same shape.** `GET /api/features/enabled` (`website_api/app/routes/features.py:48`) does the same union for the logged-in customer - one subscription (`:60`), plan features, then overrides (`:84`). It is the customer-facing twin of the admin route. **Both need the product scoping, or the accounting service and the customer portal will disagree about what the customer is entitled to.**

**The key stays the way it is.** The server key reads any tenant by design, because the caller resolves the tenant from its own signed session; a tenant key is bound to one tenant and may read only that one; **a key with no tenant binding may not read a tenant at all** (`website_api/app/core/api_key_scope.py:39-121`). The live `api_keys` table has two rows and **both are unbound** - the frontend key and the backend server key - and the server key's value matches `POS_INTERNAL_KEY` in the service's own settings, which is what makes it a server key at all. **The accounting service gets its own server key with the same properties** - it is a service, it resolves the tenant from its own records, and it never receives a tenant id from a browser.

**The notification direction.** `notify_pos_plan_changed` tells the POS that a plan changed. The accounting service needs the same: a fire-and-forget POST with the internal key and a 3-second timeout that **never breaks the billing flow** (`plan_notify.py:25-59`). **The accounting service does not depend on that notification to be correct** - it reads entitlement on every request and on its nightly sweep - so a dropped notification costs a few seconds of delay, not an outage. That asymmetry is deliberate: the POS's notification can also drop, and the POS keeps a cached feature list for exactly this reason (`backend/app/core/plan_cache.py`).

## Decision 6 - the lifecycle

**Renewal.** The renewal cron generates the next invoice for each live subscription (`generate_renewal_invoices`, called from the nightly job list at `website_api/app/routes/notifications.py:205`). **It iterates `subscriptions`.** A `feature_subscriptions` row outside that iteration is never renewed - the customer keeps access until someone notices. So the renewal job gets a **second branch**: for each live `feature_subscriptions` row whose `current_period_end` has passed, generate the next invoice from the **snapshot** price, advance the period, and - for the HitPay shape - create the next payment request.

**The snapshot is what makes this work.** If renewal re-read `feature_prices`, an admin's price edit would silently re-price every running add-on mid-term, and would do it again every month. The snapshot is the sale.

**Cancellation.** "Access continues until period end" - the house rule, and it is the right one: `POST /api/subscriptions/cancel` sets the status and the end date rather than cutting access at midnight (`subscription.py:1692`). The add-on gets the same route shape: `status='cancelled'`, `cancelled_at` set, the period end unchanged. **And a resume does not invent a new period** - the period end stays where it is and the outstanding invoice keeps its due date (`subscription.py:1752`).

**Dunning.** `effective_access` is the single place that decides what access a subscription has, and it reads the admin's `payment_due_days` as a 14-day grace and `overdue_warning_days` as a 7-day warning (`website_api/app/services/subscription_access.py:94-116`). A `pending` subscription is blocked until the payment lands - "the access opens when the gateway confirms the payment (the webhook)" (`subscription_access.py:121-133`).

**The add-on needs the same decision function, over the same settings.** `effective_access` takes a `Subscription` row and its invoices. A feature purchase is not a `Subscription`, so either the function is generalised to take "a purchase and its invoices" - which is the honest shape - or a second one is written next to it. **Generalising is better**: the rules are identical, the two paths differ only in which table the purchase lives in. A purchase that goes `past_due` and then `expired` loses the entitlement, and the accounting tenant is **not** deleted - it goes to the same read-only state a cancelled POS tenant is in.

**Downgrade.** There is no downgrade between add-ons - there is one add-on. The downgrade that exists is **the standalone plan's plan-to-plan change**, and that machinery (`next_billing_cycle`, `next_plan_id`, the proration at `subscription.py:1495`) is plan-shaped and already written. It works for an accounting plan unchanged.

**Export.** A customer who leaves takes their books. **The export is a first-class deliverable, not a support task** - and it is the answer to the question a churned accounting customer always asks, which is the question that keeps them in. It is Phase 1 work (it needs the ledger to exist), but **the entitlement path must not block it**: a cancelled or expired customer keeps read access to their own books and their own export for a defined window. The POS's own access table already has this shape - a cancelled subscription keeps a grace, and the access object reports the end date.

**Delete.** A deletion request has to answer two different questions: the **billing** record (invoices, the purchase, the money) and the **books** (the accounting tenant). They are not the same record and they are not in the same database.

- The billing record is kept - it is the money history, and the invoice number is part of the tenant's own paperwork.
- The books are the tenant's legal records. **Deleting them is not a self-service action and not a Phase 0 deliverable.** The accounting service's deletion path is a defined, audited, tenant-requested erasure with a retention rule attached, and it is Phase 6 work - the phase that is about bringing books in from elsewhere is the phase that knows what an accounting record must keep.

**The house rule holds throughout: the database is the truth and the seed is only what a fresh install starts with.** `website_api/app/core/ai_addon_prices.py` says so in as many words, and `scripts/migrate_feature_prices.py --check` reports the drift. **The same pattern applies here** - a seed for the accounting feature prices, and a `--check` mode on the migration that reports when the live database disagrees with it.

**One risk worth naming:** `website_api` has **no Alembic** - `init_db` calls `Base.metadata.create_all` (`website_api/app/core/database.py:47`), which creates missing tables but **never alters an existing one**. So every column above is a hand-written migration script in the house style, and `Base.metadata.create_all` alone is not the migration. **The `feature_subscriptions` table is the only one `create_all` would build by itself; the other six changes need scripts.**

## What this document does NOT claim

- **It does not claim the add-on sells.** The price is a proposal; the plan at `business-model.mdx` §2.3 has it at MYR 149/month, and that number is a number in a design document.
- **It does not claim the accounting service is built.** Nothing here exists. Every route, column and table named above is a proposal.

- **It does not claim the payment gateway is settled.** Stripe card and HitPay DuitNow/Touch 'n Go are the two shapes in the live code; what a feature checkout does inside HitPay's request lifecycle is a build detail.
- **It does not claim the accounting tenant is created the moment the payment lands.** It is created when the accounting service next reads the entitlement. The delay is seconds, and the notification makes it immediate in the common case.

## The open questions

1. **The add-on's renewal cycle.** Monthly and annual are the catalogue's two cycles, and the renewal branch needs one. **Does the add-on renew automatically, or does it require the customer to buy the next period?** Automatic is what a subscription means; it also means the dunning path is load-bearing from day one.
2. **Does an accounting-only customer get a `stripe_customer_id`?** The subscribe route creates or reuses one. An accounting-only customer who never touches the POS still needs one for the accounting plan's own billing - so yes, and it is created in the accounting signup.
3. **Is the add-on ever sold alone, without a POS plan?** Today the design says the add-on is bought by a POS tenant. **But the persistence model in Decision 1 does not require one** - `feature_subscriptions` has no plan behind it, so an accounting-only customer could buy the add-on too. That is a **commercial** question, not a technical one, and the answer changes the price, not the schema.
4. **What happens to a POS tenant who buys the standalone accounting plan while already having the add-on?** They would have both a `feature_subscriptions` row and an accounting-plan subscription. The entitlement union says entitled either way; **the money is charged twice.** The rule needs writing before the checkout ships.
