## 1. The short answer

Kens asked three questions. Here they are, with what was verified in the
HitPay sandbox **today** (November 2, 2026).

**1. Does HitPay support recurring billing for Touch 'n Go?** ✅ The
**technology** exists — TnG is one of the few wallets with tokenisation +
scheduled auto-charge, and `POST /v1/recurring-billing` with
`save_payment_method=true` returns a direct link (verified: **201**).

**But we are not using it.** Touch 'n Go's recurring flow belongs to a
**HitPay merchant account** (our own account, onboarded and verified as a
business). Until that account exists, **Touch 'n Go behaves exactly like
DuitNow: a one-off payment**. The customer pays; the invoice is paid; the
period continues. There is no saved wallet and no off-session charge.

*(Version 1 of this document designed the TnG auto-renewal. Kens' decision
on November 2, 2026: **both** Malaysian methods are one-off payments, so
the two behave the same and there is only one flow to build.)*

**2. Does HitPay allow our SaaS POS to use it for the Malaysian
customers?** ✅ **Yes — but there are two different models, and what was
described is the simpler one.**

| | **Model A — Ideazes is the merchant** | **Model B — the platform model** |
|---|---|---|
| Who pays | the **restaurant** pays **us** the subscription | the **diner** pays the **restaurant** through our POS |
| Who owns the HitPay account | **Ideazes** (one account, verified once) | **every restaurant** (a sub-merchant each, verified by HitPay) |
| What we build | a payment method in the customer portal | a sub-merchant onboarding flow + OAuth or key collection + a Platform Key |
| The money | comes to us | goes straight to each restaurant's own bank account |
| Effort | small — a provider, a webhook, a country rule | large — a business-registration funnel for every tenant |
| **What Kens described** | ✅ **this one** | a later, separate decision |

HitPay's own words for Model B: *"Platforms that serve other businesses —
CRMs, booking systems, marketplaces, agency tools, and vertical SaaS
products — often need to offer payment acceptance to their customers without
holding a payment institution licence themselves."* HitPay holds the
licences (BNM in Malaysia), does the KYB/KYC and processes the payments.

**Model A needs none of that.** The restaurant is simply a customer paying
an invoice. We need our **own** HitPay account, verified as a Malaysian
business.

**3. What are the catches?** Three, and they decide the design:

- **Neither Malaysian method renews itself.** DuitNow and Touch 'n Go are
  both **one-off** here. Every cycle the customer pays again — the renewal
  is a manual step. This is a deliberate choice (no dependency on a HitPay
  merchant account for the auto-charge), and it is the **single most
  important product consequence** of this document.
- **Where *we* are onboarded decides the money.** DuitNow and TnG settle in
  **MYR** for a Malaysian merchant. A Singapore account can still take the
  cross-border QR, but it settles in **SGD** with a conversion — so a
  Malaysian account is the clean answer (see the open questions).
- **The currency.** Our plans are priced in **USD** by default
  (`subscription_plans.currency_code = 'USD'`), while DuitNow and TnG
  settle in **MYR**. When a Malaysian customer pays by QR, the amount
  **converts to the MYR price of the same plan** (section 12). And the
  **method is chosen once** (section 4.4): Stripe stays USD for every
  cycle, DuitNow and TnG stay MYR for every cycle — which is why the
  invoice page needs a **Pay now** button for every unpaid invoice
  (section 6.5).
- **The customer never picks a method per invoice.** The invoice is paid
  with the subscription's method, and the QR is **generated by the API at
  the click** — the same flow for DuitNow and Touch 'n Go, so there is no
  way to end up paying a USD amount on a ringgit invoice (section 6.5).
  Stripe is unaffected: its renewal is charged automatically.

## 2. What HitPay is

HitPay is an omnichannel payments platform for Asia-Pacific, with **direct
payment licences** in its core markets — **MAS (Singapore), BNM (Malaysia),
BSP (Philippines)** — and it acquires the local methods itself.

For a Malaysian merchant account:

| Capability | Detail |
|---|---|
| Licence | **BNM Merchant Acquirer** |
| Card (online) | HitPay direct + Stripe |
| Local APMs | FPX, DuitNow Online Banking, DuitNow QR, Touch 'n Go eWallet, GrabPay, ShopeePay, MAE, Atome, SPayLater, Grab PayLater, WeChat Pay, Alipay |
| Recurring | Cards, TnG, ShopeePay, GrabPay, ZaloPay — **not used by this design** |
| Settlement | MYR, to our own Malaysian bank account |

Two facts from the docs worth keeping in mind:

- *"The market you pick is where **your business is onboarded**. The payment
  methods listed are where your customers pay from."*
- *"All alternative payment methods (APMs, regional QR rails, recurring) are
  always HitPay-acquired direct."* — HitPay is the acquirer, not a reseller
  of someone else's licence.

### 2.1 The business behind the account — a sole proprietor

**Kens' decision (November 2, 2026): the business starts as an
individual** (a sole proprietorship), not a company, targeting **Malaysia
and Cambodia**.

HitPay's own Malaysia onboarding rules say a sole proprietor is a first-class
entity type:

| The entity type | The document HitPay asks for |
|---|---|
| **Business (Sole Proprietor)** | **the SSM Business Profile** (the ROB registration) |
| Company | the SSM Company Profile |
| LLP | the LLP profile |

And the payout rule:

> *"**Companies and LLPs** must use a **corporate bank account** to receive
> payouts. **Sole Proprietors** may use a **personal bank account**
> registered under the owner's name."*

So an individual business in Malaysia **can** hold a HitPay account. The
documents:

- the **SSM Business Profile** (the sole proprietorship registration — the
  "Business (Sole Proprietor)" profile, not a company profile),
- the account representative's **ID** and a **selfie** (identity
  verification),
- a **personal bank account** in the owner's name (the payouts go there).

**Three consequences for this design:**

1. **The account must be Malaysian, not Cambodian.** HitPay does not onboard
   Cambodian businesses — the country-specific verification pages exist for
   Singapore, Malaysia, the Philippines, Thailand, Hong Kong, Australia and
   New Zealand. **There is no Cambodia page.** So the Cambodia market keeps
   **PayWay + Stripe + Pay Later**, and the HitPay account is a Malaysian
   one. (This is also why the onboarding market question resolves to
   **Malaysia** rather than Singapore: MYR settlement, and the customers who
   need TnG and DuitNow are the Malaysian ones.)
2. **A registered sole proprietorship is needed, not just an ID.** HitPay's
   sign-up accepts a personal ID (their blog mentions sole proprietors
   verifying with personal identification), but the **Malaysia verification**
   asks for the **SSM Business Profile**. So the registration (ROB) is the
   step to do first.
3. **The platform model (Model B) is unaffected by our entity type** — the
   KYB sits with each **sub-merchant** (each restaurant), not with us. But
   the **Platform Key** arrangement (commissions) is commercial and may
   assume a company on our side — worth one email to HitPay when we get
   there.

**The business side (not HitPay's business, but ours):** as an individual,
the invoices we issue to the restaurants come from an individual trading
as a business — the receipts, the tax and the contracts follow that
structure. That is a decision for Kens and his accountant; the **code does
not care** (nothing in this document assumes a company).

## 3. The two models in detail

### 3.1 Model A — Ideazes is the merchant (what we build now)

```
The restaurant (the customer)  ──pays──►  IDEAZES (the merchant)
                                          │  collects via HitPay
                                          ▼
                                    HitPay → our MYR bank account
```

- One HitPay account, verified once, owned by Ideazes.
- The restaurant pays the **subscription** with DuitNow or Touch 'n Go.
- HitPay's BNM licence covers us; we do not touch the licensing.
- The access rules stay exactly as they are today: the invoice is paid → the
  access opens (`subscription-lifecycle.mdx`).

### 3.2 Model B — the platform model (later, if we want it)

```
The diner  ──pays──►  IDEAZES (the platform)  ──►  HitPay  ──►  the restaurant's bank account
                            │                    (BNM licence)
                            └── commission (with a Platform Key)
```

HitPay's Platforms model:

- Every restaurant is a **sub-merchant**: their **own** HitPay account, their
  **own** business verification, their **own** payouts. *"Funds are not
  pooled through the platform."*
- Two ways to reach their accounts:
  - **Option 1 — their API key** (they paste it into our UI). *"Not
    recommended"* — it is full access to their account, non-expiring, and
    rotating it breaks their own integration.
  - **Option 2 — OAuth** ("Connect your HitPay account"). The clean one,
    with scoped tokens they can revoke. **It is in beta**, and webhooks /
    virtual accounts / static QR / terminals still need a merchant key.
- **The add-on — the Platform Key** (`X-PLATFORM-KEY`): asks HitPay to
  enable our account as a Platform account. It unlocks **commissions** (a
  platform-wide rate or a fixed amount per transaction) and **unified
  webhooks** (all the sub-merchants' charge events land on **our** webhook
  endpoint, not theirs). Enabling it needs an email to
  `support@hit-pay.com`.

**Model B is a separate document when we want it.** It changes what a
"restaurant" is in our data model, and it puts a business-registration
funnel in front of every new tenant.

### 3.3 Why Model A is enough for now

The thing Kens asked for is *"the customer portal for subscription
payment"* — the restaurant paying us. That is Model A. Model B is about the
**diner** paying the restaurant, which we do not do yet (the POS records the
payment, it does not settle it).

## 4. The payment methods by country (the rule, made executable)

Kens's rule:

| The customer's country of operations | The payment methods offered |
|---|---|
| **Malaysia** | Stripe, Pay Later, **DuitNow**, **Touch 'n Go** |
| **Cambodia** | PayWay (not integrated yet), Stripe, Pay Later |
| **Anything else** | Stripe only |

The two HitPay methods are **one-off payments** (section 5) — the customer
pays each cycle. The country's price currency follows the method (section
12).

### 4.1 One function, two callers

The rule must live in **one** place, and it must be enforced by the server:

```python
# website_api/app/services/payment_methods.py  (NEW)

STRIPE = "stripe"
PAY_LATER = "pay_later"
HITPAY_TNG = "hitpay_touch_n_go"
HITPAY_DUITNOW = "hitpay_duitnow"
PAYWAY = "payway"

COUNTRY_METHODS = {
    "MY": [STRIPE, PAY_LATER, HITPAY_TNG, HITPAY_DUITNOW],
    "KH": [PAYWAY, STRIPE, PAY_LATER],
}

def available_payment_methods(country: str | None) -> list[str]:
    """The payment methods a customer in this country may pay with.

    The browser asks what it may show. The API decides what it accepts.
    """
    if not country:
        return [STRIPE, PAY_LATER]          # unknown country → the safe pair
    return COUNTRY_METHODS.get(country.upper(), [STRIPE])
```

- **The site** calls it (through the API) to render the options.
- **The API** calls it to **accept** the choice. *"The client never chooses
  the tenant"* — the same rule as the tenant claim: the browser's choice is
  a claim, the server decides. A request with
  `payment_method: "hitpay_duitnow"` from a Cambodian customer is
  **rejected**, not silently downgraded.

### 4.2 The country is already there

`website_customers.country` exists ✓ and is set at signup
(`website_api/app/routes/auth.py` → the signup model). As of November 2,
2026:

| Customer | Country |
|---|---|
| Demo Restaurant Group (id 1) | **Cambodia** |
| VARMAN (id 12) | Cambodia |
| AES Restaurant (id 23) | **Malaysia** |

**The gap:** the country is set by whoever creates the account, never by the
customer. It should be a first-class field of the signup and of the portal
(a customer can change it), and the **null** case must be decided
deliberately (the safe pair: Stripe + Pay Later).

### 4.3 What "Pay Later" means here (it is not a gateway)

`pay_later` in our system is **our own invoice flow** — no gateway, no
auto-charge, the customer pays by bank transfer and the invoice is marked
paid by us. It is not a HitPay method. (The HitPay docs mention
`grabpay_paylater` and `shopee_pay_later` — those are *different* things:
Buy-Now-Pay-Later credit products. If we ever want those, they are new
methods, not our `pay_later`.)

### 4.4 The method is chosen once and it stays

**Kens' rule (November 2, 2026):** the payment method is decided **at the
subscribe step** and it applies to **every cycle** of that subscription.

> *"If subscribe [with] Stripe then it is Stripe for the following
> [cycles]. Touch 'n Go or DuitNow — also the same."*

So:

- The subscription carries the method (`subscriptions.payment_method`),
  and the **invoice inherits it**. The customer does **not** choose a
  method per invoice.
- The **currency follows the method** (section 12): a Stripe subscription
  invoices USD, a DuitNow or Touch 'n Go subscription invoices **MYR** —
  for the first payment and for every renewal.
- The **pay button on the invoice page** therefore has **one** method: the
  subscription's. A MYR invoice is paid with the MYR method — never with a
  USD Stripe checkout, and never the other way round.
- The rule already exists for Stripe: the upgrade route refuses a method
  change (*"Cannot change payment method. You are currently on Stripe.
  Please contact support to change your payment method."*). HitPay gets the
  same rule.
- The server enforces it: the invoice's method comes from the
  subscription, not from the request body. The browser's
  `payment_method` is a claim.

**Why it matters:** a method that changes per invoice would mean a
subscription that bills USD one month and MYR the next — two currencies,
two prices and two different totals for the same plan. One method per
subscription keeps the invoice, the currency and the price in one place.

*(The open question: can a customer **switch** method later — from Stripe
to Touch 'n Go, say? Today: no, they contact support. Section 15,
question 6.)*

## 5. The Malaysian methods, in detail

The matrix from HitPay's payment-method reference (the Malaysia tab):

| Method | Code | Online | In-person | Recurring (HitPay) | Refunds | Currency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Touch 'n Go eWallet** | `touch_n_go` | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ *(not used)* | ✅ | MYR |
| **DuitNow** | `duitnow` | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | MYR |
| Cards | `card` | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | MYR |
| GrabPay | `grabpay_direct` | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | MYR |
| ShopeePay | `shopee_pay` | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | MYR |
| FPX | `fpx` | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ | MYR |
| MayBank QR | `razer_maybankqr` | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ | MYR |

**What we use: the one-off flow for both.** The "Recurring" column is
HitPay's capability, not our design — the auto-charge needs our own HitPay
merchant account, so it is out of scope (section 15, question 1).

### 5.1 DuitNow — the QR

- We generate the QR (`generate_qr=true` → `qr_code_data.qr_code` +
  `qr_code_expiry`) and show it in the portal. The customer scans it with
  the DuitNow app.
- DuitNow is a **QR flow** — the code is the payment.
- `payment_request.completed` fires → the invoice is paid → the access opens.
- **No auto-charge, ever.** And **no refunds** — a DuitNow payment cannot be
  refunded through HitPay. That is a customer-facing caveat we must show
  before the customer pays.

### 5.2 Touch 'n Go — the same behaviour, a different presentation

- TnG works as a one-off too: `payment_methods[]=touch_n_go` with
  `generate_qr=true` gives a **QR** (verified in the sandbox: **201**), and
  with `generate_direct_link=true` it gives a **direct link** to the TnG app
  (also verified: **201**).
- The direct link is the nicer UX on a phone (the app opens, the customer
  confirms); the QR is the fallback (a desktop customer scans it with the
  phone, exactly like DuitNow).
- **What we ask for:** Touch 'n Go gets the **direct link**
  (`generate_direct_link=true` + the redirect back to the portal) — HitPay
  asks for **exactly one** of the two per request, so the TnG request is
  not a QR request. The panel then shows the **"Pay with Touch 'n Go"**
  button (and the QR we draw from the same link, so a desktop customer can
  scan it too).
- **Both are one-off.** The wallet is not saved and nothing is charged
  automatically — the same manual cycle as DuitNow.

### 5.2b The QR image — the sandbox serves HTML, not a PNG

HitPay gives us the **payload of the code** in `qr_code_data.qr_code` — and
it is not the same thing in the two places:

| Where | What the value is | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| **production** | a **raw payload string** (the DuitNow/EMV string) | **draw the QR** from it (HitPay's own words: *"you should convert it into a scannable QR code using a QR code library"*) |
| **the sandbox** | a **URL** (the mock payment page) | HitPay's own note: *"visit the URL directly or scan it with a standard QR reader"* → the QR carries that URL |

So the answer is the same in both: **we draw the QR**, from the value
HitPay gave us. (Only a value that is already an **image** is used as it
is.)

| Where | What the URL serves |
|---|---|
| **production** | a **PNG** (`qr_image_mime: image/png`) → an `<img>` shows it |
| **the sandbox** | the **mock payment page** (HTML!) → an `<img>` is a **broken image** ✗ |

So the API owns the image:

- `GET /api/billing/invoices/{invoice_id}/qr` → the invoice's own payment
  request → the QR URL → **fetch it; if it is not an image, draw the QR
  ourselves** (`qrcode` + Pillow → a PNG) from that same link. The URL is
  the payment either way, so the customer's app opens it after the scan —
  the same code.
- When the payment request has no QR (Touch 'n Go, or a lookup that does
  not carry it), the **checkout link** (`payment_request.url`) is used.
- The browser cannot use a plain `<img src>` (the endpoint needs the auth
  header), so the panel **fetches the image** and shows it as an object
  URL — with HitPay's own URL as the fallback.
- It is drawn **on demand**, because the code expires.

**The sandbox adds one hop — by design.** Scanning the QR in the sandbox
opens HitPay's **mock payment page** (the page with the DuitNow icon),
because the sandbox simulates the banking app. In production the QR
carries the raw DuitNow payload, so the customer's banking app pays
**directly** — there is no second page.

### 5.3 The consequence: what a HitPay subscription is

A DuitNow or Touch 'n Go customer must pay **every cycle by hand**. The
honest design:

- The first payment happens at the subscribe step (a QR, or a TnG link).
- Every cycle after that, the renewal cron writes the **invoice** and the
  billing page shows the QR / the TnG link for that invoice.
- If the customer does not pay, the normal grace rules apply (14 days) and
  then the access closes — exactly as designed in
  `subscription-lifecycle.mdx`. The subscription never falls into a state
  where it is paid but the access is closed.

**The product question this creates** (section 15, question 2): a customer
who is used to a card being charged automatically now has to remember. The
portal has to say so **before** they choose, not after.

## 6. The flows

### 6.1 The first payment (both methods)

```
The portal (the customer picks "DuitNow" or "Touch 'n Go")
   ↓
POST /api/subscriptions/  {payment_method: "hitpay_duitnow" | "hitpay_touch_n_go", ...}
   ↓ the website API
   - checks the country (section 4.1)          → refuse if not allowed
   - resolves the price in MYR (section 12)    → the MYR row of plan_prices
   - POST https://api.hit-pay.com/v1/payment-requests
        amount = the MYR price, currency = myr,
        payment_methods[] = duitnow | touch_n_go,
        generate_qr = true (or generate_direct_link = true for the TnG link),
        name = the restaurant name, email = the customer's email,
        purpose = the invoice description,
        reference_number = the invoice id
        (these are **HitPay's** parameter names - docs.hitpayapp.com,
        "Step 1: Create a Payment Request")
   ↓ 201 → qr_code_data.qr_code  /  direct_link.direct_link_url
   ↓
{ "requires_payment": true,
  "payment": { "provider": "hitpay", "method": "hitpay_duitnow",
               "qr_code": "…", "expires_at": "…", "payment_request_id": "…" } }
   ↓
The portal renders the QR (or the TnG button)
   ↓
The customer scans / confirms → payment_request.completed (webhook)
   ↓ the invoice is marked paid → the access opens → the period is set
```

**The first payment is not optional** — the subscription is created with the
invoice, and the period starts when the payment lands (the same way Stripe
works today).

### 6.2 The renewal (the cron) — no charging, only an invoice

```
The daily cron (the same one that writes the Stripe invoices)
   ↓
For each subscription due for renewal:
    if the provider is hitpay:
        → write the invoice (the MYR amount) and NOTHING else
        → the billing page shows the QR / the TnG link for that invoice
        → the customer pays → payment_request.completed → the invoice is paid
        → if they do not pay: the normal grace rules (14 days) → then expired
```

**The rule that matters:** the webhook marks the invoice paid (the gateway
is the source of truth), exactly as Stripe does today. The cron never marks
an invoice paid because *it* decided to — and it never tries to charge a
wallet, because there is no wallet.

**Monthly and yearly both work the same way** (Kens' decision,
November 2, 2026 — *"we can't force the customer to pay for annual if they
use DuitNow or Touch 'n Go"*). There is **no restriction** on the billing
cycle for the two HitPay methods:

| The cycle | The invoice | The price | When the next invoice is written |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monthly** | MYR 40 (Basic) | the MYR monthly row | a month later |
| **Annual** | MYR 400 (Basic) | the MYR annual row | **a year** later |

The customer chooses the cycle at the subscribe step, exactly as they do
with Stripe. An annual HitPay customer pays **once a year** — which is
the nicest thing about the annual plan for them: one QR, twelve months of
access. The monthly customer pays once a month. Same flow, same button,
same MYR invoice — only the amount and the interval differ.

Nothing in the design treats the cycles differently: `resolve_price()`
reads the `annual` column of `plan_prices` when `billing_cycle ==
'annual'`, and the cron writes the next invoice when the period ends.

### 6.3 The upgrade

- An upgrade (a plan change) with a **prorated amount**: the new amount is
  written as an invoice in the same currency as the old subscription, and
  the customer pays it with the same method.
- The amount is resolved the same way as section 12 — the MYR row for a
  HitPay payment.

### 6.4 The cancellation

- The customer cancels in the portal → the subscription is cancelled → **the
  invoices stop**. There is nothing to cancel at the gateway (a one-off
  payment method has no subscription), so the provider call is a no-op that
  returns "nothing to cancel".
- The existing cancel route already knows how to tell a provider
  (`payment_providers.py`, phase 4 of the lifecycle work) — HitPay slots in
  there.

### 6.5b Where the customer lands after the payment

**The same place for every method** — the **overview** (`/portal`), exactly
like the Stripe checkout:

| The method | What happens |
|---|---|
| **DuitNow** (the QR) | the customer stays on our page → the panel polls the status every 8 seconds → the webhook flips the invoice → the panel closes, the portal goes to the **overview** and says *"Payment received — your subscription is active"* ✓ |
| **Touch 'n Go** (the link) | the browser goes to HitPay → HitPay redirects back to `?payment=success&status=completed` → the portal cleans the query, shows *"Payment received — we are confirming it"* and stays on the **overview** ✓ |

The message differs on purpose: the **QR flow** knows the payment arrived
(the webhook confirmed it), the **redirect** only says it is being
confirmed — the redirect alone proves nothing (the invoice turns paid when
the webhook arrives ✓).

### 6.5 Paying an invoice from the portal — the Pay button

The subscription is not the only thing the customer can pay: **every unpaid
invoice** gets a **Pay now** button on the billing page.

**The rule Kens confirmed (November 2, 2026):** the customer never chooses
a method at the invoice. The **invoice is paid with the subscription's
method**, and **the QR is generated by the API at the moment the customer
clicks "Pay now"** — for the second invoice onward, and for every invoice
after it. There is no stored QR and no method picker on the invoice page.

That is what keeps the subscription to **one method and one currency**
(section 4.4): the same QR flow for DuitNow and Touch 'n Go, the same
MYR invoice, the same MYR price — no way to end up paying a USD amount on
a ringgit invoice, and no way for a customer to wander into another
gateway.

**What is there today** (`site/src/app/[locale]/portal/billing/page.tsx`):

```jsx
{invoice.status === 'pending' && (
  <button onClick={() => handlePayInvoice(invoice.id)}>
    {t('billing.payNow')}
  </button>
)}
```

Two gaps:

1. **Only `pending` invoices get the button.** An **`overdue`** invoice —
   the one that actually needs paying — has **no button**. The button must
   be shown for `pending` **and** `overdue` (everything except `paid`).
2. **The method is hard-coded.** `handlePayInvoice(invoice.id)` uses the
   default `payment_method = 'stripe'` → a Malaysian subscription would be
   sent to a USD Stripe checkout. The method must come from the
   **subscription** (section 4.4).

**The new flow:**

```
The billing page → the customer clicks "Pay now" on an unpaid invoice
   ↓
POST /api/billing/invoices/{invoice_id}/pay
     (the method is NOT taken from the body - the API reads it from the
      subscription; the body may only confirm it)
   ↓ the website API
   - the invoice belongs to the customer and is not paid  → else 404 / 400
   - the method = subscription.payment_method (the locked one)
   - the method is allowed in the customer's country     → else 400
   - the currency = the method's currency (section 12)
   ↓
   STRIPE:  create the checkout session (today's behaviour)
            → { "requires_checkout": true, "checkout_url": "https://…" }
   HITPAY:  POST /v1/payment-requests  (amount = the invoice amount in MYR,
            currency = myr, payment_methods[] = duitnow | touch_n_go,
            generate_qr = true            (DuitNow),
            generate_direct_link = true   (Touch 'n Go),
            name = the restaurant name, email = the customer's email,
            purpose = the invoice description,
            reference_number = the invoice id)
            → { "requires_payment": true,
                "payment": { "provider": "hitpay",
                             "method": "hitpay_duitnow",
                             "qr_code": "…",
                             "expires_at": "…",
                             "payment_request_id": "…" } }
            (for Touch 'n Go the panel may also offer the direct link:
             "direct_link": "https://…")
   ↓
The portal opens the payment panel:
   - a QR code (with the expiry) or the "Pay with Touch 'n Go" button
   - "the code expires in N minutes" + a "Get a new code" button
   - a note: "the payment is confirmed automatically" (the webhook)
   ↓
payment_request.completed → the invoice is paid → the portal refreshes
```

**The QR is created at the click — never before**

| | |
|---|---|
| Nothing is pre-generated | no QR, no payment link and no payment request exist before the customer clicks. The invoice only remembers the **last** payment request id (the audit trail and the idempotency) |
| Why | a QR generated at renewal time would be **expired** by the time the customer opens the page — HitPay's QR codes carry an expiry (`qr_code_expiry`). Generating it at the click means the code the customer scans is always live |
| The second invoice onward | exactly the same click → the same API → a fresh QR for **that** invoice. Nothing about the first invoice is reused |
| An expired code | the customer clicks again → a **new** payment request for the same invoice (the old one is abandoned). The invoice is still the same invoice, the same MYR amount |
| One click, one request | the panel keeps the request id; the "Get a new code" action is a second click, and it does **not** create a second invoice — only a second payment request for the first |

**DuitNow and Touch 'n Go: the same flow, one difference**

| | DuitNow | Touch 'n Go |
|---|---|---|
| The QR (`generate_qr=true`) | ✅ verified: **201** + a QR + an expiry | ✅ verified: **201** + a QR |
| The direct link (`generate_direct_link=true`) | ❌ (DuitNow is QR only) | ✅ verified: **201** + a link to the TnG app |
| What the panel shows | the QR | the QR **and** the "Pay with Touch 'n Go" button (the app opens on a phone — the nicer way to pay) |
| Refunds | ❌ **not refundable** through HitPay | ✅ refundable (cards and TnG) |
| The flow | identical | identical |

**And the contrast with Stripe:** a Stripe subscription **never needs the
button** — the renewal is charged automatically (that is the existing
Stripe behaviour and it is unaffected). The button exists for the two
methods that require the customer to act: the **first** payment of any
subscription, and **every** invoice of a HitPay subscription.

**The panel states** (the site must handle all of them):

| The state | What the customer sees |
|---|---|
| waiting | the QR + the expiry countdown |
| the code expired | "This code has expired" + **Get a new code** (a new payment request for the same invoice; the old one is abandoned) |
| paid | "Payment received — thank you" (the webhook already marked the invoice) |
| failed | the reason + a retry |

**A note for the customer:** with DuitNow the payment cannot be refunded

not after.

## 7. The data model

### 7.1 No saved wallets

**There is no `hitpay_payment_methods` table in this design.** A saved
wallet belongs to the auto-charge flow we are not building: without it there
is nothing to store (no token, no recurring billing id, no
`method_attached` event). The wallet token would stay at HitPay anyway —
we would only have stored a handle — so there is nothing to lose.

*(If the auto-charge is ever built, the table comes back: `customer_id`,
`recurring_billing_id`, `payment_method`, `currency`, `status`,
`attached_at`, `detached_at`.)*

### 7.2 Modified — `subscriptions`

| Column | Notes |
|---|---|
| `payment_provider` | `'hitpay'` (the column phase 4 of the lifecycle work added) |
| `payment_method` | `'hitpay_duitnow'` / `'hitpay_touch_n_go'` (our own vocabulary, not HitPay's) |
| `currency` | **MYR** for a HitPay subscription (today it follows the plan's USD default — see section 12) |

### 7.3 Modified — `invoices`

| Column | Notes |
|---|---|
| `payment_method` | **exists already** ✓ → `'hitpay_duitnow'` / `'hitpay_touch_n_go'` — no new column needed for the method |
| `amount`, `currency` | **exist already** ✓ → the MYR amount and the MYR currency (section 12) |
| `hitpay_payment_request_id` | **NEW** — the HitPay payment request (the QR / the link), the audit trail. `payment_reference` also exists, but it is **our own** field (the admin types it, it is printed on the invoice) — we do not overwrite it |

### 7.4 New table — `hitpay_webhook_events` (the idempotency)

HitPay retries a webhook that is not answered with a 2xx. The same event can
therefore arrive twice, and it must not mark an invoice paid twice. Every
webhook is recorded **before** it is processed:

| Column | Notes |
|---|---|
| `event_id` | the HitPay event id — **unique** |
| `event_type` | `payment_request.completed`, … |
| `received_at` | |
| `processed_at` | |
| `payload` | the raw JSON (for the audit) |

A duplicate insert → the second delivery is ignored (and answered 200).

## 8. The API integration

### 8.1 The environment variables

```bash
# website_api/.env
HITPAY_API_KEY=<the sandbox key>          # the production key in production
HITPAY_API_URL=https://api.sandbox.hit-pay.com   # https://api.hit-pay.com in production

```

**The credentials are server-side only.** They go in `.env` (never in git,
never in the browser, never in the site's environment variables — the site
talks to **our** API, our API talks to HitPay).

### 8.2 The client — `website_api/app/services/hitpay.py` (NEW)

A thin, synchronous `httpx`/`requests` client:

- `X-BUSINESS-API-KEY: <key>` on every request, `X-Requested-With:
  XMLHttpRequest`, `Content-Type: application/x-www-form-urlencoded`.
- The **base URL comes from the environment** — the same code runs against
  the sandbox and production.
- Every method returns a small result object: `{ok, id, raw, error}` — the
  errors never raise into the caller's face (the billing flow must not
  break because a gateway hiccuped — the same rule the Stripe provider
  follows).

### 8.3 The provider — `HitPayProvider`

The lifecycle work (phase 4) built the interface
(`website_api/app/services/payment_providers.py`):

```python
provider = get_provider(sub.payment_provider)   # stripe | hitpay | payway
provider.cancel_at_period_end(sub)
```

`HitPayProvider` implements the same three methods
(`cancel_at_period_end`, `resume`, `is_active`):

| Method | What HitPay does |
|---|---|
| `cancel_at_period_end` | **nothing to cancel** — a one-off method has no subscription. It stops our invoices and returns a "nothing to cancel" note (not an error) |
| `resume` | nothing to resume at the gateway — the invoices start again |
| `is_active` | the subscription's own state (the last invoice paid, the period not ended) |

The subscription code never talks to HitPay directly.

### 8.4 The signature — the salt

The webhook carries `Hitpay-Signature: HMAC-SHA256 of the raw JSON body,
using that webhook endpoint's salt`.

```python
import hmac, hashlib

def verify(raw_body: bytes, signature: str, salt: str) -> bool:
    expected = hmac.new(salt.encode(), raw_body, hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()
    return hmac.compare_digest(expected, (signature or "").strip())
```

Three rules:

- The **raw** body — the parsed-and-re-serialised JSON has a different
  signature. Read the body as bytes, verify, *then* parse.
- `hmac.compare_digest` — a constant-time comparison.
- A wrong signature → **400, nothing else happens**.

**The salt is per endpoint, not per account.** HitPay's own words: *"Each
webhook endpoint you create under **Developers → Webhook Endpoints** has
its own salt, used to verify `Hitpay-Signature`."* So there are **two**
salts in play:

| The salt | Where it comes from | What it signs |
|---|---|---|
| `HITPAY_SALT` | the account (Settings → API Keys) | the **API requests** |
| `HITPAY_WEBHOOK_SALT` | **each webhook endpoint** (Settings → Developers → Webhook Endpoints) | the **webhooks** |

The code uses `HITPAY_WEBHOOK_SALT` and falls back to `HITPAY_SALT` (so
the account salt works until the endpoint's own salt is pasted in).

**Status (November 3, 2026):** the endpoint's salt is in
`website_api/.env` (`HITPAY_WEBHOOK_SALT`) ✓ and the webhook **verifies**
✓ — the sandbox's `payment_request.completed` marks the invoice paid and
starts the subscription within seconds ✓.

## 9. The webhook

### 9.1 The endpoint

```
POST https://apisite.ideazes.com/api/webhooks/hitpay
```

HitPay's rules: the URL must be **HTTPS** and resolve to a **public**
destination — *"Loopback and private network addresses are rejected."*
`apisite.ideazes.com` is public ✓. The endpoint must be registered in the
HitPay dashboard (**Developers → Webhook Endpoints**) or via
`POST /v1/webhook-events`, and it must be **the same URL for production and
a different one for the sandbox** (the sandbox sends to the test endpoint).

**One URL, the event type in the header.** HitPay posts to
`/api/webhooks/hitpay` — *not* to a URL with the event in the path. The
event comes in the headers:

```
Hitpay-Event-Type: completed | created | updated | failed
Hitpay-Event-Object: payment_request | charge | payout | invoice | order | transfer
Hitpay-Signature: <the HMAC>
```

So the route is `POST /api/webhooks/hitpay` and the event type is built
from the headers (`completed` → `payment_request.completed` ✓). The route
with the event in the path is kept as well — it costs nothing.

**Two bugs that kept the webhooks from ever arriving** (found November 3,
2026 — they are fixed):

1. **Our own security middleware blocked them.** The tier-1 middleware
   checks the `Origin` header — a payment gateway sends **none**, so
   every webhook was answered with **403 Forbidden** before the handler
   could verify the signature. The webhooks are now exempt from the
   origin check (their security is the **signature** ✓). This also
   affected the **Stripe** webhook ✓.
2. **The `.env` was read by a relative path** (`env_file = ".env"`), so
   every process that did not sit in the project folder — the
   reconciliation cron among them — ran with an **empty HitPay key**
   (*"Invalid business api key"*). The path is now absolute ✓.

### 9.2 The events we listen to

| Event | What it means for us |
|---|---|
| `payment_request.completed` | the QR / the TnG link was paid → **the invoice is paid** |
| `payment_request.failed` | the payment failed → the invoice stays unpaid |
| `charge.created` | a charge succeeded (the cards) → the invoice is paid |
| `charge.failed` | a charge failed → the invoice stays unpaid → the grace rules |
| `charge.updated` | a refund happened (the cards and TnG — **not** DuitNow) |

**Not used:** `recurring_billing.method_attached` /
`method_detached` / `subscription_updated` — they belong to the auto-charge
flow that is out of scope. They are harmless to receive; the handler simply
does not know them.

### 9.3 The allowlist

If the origin firewall filters, allow:

| Environment | IPs |
|---|---|
| Production | `3.1.13.32`, `52.77.254.34` |
| Sandbox | `54.179.156.147` |

The site sits behind Cloudflare → the **origin** (Caddy) must let those IPs
through, and the endpoint must not be blocked by the WAF.

**And the application must not block them either** — see 9.1 ✓ (the
origin check is for the browser, not for the gateways).

### 9.4 How we know the customer really paid

**Yes — the webhook tells us.** `payment_request.completed` means the money
landed; `payment_request.failed` means it did not. But a webhook is an
**inbound message from the internet**, so it is verified in four steps
before it touches an invoice:

| # | The check | What it stops |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The signature** — `Hitpay-Signature` = HMAC-SHA256 of the **raw** body with the salt, compared with `hmac.compare_digest` | a forged "paid" event |
| 2 | **The event id** — the `hitpay_webhook_events` insert is unique | the same payment being counted twice (HitPay retries) |
| 3 | **The reference** — our `reference` is the invoice id; the invoice must exist, belong to the customer and still be unpaid | a payment landing on the wrong invoice |
| 4 | **The amount and the currency** — the event's amount and currency must equal the invoice's | a MYR 1.00 payment marking a MYR 80.00 invoice paid |

If any check fails → the invoice is **not** touched, the event is logged,
and (for checks 3 and 4) it raises an alert: that means somebody paid the
wrong amount and **the money has to go back to them**.

**The webhook can be delayed, or lost.** Two fallbacks:

- **The reconciliation poll** — the daily cron asks HitPay for the state of
  the payment requests it is waiting for (`GET /v1/payment-requests/{id}`)
  and marks the invoice paid if HitPay says so. The webhook is the fast
  path; the poll is the safety net. (The same pattern as the plan cache: a
  push, and a pull that repairs a lost push.)
- **The portal polls while the panel is open** — the payment panel asks the
  API every few seconds *"is this invoice paid yet?"* so the customer sees
  **"Payment received — thank you"** without refreshing the page. The
  answer comes from our invoice's state, which the webhook already set.

**The order of the truth:** the **gateway** is the source of truth for the
payment; **our** invoice is the source of truth for the access. The webhook
moves the invoice; the access rules read the invoice. Neither one guesses.

## 10. The renewal processor (the cron)

The existing daily cron (`website_api/scripts/send_notifications.py`) already
writes the invoices and tells the POS backend. For HitPay there is **no
extra step** — the only difference is the currency and the invoice:

```
For each subscription due for renewal:
    if the provider is hitpay:
        → the invoice is written in MYR (section 12)
        → nothing is charged (there is no wallet)
        → the billing page shows the QR / the TnG link for that invoice
        → the customer pays → payment_request.completed → the invoice is paid
        → if they do not pay: the normal grace rules (14 days) → then expired
```

**The rule that matters:** the webhook marks the invoice paid (the gateway
is the source of truth), exactly as Stripe does today. The cron never marks
an invoice paid because *it* decided to.

**The safety net (built):** the webhook is the fast path, but a webhook can
be lost. `reconcile_hitpay_payments()` — every 10 minutes
(`send_notifications.py --reconcile-only`) — asks HitPay about every invoice
that waits for a payment and applies **the same checks** the webhook
applies (the amount, the currency). It never invents a payment: only the
gateway can say that the money arrived.

## 12. The currency — the rule and the conversion

### 12.1 The situation today

- `subscription_plans.currency_code = 'USD'` (Starter/Basic/Pro) with a
  `plan_prices` table that already holds **MYR** rows:
  Starter 0/0, Basic **MYR 40 / 400**, Pro **MYR 80 / 800**, and a fourth
  plan at **MYR 200 / 2000**.
- The Stripe checkout hard-codes `'currency': 'usd'`.
- The invoice takes `plan.currency_code or "MYR"`.
- The prices are **already prepared for ringgit** — but nothing selects the
  row by the customer's country yet.

### 12.2 The rule (Kens' decision, November 2, 2026)

> **A Malaysian customer who selects a USD plan and then chooses DuitNow or
> Touch 'n Go is charged the MYR price of the same plan.**

And (Kens, later the same evening): **a Malaysian customer chooses the
currency**, and **the country decides whether ringgit is on the table at
all**:

| The customer's country | The currency switch | The method | The plan price used | The invoice currency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Malaysia** | **USD or MYR — the customer chooses** | Stripe | the row of the chosen currency | **USD or MYR** |
| **Malaysia** | **USD** | Pay Later | the row of the chosen currency | **USD or MYR** |
| **Malaysia** | **MYR** | **DuitNow / Touch 'n Go** | **the MYR row of the same plan** | **MYR** (always) |
| Cambodia | **USD — locked** | anything | the USD row | USD |
| Anywhere else | **USD — locked** | Stripe / Pay Later | the USD row | USD |

**The methods follow the currency (Malaysia):** in **USD** only **Stripe and
Pay Later** are on the table; in **MYR** all **four** are. The Malaysian
wallets belong to the ringgit column — they settle in ringgit, so they are
offered with it.

**The country decides whether ringgit exists.** MYR is a Malaysian currency:
a customer outside Malaysia sees a **disabled switch** (USD) and **the
server enforces the same rule** — the body's `currency` is only a claim,
so a Cambodian customer asking for MYR is charged in **USD** (and one
asking for DuitNow is refused outright).

**And the method is locked at the subscribe step (section 4.4)** — so the
currency is locked with it. The customer chooses the method **once**:

```
Subscribe with Stripe   → every invoice of that subscription is in the
                           currency the customer chose (USD or MYR)
Subscribe with DuitNow  → every invoice of that subscription is MYR
Subscribe with TnG      → every invoice of that subscription is MYR
```

That is why the renewal cron and the invoice page's **Pay now** button both
read the method from the **subscription**: there is only ever one method and
one currency per subscription.

The implementation (one place, called by the subscribe route, the renewal
cron and the invoice's pay endpoint):

```python
# website_api/app/services/plan_pricing.py  (NEW)

CURRENCY_BY_METHOD = {
    "hitpay_duitnow":    "MYR",
    "hitpay_touch_n_go": "MYR",
}

def resolve_price(db, plan_id: str, billing_cycle: str, method: str | None):
    """The price a customer is actually charged, in the currency they pay in."""
    currency = CURRENCY_BY_METHOD.get(method)      # None → the plan's own currency
    row = db.query(PlanPrice).filter(
        PlanPrice.plan_id == plan_id,
        PlanPrice.currency_code == currency,        # None → the plan's default
        PlanPrice.is_active == True,
    ).one_or_none()

    if row is None:
        # No `|| 1` fallback: if the plan has no MYR price, we do not
        # silently charge the USD amount into a ringgit wallet.
        raise NoPriceForCurrency(plan_id, currency)

    return (row.price_annual if billing_cycle == "annual" else row.price_monthly), currency
```

**One source of truth for the invoice amount:** the subscribe route writes
the invoice with the resolved amount and currency; the renewal cron writes
the next invoice with **the same method → the same currency → the same
price row**. The invoice page only *shows* what the invoice already says —
it never re-resolves a price (that would be a second price for the same
plan).

**What the portal shows** (the customer must see the ringgit before they
pay):

```
IDEAZES Pro — monthly
USD 20.00   →   MYR 80.00        (you will be charged MYR 80.00)
```

### 12.3 The two things this needs

1. **The MYR price must exist** for every plan that a Malaysian customer may
   buy. The admin app already has the plan-price editor (it writes
   `plan_prices`) — the four MYR rows exist; the rule needs a **guard**: a
   plan published to Malaysia without a MYR price must not be sellable by
   DuitNow/TnG. The rule above **fails loudly** instead of charging the USD
   amount.
2. **The invoice currency** must be MYR for a HitPay invoice, and the
   renewal cron must use the same resolution — otherwise the first invoice
   is MYR and the second is USD.

### 12.4 The pricing decision (Kens, November 2, 2026)

~~Should the **Stripe** price for a Malaysian customer also become MYR?~~

**Decided: the customer chooses.** The portal shows the **$ USD / RM MYR**
switch at the plan step, and the choice is stored on the subscription
(`subscriptions.currency`). It is locked from then on — the renewal, the
trial's first invoice and the reminder email all follow the subscription's
currency.

The **existing** customers are not moved: a subscription that was created
in USD stays in USD (its `currency` is on the row).

## 13. The error handling

| Situation | What we do |
|---|---|
| The gateway is down | the payment request fails → the customer is not charged and the subscription is not created → they can retry. **An existing subscription is never touched** by a gateway outage (no charge is attempted) |
| The QR is never paid | the invoice stays unpaid → the normal grace rules (14 days) → a reminder email → the access closes |
| The QR expires before it is paid | the customer asks for a new one → a new payment request for the same invoice (the old one is abandoned) |
| The webhook is delivered twice | the `hitpay_webhook_events` insert is unique → the second one is ignored |
| The signature does not match | **400**, nothing happens |
| The customer wants a refund | **cards and TnG can be refunded** (`charge.updated`); **DuitNow cannot** — the portal must say so before the payment |

### 16.2 The country rule — `app/services/payment_methods.py`

The whole rule is a table. The DB stores the country **name** ("Malaysia"),
the signup form sends the name, so the name is mapped to the code:

```python
COUNTRY_METHODS = {
    "MY": [STRIPE, PAY_LATER, HITPAY_DUITNOW, HITPAY_TNG],
    "KH": [PAYWAY, STRIPE, PAY_LATER],
}
DEFAULT_METHODS = [STRIPE, PAY_LATER]      # an unknown country
```

Three functions, and the split matters:

| The function | Who calls it | What it answers |
|---|---|---|
| `available_payment_methods(country)` | **the site** (via `/billing/payment-methods`) | "what may I show?" |
| `is_method_allowed(country, method)` | **the API** (the subscribe + the upgrade) | "may this customer pay with that?" — the request body is only a *claim* |
| `is_hitpay_method(method)` | the API | "is this one of the two HitPay methods?" |

A Cambodian customer that sends `payment_method: "hitpay_duitnow"` is
**refused**, not silently downgraded.

### 16.3 The currency rule — `app/services/plan_pricing.py`

The currency follows the **method**, not the plan:

```python
CURRENCY_BY_METHOD = {
    "hitpay_duitnow": "MYR",
    "hitpay_touch_n_go": "MYR",
}
```

`resolve_price(db, plan, billing_cycle, method)` returns
`(amount, currency)`:

- a HitPay method → the **MYR row** of `plan_prices`;
- every other method → the plan's own currency and price (today USD).

**There is no fallback.** If a plan has no MYR price, `NoPriceForCurrency`
is raised — we do not quietly charge a dollar amount into a ringgit wallet.
`resolve_price_safe()` is the same thing but returns `(None, None)`, for the
places where a failure must not stop the flow (the renewal cron logs it and
leaves the subscription alone).

### 16.4 The pay route — the method is **resolved, never chosen**

`POST /api/billing/invoices/{invoice_id}/pay` — the actual code:

```python
locked_method = (invoice.payment_method or '').strip() or None
subscription = invoice.subscription
if not locked_method and subscription is not None:
    locked_method = (subscription.payment_method or '').strip() or None

body_method = (request.payment_method or '').strip() or None
if locked_method and body_method and body_method != locked_method:
    raise HTTPException(status_code=400, detail=(
        f"This invoice is paid with {locked_method}. "
        "The payment method of a subscription never changes."
    ))

payment_method = locked_method or body_method or 'stripe'
```

Three consequences:

1. the invoice's method wins; the subscription's method is the fallback; the
   body is the last resort — and it may be **empty**;
2. a body that names another method is a **400**;
3. the HitPay branch creates the payment request with **that** method and
   stores the id on the invoice (`invoice.hitpay_payment_request_id`) — that
   id is what the reconciliation poll uses.

The answer the site gets:

```json
{
  "message": "Scan the code or use Touch 'n Go to pay",
  "requires_payment": true,
  "payment": {
    "provider": "hitpay", "method": "hitpay_duitnow",
    "payment_request_id": "a320f131-…",
    "qr_code": "https://api.sandbox.hit-pay.com/mock/payment/…/qr?…",
    "qr_code_expiry": null, "direct_link": null,
    "amount": 80.0, "currency": "MYR",
    "invoice_id": 7, "invoice_number": "INV-20261102-…"
  }
}
```

### 16.5 The subscribe + the upgrade — the QR at the click

In `app/routes/subscription.py` the HitPay branch (line ~446):

1. the invoice is created **in MYR** (`resolve_price_safe` → the ringgit
   price of the chosen plan);
2. the payment request is created **right there** (`generate_qr=True`);
3. the payment request id is stored on the invoice;
4. the subscription stays **pending** — the access does not open until the
   webhook (or the poll) confirms the payment.

The answer:

```json
{
  "id": 5, "status": "pending", "payment_status": "pending",
  "payment_method": "hitpay_duitnow",
  "requires_payment": true,
  "payment": { "…": "the QR, as above" }
}
```

If the gateway does not answer, the subscription **and** the invoice are
deleted again — we do not leave a half-created subscription behind.

### 16.6 The webhook — the four checks

`POST /api/webhooks/hitpay/{event_type}` — the actual order:

| # | The check | What it stops |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **the signature** — `verify_signature(raw_body, header)` — the HMAC-SHA256 of the **raw** body with the salt, compared with `compare_digest` | a forged "paid" |
| 2 | **the event id** — `{event_type}:{payment_request_id}` in `hitpay_webhook_events` | HitPay's retries doing the work twice |
| 3 | **the amount and the currency** — the invoice's own numbers | a payment of the wrong size settling the wrong invoice |
| 4 | **the invoice** — the payment request's reference is our invoice id | a payment landing on nothing |

A webhook we have seen answers **2xx** (so HitPay stops retrying); a webhook
we cannot verify answers **400**. An event that failed (an amount mismatch)
may be delivered again — it is retried, not swallowed.

Then `_hitpay_activate_subscription()` does what the webhook branch of the
existing lifecycle does: the invoice becomes paid, the subscription becomes
active, and the POS is notified.

### 16.7 The renewal cron — MYR + the safety net

**The renewal invoice** (`generate_renewal_invoices`): the amount and the
currency come from `resolve_price_safe` for a HitPay subscription:

```python
invoice_currency = plan.currency_code or 'USD'
if sub.payment_method in ('hitpay_duitnow', 'hitpay_touch_n_go'):
    resolved_amount, resolved_currency = resolve_price_safe(
        db, plan, effective_billing_cycle, sub.payment_method)
    if resolved_amount is None:
        print(f"  ⚠ Subscription {sub.id}: the plan '{plan.name}' has no MYR price …")
        continue
    amount = resolved_amount
    invoice_currency = resolved_currency
```

A HitPay renewal is therefore invoiced in **MYR 80.00** — never USD 20.00.

**The reconciliation poll** (`reconcile_hitpay_payments`): the webhook is the
fast path, this is the pull. For every invoice that waits for a HitPay
payment it asks the gateway what happened, and it applies **the same
checks** as the webhook (the amount, the currency). A paid invoice is
flipped, the subscription is activated, and the event is recorded as
`reconcile:{payment_request_id}` so the webhook that arrives later is
ignored instead of doing the work twice.

It runs **every 10 minutes**:

```cron
*/10 * * * * /usr/bin/python3 …/website_api/scripts/send_notifications.py --reconcile-only >> …/logs/hitpay-reconcile.log 2>&1
```

(the daily 09:00 job still runs the whole notification set).
