**Document Version:** 1.0  
**Created:** 2026-11-02  
**Status:** Design complete — pending implementation  
**Scope:** the website API (the billing system), the site (the customer portal), the renewal cron  
**Related:** `payway-integration.mdx` (Cambodia), `program-changes/subscription-lifecycle/subscription-lifecycle.mdx` (the access rules we just built), `program-changes/customer-portal/subscription-wizard-proposal.mdx` (the wizard UI this plugs into)

## 1. The short answer

Kens asked three questions. Here they are, with what was verified in the
HitPay sandbox **today** (November 2, 2026).

**1. Does HitPay support recurring billing for Touch 'n Go?** ✅ **Yes.**
Touch 'n Go is one of the few wallets with tokenisation + scheduled
auto-charge. Verified: `POST /v1/recurring-billing` with
`payment_methods[]=touch_n_go` and `save_payment_method=true` returned
**201** and a direct link — the customer authorises the wallet in the
browser, and HitPay charges it off-session later.

**2. Does HitPay allow our SaaS POS to use it for the Malaysian
customers?** ✅ **Yes — but there are two different models, and what was
described is the simpler one.**

| | **Model A — Ideazes is the merchant** | **Model B — the platform model** |
|---|---|---|
| Who pays | the **restaurant** pays **us** the subscription | the **diner** pays the **restaurant** through our POS |
| Who owns the HitPay account | **Ideazes** (one account, verified once) | **every restaurant** (a sub-merchant each, verified by HitPay) |
| What we build | a payment method in the customer portal | a sub-merchant onboarding flow + OAuth or key collection + a Platform Key |
| The money | comes to us | goes straight to each restaurant's own bank account |
| Effort | small — a provider, a webhook, a country rule | large — a business-registration funnel for every tenant |
| **What Kens described** | ✅ **this one** | a later, separate decision |

HitPay's own words for Model B: *"Platforms that serve other businesses —
CRMs, booking systems, marketplaces, agency tools, and vertical SaaS
products — often need to offer payment acceptance to their customers without
holding a payment institution licence themselves."* HitPay holds the
licences (BNM in Malaysia), does the KYB/KYC and processes the payments.

**Model A needs none of that.** The restaurant is simply a customer paying
an invoice. We need our **own** HitPay account, verified as a Malaysian
business.

**3. What are the catches?** Three, and they decide the design:

- **DuitNow cannot renew itself.** DuitNow is **not recurring** and **not
  refundable**. Touch 'n Go is the only Malaysian wallet that can auto-charge
  a subscription. A DuitNow subscription is a manual payment every cycle.
- **Where *we* are onboarded decides what we can do.** TnG recurring exists
  **only for a merchant onboarded in Malaysia** (BNM licence, MYR
  settlement, MYR only). A Singapore account gets TnG only as a
  cross-border QR — **without recurring** → no auto-renewal at all.
- **The currency.** Our plans are priced in **USD** by default
  (`subscription_plans.currency_code = 'USD'`), while TnG and DuitNow settle
  in **MYR**. The amount charged must come from the **MYR row of
  `plan_prices`** — otherwise we would charge $10 into a MYR wallet.

## 2. What HitPay is

HitPay is an omnichannel payments platform for Asia-Pacific, with **direct
payment licences** in its core markets — **MAS (Singapore), BNM (Malaysia),
BSP (Philippines)** — and it acquires the local methods itself.

For a Malaysian merchant account:

| Capability | Detail |
|---|---|
| Licence | **BNM Merchant Acquirer** |
| Card (online) | HitPay direct + Stripe |
| Local APMs | FPX, DuitNow Online Banking, DuitNow QR, Touch 'n Go eWallet, GrabPay, ShopeePay, MAE, Atome, SPayLater, Grab PayLater, WeChat Pay, Alipay |
| Recurring | **Cards, TnG, ShopeePay, GrabPay, ZaloPay** |
| Settlement | MYR, to our own Malaysian bank account |

Two facts from the docs worth keeping in mind:

- *"The market you pick is where **your business is onboarded**. The payment
  methods listed are where your customers pay from."* — so onboarding in
  Malaysia is what unlocks TnG recurring for our Malaysian restaurants.
- *"All alternative payment methods (APMs, regional QR rails, recurring) are
  always HitPay-acquired direct."* — HitPay is the acquirer, not a reseller
  of someone else's licence.

## 3. The two models in detail

### 3.1 Model A — Ideazes is the merchant (what we build now)

```
The restaurant (the customer)  ──pays──►  IDEAZES (the merchant)
                                          │  collects via HitPay
                                          ▼
                                    HitPay → our MYR bank account
```

- One HitPay account, verified once, owned by Ideazes.
- The restaurant pays the **subscription** with TnG or DuitNow.
- HitPay's BNM licence covers us; we do not touch the licensing.
- The access rules stay exactly as they are today: the invoice is paid → the
  access opens (`subscription-lifecycle.mdx`).

### 3.2 Model B — the platform model (later, if we want it)

```
The diner  ──pays──►  IDEAZES (the platform)  ──►  HitPay  ──►  the restaurant's bank account
                            │                    (BNM licence)
                            └── commission (with a Platform Key)
```

HitPay's Platforms model:

- Every restaurant is a **sub-merchant**: their **own** HitPay account, their
  **own** business verification, their **own** payouts. *"Funds are not
  pooled through the platform."*
- Two ways to reach their accounts:
  - **Option 1 — their API key** (they paste it into our UI). *"Not
    recommended"* — it is full access to their account, non-expiring, and
    rotating it breaks their own integration.
  - **Option 2 — OAuth** ("Connect your HitPay account"). The clean one,
    with scoped tokens they can revoke. **It is in beta**, and webhooks /
    virtual accounts / static QR / terminals still need a merchant key.
- **The add-on — the Platform Key** (`X-PLATFORM-KEY`): asks HitPay to
  enable our account as a Platform account. It unlocks **commissions** (a
  platform-wide rate or a fixed amount per transaction) and **unified
  webhooks** (all the sub-merchants' charge events land on **our** webhook
  endpoint, not theirs). Enabling it needs an email to
  `support@hit-pay.com`.

**Model B is a separate document when we want it.** It changes what a
"restaurant" is in our data model, and it puts a business-registration
funnel in front of every new tenant.

### 3.3 Why Model A is enough for now

The thing Kens asked for is *"the customer portal for subscription
payment"* — the restaurant paying us. That is Model A. Model B is about the
**diner** paying the restaurant, which we do not do yet (the POS records the
payment, it does not settle it).

## 4. The payment methods by country (the rule, made executable)

Kens's rule:

| The customer's country of operations | The payment methods offered |
|---|---|
| **Malaysia** | Stripe, Pay Later, **DuitNow**, **Touch 'n Go** |
| **Cambodia** | PayWay (not integrated yet), Stripe, Pay Later |
| **Anything else** | Stripe only |

### 4.1 One function, two callers

The rule must live in **one** place, and it must be enforced by the server:

```python
# website_api/app/services/payment_methods.py  (NEW)

STripe = "stripe"
PAY_LATER = "pay_later"
HITPAY_TNG = "hitpay_touch_n_go"
HITPAY_DUITNOW = "hitpay_duitnow"
PAYWAY = "payway"

COUNTRY_METHODS = {
    "MY": [STRIPE, PAY_LATER, HITPAY_TNG, HITPAY_DUITNOW],
    "KH": [PAYWAY, STRIPE, PAY_LATER],
}

def available_payment_methods(country: str | None) -> list[str]:
    """The payment methods a customer in this country may pay with.

    The browser asks what it may show. The API decides what it accepts.
    """
    if not country:
        return [STRIPE, PAY_LATER]          # unknown country → the safe pair
    return COUNTRY_METHODS.get(country.upper(), [STRIPE])
```

- **The site** calls it (through the API) to render the options.
- **The API** calls it to **accept** the choice. *"The client never chooses
  the tenant"* — the same rule as the tenant claim: the browser's choice is
  a claim, the server decides. A request with
  `payment_method: "hitpay_touch_n_go"` from a Cambodian customer is
  **rejected**, not silently downgraded.

### 4.2 The country is already there

`website_customers.country` exists ✓ and is set at signup
(`website_api/app/routes/auth.py` → the signup model). Today:

| Customer | Country |
|---|---|
| Demo Restaurant Group | *(null)* |
| VARMAN | Cambodia |
| AES Restaurant | Cambodia |

**The gap:** the country is **never set by a customer** — it is set by
whoever creates the account (the admin, or the signup default). It needs to
be a first-class field of the signup and of the portal (a customer can
change it), and the **null** case must be decided deliberately
(the safe pair: Stripe + Pay Later).

### 4.3 What "Pay Later" means here (it is not a gateway)

`pay_later` in our system is **our own invoice flow** — no gateway, no
auto-charge, the customer pays by bank transfer and the invoice is marked
paid by us. It is not a HitPay method. (The HitPay docs mention
`grabpay_paylater` and `shopee_pay_later` — those are *different* things:
Buy-Now-Pay-Later credit products. If we ever want those, they are new
methods, not our `pay_later`.)

## 5. The Malaysian methods, in detail

The matrix from HitPay's payment-method reference (the Malaysia tab):

| Method | Code | Online | In-person | **Recurring** | **Refunds** | Currency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Touch 'n Go eWallet** | `touch_n_go` | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | MYR |
| **DuitNow** | `duitnow` | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | MYR |
| Cards | `card` | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | MYR |
| GrabPay | `grabpay_direct` | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | MYR |
| ShopeePay | `shopee_pay` | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | MYR |
| FPX | `fpx` | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ | MYR |
| MayBank QR | `razer_maybankqr` | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ | MYR |

And the save-payment-method reference is stricter about Touch 'n Go:
**"Touch 'n Go | Malaysia | MYR"** — the saved wallet is a Malaysian,
ringgit-only thing. Cross-border, TnG is listed as **not recurring**.

### 5.1 Touch 'n Go — the auto-renewal method

- The customer authorises the wallet **in the browser** through a direct
  link (`generate_direct_link=true` → `direct_link.direct_link_url`). No
  redirect to a HitPay page, no app switch we cannot control.
- HitPay stores the wallet token and fires `recurring_billing.method_attached`.
- From then on we charge it off-session: `POST /v1/charge/recurring-billing/{id}`.
- **MYR only.** The amount is ours to choose each time (the save-payment-method
  API is the "variable amount" one — an upgrade or a proration is just a
  different number).

### 5.2 DuitNow — the one-off QR

- We generate the QR (`generate_qr=true` → `qr_code_data.qr_code` +
  `qr_code_expiry`) and show it in the portal. The customer scans it with
  the DuitNow app.
- `payment_request.completed` fires → the invoice is paid → the access opens.
- **No auto-charge, ever.** And **no refunds** — a DuitNow payment cannot be
  refunded through HitPay. That is a customer-facing caveat we must show
  before the customer pays.

### 5.3 The consequence: what a DuitNow subscription is

A DuitNow customer must pay **every cycle by hand**. The honest design:

- **DuitNow = the first payment + a saved invoice.** After the first QR
  payment, the subscription continues on our existing **invoice** machinery
  (the same as `pay_later`), with the DuitNow QR shown on the invoice page
  each cycle. The customer is reminded; if they do not pay, the normal
  grace rules apply (14 days) and then the access closes — exactly as
  designed in `subscription-lifecycle.mdx`.
- **The recommendation to the customer:** *"Pay with Touch 'n Go to switch
  on automatic renewal."* TnG is the method that makes the subscription
  hands-off, and the upgrade path is one click (the saved wallet already
  exists — the flow is identical to the one in section 6.1).

## 6. The flows

### 6.1 Touch 'n Go — the first payment and the saved wallet

```
The portal (the customer picks "Touch 'n Go")
   ↓
POST /api/subscriptions/  {payment_method: "hitpay_touch_n_go", ...}
   ↓ the website API
   POST https://api.hit-pay.com/v1/recurring-billing
        name, customer_email, amount (the MYR price), currency=myr,
        save_payment_method=true, payment_methods[]=touch_n_go,
        generate_direct_link=true, redirect_url=…/portal/billing?hitpay=return,
        reference = the subscription id
   ↓ 201 → direct_link.direct_link_url
   ↓
The browser shows the button → the customer authorises the TnG wallet
   ↓
HitPay → recurring_billing.method_attached (webhook)
   ↓ we store the token → the subscription is active, the auto-renewal is on
```

The **first** charge: either we charge immediately after the wallet is
attached (`POST /v1/charge/recurring-billing/{id}`) or we let the first
renewal date be the first charge (the "delayed first charge" the
save-payment-method API is built for — the right choice when the customer
came from a trial).

### 6.2 The renewal (the cron)

```
The daily cron (the same one that writes the Stripe invoices)
   ↓
For each subscription due for renewal, with a saved TnG wallet:
   POST /v1/charge/recurring-billing/{recurring_billing_id}
        amount = the current MYR price, currency=myr, reference = the invoice id
   ↓
charge.created  → the invoice is paid → the period extends (the same path
                  Stripe takes today — the access rules do not change)
charge.failed   → the invoice stays unpaid → the normal grace rules apply
```

### 6.3 DuitNow — a one-off payment

```
The portal shows the QR (the invoice page, or the subscribe button)
   ↓
POST /v1/payment-requests  {amount, currency=myr, payment_methods[]=duitnow,
                            generate_qr=true, reference = the invoice id}
   ↓ 201 → qr_code_data.qr_code (+ the expiry)
   ↓
The customer scans → payment_request.completed → the invoice is paid ✓
```

### 6.4 The upgrade and the cancellation

- **The upgrade** (a plan change with a prorated amount): the
  save-payment-method API is the *variable amount* one, so an upgrade is
  simply another charge for the difference ✓ (the same simplification the
  PayWay design chose with `MITU_FLEX`).
- **The cancellation**: the customer cancels in the portal → the
  subscription is cancelled → **the saved wallet is detached**
  (`recurring_billing.method_detached`) so we never charge a customer who
  left. The existing cancel route already knows how to tell a provider
  (`payment_providers.py`, phase 4 of the lifecycle work) — HitPay slots in
  there.

## 7. The data model

### 7.1 New table — `hitpay_payment_methods` (the saved wallets)

The saved wallet is **not** a card: it has no brand, no last4 and no expiry.
The existing `payment_methods` table is Stripe-shaped
(`stripe_payment_method_id`, `brand`, `last4`, `exp_month`, `exp_year`), so
the wallets get their own table.

| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `id` | int | |
| `customer_id` | int | the website customer |
| `recurring_billing_id` | str | HitPay's recurring billing id — **the handle we charge with** |
| `payment_method` | str | `touch_n_go`, `shopee_pay`, `grabpay_direct`, `card` |
| `currency` | str | `myr` |
| `status` | str | `attached` / `detached` / `expired` |
| `is_default` | bool | |
| `attached_at` / `detached_at` | datetime | |
| `created_at` / `updated_at` | datetime | |

The **wallet token itself stays at HitPay** — we store the handle, not the
credential. (A wallet token in our database would be a liability; HitPay's
recurring billing is designed so we never hold it.)

### 7.2 Modified — `subscriptions`

| Column | Notes |
|---|---|
| `payment_provider` | `'hitpay'` (the column phase 4 of the lifecycle work added) |
| `payment_method` | `'hitpay_touch_n_go'` / `'hitpay_duitnow'` (our own vocabulary, not HitPay's) |
| `hitpay_recurring_billing_id` | the handle for the auto-charge |
| `hitpay_payment_method_id` | the saved wallet (the row in 7.1) |
| `hitpay_charge_id` | the last charge (for the reconciliation) |

### 7.3 Modified — `invoices`

| Column | Notes |
|---|---|
| `hitpay_payment_request_id` | the one-off QR payment request |
| `hitpay_charge_id` | the auto-charge |
| `currency` | **MYR** for a HitPay invoice (today the invoice takes `plan.currency_code`, which is USD — see section 12) |

### 7.4 New table — `hitpay_webhook_events` (the idempotency)

HitPay retries a webhook that is not answered with a 2xx. The same event can
therefore arrive twice, and it must not charge twice or extend the period
twice. Every webhook is recorded **before** it is processed:

| Column | Notes |
|---|---|
| `event_id` | the HitPay event id — **unique** |
| `event_type` | `charge.created`, … |
| `received_at` | |
| `processed_at` | |
| `payload` | the raw JSON (for the audit) |

A duplicate insert → the second delivery is ignored (and answered 200).

## 8. The API integration

### 8.1 The environment variables

```bash
# website_api/.env
HITPAY_API_KEY=<the sandbox key>          # the production key in production
HITPAY_API_URL=https://api.sandbox.hit-pay.com   # https://api.hit-pay.com in production
HITPAY_SALT=<the webhook salt>            # the signature secret of the webhook endpoint
```

**The credentials are server-side only.** They go in `.env` (never in git,
never in the browser, never in the site's environment variables — the site
talks to **our** API, our API talks to HitPay).

### 8.2 The client — `website_api/app/services/hitpay.py` (NEW)

A thin, synchronous `httpx`/`requests` client:

- `X-BUSINESS-API-KEY: <key>` on every request, `X-Requested-With:
  XMLHttpRequest`, `Content-Type: application/x-www-form-urlencoded`.
- The **base URL comes from the environment** — the same code runs against
  the sandbox and production.
- Every method returns a small result object: `{ok, id, raw, error}` — the
  errors never raise into the caller's face (the billing flow must not
  break because a gateway hiccuped — the same rule the Stripe provider
  follows).

### 8.3 The provider — `HitPayProvider`

The lifecycle work (phase 4) built the interface
(`website_api/app/services/payment_providers.py`):

```python
provider = get_provider(sub.payment_provider)   # stripe | hitpay | payway
provider.cancel_at_period_end(sub)
```

`HitPayProvider` implements the same three methods
(`cancel_at_period_end`, `resume`, `is_active`) with HitPay's calls
(detach the wallet / re-attach / read the recurring billing's status). The
subscription code never talks to HitPay directly.

### 8.4 The signature — the salt

The webhook carries `Hitpay-Signature: HMAC-SHA256 of the raw JSON body,
using that webhook endpoint's salt`.

```python
import hmac, hashlib

def verify(raw_body: bytes, signature: str, salt: str) -> bool:
    expected = hmac.new(salt.encode(), raw_body, hashlib.sha256).hexdigest()
    return hmac.compare_digest(expected, (signature or "").strip())
```

Three rules:

- The **raw** body — the parsed-and-re-serialised JSON has a different
  signature. Read the body as bytes, verify, *then* parse.
- `hmac.compare_digest` — a constant-time comparison.
- A wrong signature → **400, nothing else happens**.

## 9. The webhook

### 9.1 The endpoint

```
POST https://apisite.ideazes.com/api/webhooks/hitpay
```

HitPay's rules: the URL must be **HTTPS** and resolve to a **public**
destination — *"Loopback and private network addresses are rejected."*
`apisite.ideazes.com` is public ✓. The endpoint must be registered in the
HitPay dashboard (Developers → API Keys → the webhook URL) or via
`POST /v1/webhook-events`, and it must be **the same URL for production and
a different one for the sandbox** (the sandbox sends to the test endpoint).

### 9.2 The events we listen to

| Event | What it means for us |
|---|---|
| `payment_request.completed` | a DuitNow QR was paid → the invoice is paid |
| `payment_request.failed` | the QR payment failed → the invoice stays unpaid |
| `recurring_billing.method_attached` | the customer authorised the TnG wallet → the auto-renewal is on |
| `recurring_billing.method_detached` | the wallet was detached → no more auto-charges |
| `recurring_billing.subscription_updated` | the subscription changed (active, cancelled, paused, expired) → mirror it |
| `charge.created` | an auto-charge succeeded → the invoice is paid |
| `charge.failed` | an auto-charge failed → the invoice stays unpaid → the grace rules |
| `charge.updated` | a refund happened (HitPay's cards and TnG — **not** DuitNow) |

### 9.3 The allowlist

If the origin firewall filters, allow:

| Environment | IPs |
|---|---|
| Production | `3.1.13.32`, `52.77.254.34` |
| Sandbox | `54.179.156.147` |

The site sits behind Cloudflare → the **origin** (Caddy) must let those IPs
through, and the endpoint must not be blocked by the WAF.

## 10. The renewal processor (the cron)

The existing daily cron (`website_api/scripts/send_notifications.py`) already
writes the invoices and tells the POS backend. HitPay adds one step:

```
For each subscription due for renewal:
    if the provider is hitpay and a saved wallet exists:
        charge = hitpay.charge_recurring(sub, amount=the MYR price)
        if charge.ok  → the invoice is marked paid by the webhook
        else          → the invoice stays unpaid → the normal grace rules
    if the provider is hitpay and there is NO saved wallet (a DuitNow customer):
        → nothing to charge → the invoice is sent (the QR is on the invoice page)
```

**The rule that matters:** the webhook marks the invoice paid (the gateway
is the source of truth), exactly as Stripe does today. The cron never marks
an invoice paid because *it* decided to.

## 12. The currency — the landmine

Today:

- `subscription_plans.currency_code = 'USD'` (Starter/Basic/Pro) with a
  `plan_prices` table that already holds **MYR** rows:
  Starter 0/0, Basic **MYR 40 / 400**, Pro **MYR 80 / 800**, and a fourth
  plan at **MYR 200 / 2000**.
- The Stripe checkout hard-codes `'currency': 'usd'`.
- The invoice takes `plan.currency_code or "MYR"`.

So the prices are **already prepared for ringgit** — but nothing selects the
row by the customer's country yet. Before the first HitPay charge:

1. The **amount** must come from `plan_prices` for the customer's country
   (`MY` → the MYR row), not from the plan's USD default.
2. The **invoice currency** must be MYR for a HitPay invoice, and the
   portal must show the ringgit price (a customer in Kuala Lumpur must not
   be quoted $20).
3. The **Stripe** prices stay in USD for the customers who pay by card
   today — unless we decide that MY customers should see ringgit for
   Stripe as well. **That is a pricing decision for Kens, not a technical
   one** (and it changes what the existing customers are charged).

## 13. The error handling

| Situation | What we do |
|---|---|
| The gateway is down | the charge fails → the invoice stays unpaid → **the normal grace rules** (14 days) apply. The customer is never locked out by our outage. The access rules already survive a late payment ✓ |
| The customer refuses the wallet authorisation | no `method_attached` → the subscription stays unpaid → the invoice is sent (a DuitNow QR) |
| The charge fails (no balance, the wallet was closed) | `charge.failed` → the invoice stays unpaid → the grace → a reminder email |
| The webhook is delivered twice | the `hitpay_webhook_events` insert is unique → the second one is ignored |
| The signature does not match | **400**, nothing happens |
| The customer wants a refund | **cards and TnG can be refunded** (`charge.updated`); **DuitNow cannot** — the portal must say so before the payment |

## 15. The open questions

1. **Where is Ideazes onboarded?** This is the first decision, because it
   decides whether Touch 'n Go can renew itself at all. **Malaysia** (BNM,
   MYR, TnG recurring ✅) or **Singapore** (MAS, SGD, TnG cross-border
   **without** recurring ❌). Our customers are in Malaysia and Cambodia and
   the prices are already in MYR → **Malaysia is the answer** — but it needs
   the business verification.
2. **Do we want Model B** (the POS collecting the diners' payments for the
   restaurants)? It is a much bigger product decision, and the platform
   programme needs an email to HitPay. Not needed for the subscription.
3. **The DuitNow customer experience** — a manual payment every cycle, and
   no refunds. Do we show DuitNow at all for the **annual** plan, or only
   as the "pay this invoice now" method (with the recommendation to switch
   to TnG for the auto-renewal)?
4. **The pricing** — do the Malaysian customers see MYR for **Stripe** as
   well (a pricing change for the existing customers), or only for HitPay?
5. **PayWay (Cambodia)** — the same document already exists
   (`payway-integration.mdx`); the country rule puts it next to Stripe and
   Pay Later for Cambodia, and it is "not integrated yet".
6. **Does HitPay's business verification accept our structure** (Ideazes as
   a Malaysian entity selling SaaS to restaurants)? Their Sales team
   (`sales@hit-pay.com`) is the right address for it.
