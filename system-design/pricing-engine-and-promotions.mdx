# The Pricing Engine and the Promotions

> The money of an order is the server's. The React app and the customer app show the numbers the
> server gives them — they never work them out themselves.

Written Nov 9, 2026, after the happy hour of Demo Cafe 1 (Kens SS17, Phnom Penh) charged a guest
for a $55.80 basket the wrong way.

---

## The short version

| | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| The tax | on the **gross** subtotal | on the amount the guest **pays** |
| The discount | computed by the client, by the server, and by both | **one** source, and the order says which |
| The happy hour | ran on the **server's** clock | runs on the **outlet's** clock |
| A promotion with `required_item_ids = 'null'` | silently skipped, forever | applies to the whole cart |
| Two drinks | charged as four | charged as two |
| A bundle | the components at the menu's price | the components minus the saving = the bundle price |

---

## The five bugs

### 1. The engine read the server's clock

`datetime.now()` on the server is **Asia/Kuala_Lumpur (UTC+8)**. The branch in Phnom Penh is
**UTC+7** — an hour behind. A happy hour of `14:00-18:00` in Phnom Penh was `15:00-19:00` on the
server, and the server's days are the same days, so the bug only showed itself at the edges of
the window.

**Now:** every window and every weekday of a promotion is read in the **outlet's** timezone
(`backend/app/core/date_window.py` → `outlet_timezone`, `local_moment`).

### 2. The JSON columns of SQLite arrived as text

`required_item_ids` and `required_category_ids` are JSON columns, but SQLite handed the engine the
raw text: `'null'` and `'[1, 7, 10]'`.

`set('null')` is `{n, u, l}` — three letters, and **not** empty. So a promotion whose
`required_item_ids` was `'null'` asked for "an item called n, u or l", found none, and skipped the
promotion **forever**. The happy hour of Demo Cafe 1 never ran.

**Now:** every JSON column is read through `as_list()` / `as_ids()`
(`backend/app/services/promotion_service.py`), which understands a list, the raw text and `'null'`.

### 3. The quantity was multiplied twice

The engine multiplied `price x quantity`, and the callers handed it the **line total**. A guest who
ordered two drinks was charged for four.

**Now:** the engine reads **unit** prices (`price` is one unit, `quantity` is how many) and
multiplies exactly once. The callers (`order_service.py`, `routes/promotions.py`) send the unit
price.

### 4. The tax was on the gross, and the discount was subtracted by hand

`add_items_to_order` re-taxed the subtotal **before** the discount and then added the tax to the
total without taking the discount off. The receipt did not add up.

**Now:** the tax is computed on the amount the guest pays — the subtotal minus the discount — by
`compute_totals()` in `backend/app/services/order_pricing.py`. The total comes from the tax
service, so a price that already carries the tax keeps carrying it.

### 5. Nobody knew where the discount came from

The order had a `discount` but no idea whether it was a voucher, a promotion or something the
staff gave at the till. So a repricing had to guess, and it guessed wrong.

**Now:** the order has a `discount_source` (`voucher` | `promotion` | `manual` | `none`).

## The rules

### The order says where its discount came from

| `discount_source` | What it means | What happens on a reprice |
|---|---|---|
| `voucher` | a validated voucher | the voucher's amount is recomputed — it is **never** redeemed again |
| `promotion` | the promotions engine | the promotions are re-evaluated at `order.created_at`, **ignoring the usage caps** |
| `manual` | the staff gave it at the till | the amount is kept |
| `none` | there is no discount | — |

The orders of before the fix have no source. It is inferred (`infer_source()`):

1. a `voucher_code` means a **voucher**,
2. a promotion that gives the very same amount (within a cent) at the moment the order was placed
   means a **promotion**,
3. a guest's discount came from the server, so it is a **promotion**,
4. anything else was a discount the staff gave: a **manual** one, and the amount is kept.

A reprice never takes money away: if the promotion that gave an order its discount is gone, the
amount the guest was charged is kept.

### The precedence is strict

**voucher > staff manual discount > best promotion > nothing.**

A staff discount of **zero** does **not** switch the promotions off — the staff who gives nothing
gets the promotion the guest would get. A staff discount **over zero** suppresses the promotion
(what the staff gives is what the guest gets).

### The price of an order is locked when it is placed

The promotions of an existing order are re-evaluated at **`order.created_at`**, never at the moment
of the repricing, and the usage caps are ignored while repricing. Otherwise an order would change
its price every time somebody else used the promotion.

:::info
**This is a decision, not an accident.** An order placed at 13:00 keeps its 13:00 price even if a
drink is added at 15:00 inside the happy hour. If Kens wants the other rule ("the price follows the
moment of the change"), it is one line in `order_pricing.reprice_order()` — the `at` argument.
:::

### The usage counters

| The promotion's field | The counter | The table |
|---|---|---|
| `max_uses` | the global counter | `promotions.current_uses` |
| `max_uses_per_outlet` | the counter of **this** branch | `promotion_outlet_usage(promotion_id, outlet_id, uses)` |

A promotion counts **once**, when it is really applied at the creation of an order. A preview
(`/api/promotions/calculate`) never counts. The table is created by the migration in
`backend/app/main.py` (it did not exist before).

### The promotion rules

| The type | The rule |
|---|---|
| `time_based` | the window and the weekdays of the **outlet**; the items are narrowed by `required_item_ids` / `required_category_ids` when there are any |
| `percentage` | a share of the eligible items — **all** of them when there is no category filter |
| `bundle` | a **complete set**: every component has to be in the cart; the number of bundles is the smallest count among the components; the saving is `(the set - the bundle price) x the number of bundles` |
| `bogo` | one unit of the cheapest eligible item is free — and the cart has to hold **at least two eligible units** |
| `fixed_discount` | an amount, never more than the subtotal it comes from |

The discount is never negative and never more than the subtotal it is taken from.

### The tax

The tax is on the **amount the guest pays**. For outlet 1 (VAT 8%, not included):

```
subtotal 55.80 - discount 11.16 = 44.64
44.64 x 8% = 3.57
44.64 + 3.57 = 48.21
```

## The API

### `POST /api/promotions/calculate?outlet_id=<id>`

The server's answer to "what is this cart worth?". The body is either
`{"items": [...], "voucher_code": "..."}` or the bare list of the items (the old shape, still
accepted).

```json
{
  "subtotal": 55.8, "discount": 11.16, "after_discount": 44.64,
  "tax": 3.57, "total": 48.21,
  "tax_breakdown": { "...": "..." },
  "best_discount": 11.16,
  "best_promotion": { "id": 4, "name": "Happy Hour Special", "badge_text": "20% OFF" },
  "discount_breakdown": [ { "promotion_id": 4, "name": "...", "discount": 11.16 } ],
  "discount_source": "promotion",
  "voucher_error": null,
  "items": [ { "id": 6, "price": 22.9, "quantity": 1, "category_id": 2, "line_total": 22.9 } ]
}
```

- A **guest** may not post a price — the menu's price is the price, and an item the menu does not
  know is not in the cart.
- A **bundle** may arrive as one line (`bundle_<promotion id>`); the server expands it into its
  components at the menu's prices. The expansion is idempotent: the components carry the real menu
  item ids.
- A **voucher** is checked here but **never** redeemed here — it is redeemed once, when the
  order is created. A refusal comes back with a **code** as well as a sentence
  (`voucher_error_code`: `not_found` · `used` · `not_yet` · `expired` · `other_member` ·
  `other_tenant` · `min_spend`) and, for the minimum spend, the amount
  (`voucher_error_amount`) — the guest's language is not the server's, so the client translates
  the code. When the server refuses, the guest app takes the voucher off the cart and asks the
  server again: the money is always the server's.

### `GET /api/promotions/prices?outlet_id=<id>`

The price of every menu item when a promotion is running. The staff order screen shows the price
the guest pays — the menu price with the promotion of the moment on it.

```json
[ { "menu_item_id": 1, "price": 3.0, "promo_price": 2.4, "discount": 0.6,
    "promotion_id": 4, "promotion_name": "Happy Hour Special",
    "badge_text": "20% OFF", "badge_color": "red" } ]
```

- It is **one unit** of the item, priced the way the engine prices a cart: the same rules, the same
  caps, the same clock (the outlet's own).
- A **bundle** and a **BOGO** need more than one item, so they leave the single price alone — the
  bundle price belongs to the **cart**, not to one tile of the menu.
- The **server** owns the money: the tile shows what the server says, and the cart still sends the
  **menu's** price to the server. The two agree to the cent — the tiles of a cart add up to the
  server's `after_discount` (2 × $2.40 + $10.32 = $15.12 = the cart's `after_discount`).

### The permissions

| Who | May |
|---|---|
| the customer app (`customer_app_key`) | **read** the promotions of a branch and ask for the price of a cart |
| the staff (the session, the role `staff`) | **read** the promotions of their branch |
| the manager and the admin | **write** the promotions |

The **prices** of the menu are a **staff** route: it asks for `staff:promotions:read`. The
customer app's key is public (it ships in the PWA bundle) and it carries `promotions:read`, so the
plain permission would not stop it — the `staff:` namespace does, and the `staff` role carries it.

A tenant may not read another tenant's promotions (`require_outlet_access`).

## What changed

### The backend — 7 files changed, 1 new

**`backend/app/services/order_pricing.py`** — **new, 334 lines.** The money of an order in one
place, so there is one rule and not five:

| The function | What it does |
|---|---|
| `compute_totals(db, outlet_id, subtotal, discount)` | the tax on the amount the guest **pays**; the total comes from the tax service |
| `resolve_discount(db, outlet_id, items, voucher=..., manual_discount=..., at=...)` | the strict precedence; returns `{discount, source, promotion, breakdown}` |
| `reprice_order(db, order, at=None, lines=None)` | reprices from the order's own `discount_source`, at `order.created_at`, ignoring the usage caps |
| `infer_source(db, order, moment, lines)` | the legacy inference for the orders without a source |
| `order_lines(db, order)` | the order's cart in the engine's shape (the unit price = the price + the add-ons) |
| `subtotal_of(lines)` | what the cart is worth — the stored line totals |

**`backend/app/services/promotion_service.py`** — **rewritten (+557 lines).** The engine:

| The function | What it does |
|---|---|
| `parse_time(value)` | a time from the DB or from a string — the route and the engine read a time the **same** way now |
| `as_list(value)` / `as_ids(value)` | the JSON columns of SQLite: a list, the raw text (`'[0, 1, 2]'`) or `'null'` (which means **nothing**) |
| `promotion_is_running(db, promo, outlet_id, at=None, ignore_usage=False)` | is it running — the outlet's clock, the usage caps |
| `outlet_uses(...)` / `record_usage(...)` | the per-outlet counter and the counting (both counters move) |
| `calculate_best_discount(db, outlet_id, items, at=None, ignore_usage=False)` | **the same name and the same return shape** as before — `(discount, promotion, breakdown)` — so the old callers still work |
| `promotion_prices(db, outlet_id, items, at=None)` | **new.** The price of every menu item when a promotion covers it — one unit, priced by the engine |

**`backend/app/services/order_service.py`** — **114 lines.** The creation and the adding of an
item use the shared pricing. The cart goes to the engine as **unit** prices (the old code sent the
line total, so the engine multiplied it by the quantity again). The discount is resolved once and
the order says where it came from. A voucher is redeemed **once**, here. A promotion counts
**once**, here.

**`backend/app/routes/promotions.py`** — **420 lines.** Every route gained its permission
(`promotions:read` to read, `promotions:write` to write) and `require_outlet_access` (a tenant may
not read another tenant's promotions). `/calculate` was rewritten to the new contract: the bundle
expansion, the guest's prices from the menu, the voucher validated but never redeemed.
`/increment-usage` now moves **both** counters (the global one and the per-outlet one). **`/prices`
is new**: the price of every menu item when a promotion is running — a **staff** route
(`staff:promotions:read`), because the customer app's key is public.

**`backend/app/routes/order.py`** — **45 lines.** The cancelling and the restoring of an item
reprice the order (they used to zero the tax and subtract the discount by hand). `discount_source`
is now in the five order responses, so the client is told too.

**`backend/app/models/models.py`** — **9 lines.** `orders.discount_source`.

**`backend/app/main.py`** — **29 lines.** The migration: the `orders.discount_source` column and
the `promotion_outlet_usage` table (it did not exist).

**`backend/app/middleware/auth.py`** — **11 lines.** `promotions:read` for the customer app key
and for the `staff` role. `promotions:write` stays with the manager and the admin. And
`staff:promotions:read` for the `staff` role — the prices of the menu are a staff route, and the
plain `promotions:read` would not stop the customer app's key.

### The staff order screen shows the promotion (Nov 9, 2026)

The staff order screen and the full screen (the kiosk) show the **price the guest pays**: the
menu price with the promotion of the moment on it, the menu price struck through and the badge of
the promotion (`20% OFF`) beside it. The cart shows the same.

- The prices come from `GET /api/promotions/prices` — the **server** says what the price is.
- They are refreshed **every minute**: the promotions run on the outlet's clock, so the happy hour
  starts and ends while the screen is open.
- The cart keeps the **menu's** price — that is what the server is sent, and the server owns the
  money. The client never works the money out a second time.

### The React staff app — 3 files

**`frontend/src/pages/StaffOrder.jsx`** — the cart asks `POST /api/promotions/calculate`
(debounced 300 ms, with a stale-answer guard: a sequence number and a fingerprint of the cart) and
shows the server's subtotal, discount, tax and total. The old `calculateTax` is only the **offline
fallback**, so the till never freezes. The order payload is **unchanged** — the server re-prices
the order.

**`frontend/src/components/pos/CartPanel.jsx`** — the promotion's price on a cart line (the menu
price struck through) and the discount row (with the promotion's badge)
between the subtotal and the tax. The row was hidden by one line: `display='flex'` followed
immediately by `display='none'`.

**`frontend/src/pages/OrderQueue.jsx`** — the **table tab** threw `ReferenceError: lastStep is not
defined` when a table row was clicked. `TableBillCard` is a component of its own, and it read
`lastStep` — a variable of the **page** above it. The wording now comes in as a prop
(`servedLabel`), the way the other components of the file already did it. The rule: a component of
its own never reaches for the page's variables.

### The customer app — 1 file

**`customer-app/js/app.js`** — four functions. The preview asks the server (and sends the voucher's
code), the cart's subtotal, discount, tax and total come from the answer, a bundle line is
expanded into its components for the preview, and the confirmation shows the **server's** order
total. The discount row had the same one-line bug as the staff app. A voucher the server refuses
comes off the cart, the guest is told why **in their own language** (`cart.voucherRefused` and
`cart.voucherMinSpend`, in en/zh/km) and the server is asked again without it.

## The repair

**Promotion 4 (Happy Hour Special)** — the description said *"20% off all drinks during happy
hour"*, but the promotion has no item and no category filter: it takes 20% off **every** item. The
description now says what it does:
*"20% off all items during happy hour, 14:00-18:00, Monday-Friday"*.

**Order 368 (`ORD-20261109-5830`)** — it was created with **one** item, so the happy hour was 20%
of 22.90 (4.58). A second item was added afterwards, and the old code re-taxed the gross subtotal
(55.80 → 4.46) and added the tax to the total without taking the discount off (60.26). The order
now carries the money it should:

| | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| subtotal | 55.80 | 55.80 |
| discount | 4.58 | **11.16** (20% of 55.80) |
| tax | 4.46 | **3.57** (8% of 44.64) |
| total | 60.26 | **48.21** |
| `discount_source` | (none) | **promotion** |

The order was created at `2026-11-09 08:38:46 UTC` = **15:38:46 in Phnom Penh**, on a **Monday** —
inside the happy hour (14:00-18:00, Monday to Friday).

Only 4 orders of the 333 have a discount at all, and the other 3 add up exactly (their outlet had
no tax back then) — **order 368 was the only broken one.**

## How it is proved

```bash
cd /root/.openclaw/workspace/mydoc/Projects/POS_AI/backend
python3 /root/.openclaw/workspace/scripts/pos_pricing_probe.py      # 58 checks
python3 /root/.openclaw/workspace/scripts/pos_pricing_repair.py     # the repair
```

The probe checks the engine against the database's own truth **and** the running server over HTTP:
the happy hour, the timezone, the weekdays, the raw JSON, the bundle, the quantity, the reprice of
order 368, the creation of an order, the usage caps, the permissions, the tenant's isolation, the
bundle expansion, the bundle order of the guest app and the voucher's refusal (the code, the
minimum spend, a good voucher and a code that does not exist). It cleans up after itself — the promotions and the orders it creates are
deleted again.

## The open questions for Kens

1. **The price is locked when the order is placed.** An order that gets an item later keeps the
   promotion of its first minute. The other rule ("the price follows the change") is one line.
2. **The other 333 orders were not repriced.** Their money adds up (they were not broken), and
   rewriting the history of a ledger is a decision, not a cleanup. Say the word and a reprice of
   every order is one script.
3. **The staff app has no manual discount field yet.** The server honours one (it wins over the
   promotion), but there is no place in the UI to type it.
