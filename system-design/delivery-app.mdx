## 1. The short answer

**One codebase, one deployment, one API — and every tenant gets its own branded
delivery PWA.**

**The principle that decides every scope question:**
> **The tenant owns its delivery operation. We own the commerce.**
> The tenant sets its own charges, allocates its own drivers, manages them its own way
> — **we do not care how**.

**The commercial line** — Grab charges the customer and the merchant a commission and
carries the courier network → **our pitch is the same job at a fraction of the cost:
no commission, no driver marketplace, and no driver GPS on the free tier** — and the
tenant's own drivers are a **feature for them** (they know their customers).
→ **and the customer's map, the route and the ETA are on EVERY plan** (§11.1) → the
thing we sell is the **depth** (the zones, the scheduling, the analytics), never the
map.

**And the plan gating (the question) — mapped onto our REAL plans:**

| Plan | The shape | The delivery features |
|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | 1 outlet · 3 staff | **Delivery** + **Delivery Management** — the order type, the storefront, the fee, the driver roster, the live progress, the customer's map and ETA |
| **Basic** | 3 outlets · 10 staff | **Delivery** + **Delivery Management** (+ the outlets) |
| **Pro** | 5 outlets · 25 staff | the two + **Delivery Zones** (the postcode pricing) |
| **Enterprise** | unlimited | the three + **Delivery Scheduling** + **Delivery Tracking** (the live driver marker + the live ETA) + **Delivery Analytics** |

> **The prices are NOT in this document — they are data.** The catalogue lives in the
> **website API** (`plan_prices` — per currency, per billing cycle, with the
> promotions and their end dates) and it **changes** → **a design document must
> never quote a price**. The **source of truth** is
> `GET /api/plans/published`.
>
> **The snapshot (for the reasoning below only — Nov 5 2026):** the **Starter is
> free** (USD 0 / MYR 0) · Basic USD 10 / MYR 40 · Pro USD 20 / MYR 80
> · Enterprise USD 50 / MYR 200 — **so the delivery on the Starter costs us
> nothing and the free plan is the hook**.

**The split — what is in the core ("Delivery")**
- the delivery **order type** — just another order type (the address, the fee,
  the driver);
- the **white-label storefront** — the branding fields **already exist**
  (`restaurant_name`, `restaurant_logo`, `brand_color`) → it costs us almost nothing
  → **charging for it would fight the adoption**;
- the tenant's **own fee settings** — the whole value is **"no commission"** →
  charging for the fee settings would contradict the pitch;
- the **driver assignment** — two fields on the order;
- the **driver roster** — the tenant maintains its own drivers (a list → a table —
  §3) → **v1.5 changed this**: a roster the tenant *maintains* cannot live in two
  fields on an order;
- the **live progress** — the queue shows where every delivery is (assigned / out
  for delivery / delivered) → **no map needed** → **the free tier gets the progress**;
- **the customer's map, the route and the ETA** — the customer must see where the
  order is and when it lands → **the map costs pennies** (one route request per order)
  and it needs **no driver GPS** → **so it is on every plan** (§11.1);
- **the settlement rule** — a delivery has TWO endings (the handover and the money) and
  they can happen in either order → **one delivery-aware rule** (§4.1) → **the shared
  machine is untouched**.

**What is paid (the ops depth)**
- **Delivery Zones** — the postcode pricing → **Pro**;
- **Delivery Scheduling** — the promised windows → **Enterprise**;
- **Delivery Tracking** — the **live driver marker** and the live ETA → **Enterprise**
  (§11.1) — **the map itself is free** (§11.1);
- **Delivery Analytics** — the delivery revenue, the fee per order → **Enterprise**.

**The adoption argument — the decisive one** — a tenant that switches to us **for the
delivery** but has to pay for it loses the switch → **the basics must be free**
→ **the paid tier is the sophistication** (the zones, the scheduling, the
analytics).

**And the Starter argument — strengthened by the free Starter** — the delivery
costs us **almost nothing on Starter** (one JSON column, the storefront reads the
existing menu) → and the Starter is **free** → so the delivery is a
**customer-acquisition feature** **and a viral loop**: the tenant's own
customers order delivery → the tenant sees the orders arriving → the upgrade →
**the zones, the scheduling and the analytics are the upgrade path** — **the
delivery is the Starter's hook**.

## 2. What we build and what we do NOT

| We build | We do NOT build |
|---|---|
| the **white-label storefront PWA** (the tenant's branding, the menu, the cart, the checkout, the order status) | a **marketplace** — the customers are the tenant's own |
| the **delivery order type** in the core POS | a **driver APP** — the tenant's own drivers (the tracking tier ships a **one-page driver LINK**, not an app — §11.2, §12) |
| the **fee settings** (the flat fee, the minimum, the free-delivery threshold, the radius) | **the live driver marker on the free tier** — the MAP, the route and the ETA are on every plan (§11.1); what costs is the driver's own phone, so the MARKER is the **Enterprise** tier |
| the **driver assignment** (a name + a phone on the order) | a **courier matching engine** |
| the **status flow** — a **parallel leg** on the order (assigned → out for delivery → delivered) **plus one delivery-aware settlement rule** (the handover and the money are two facts — §4.1), **the shared machine untouched** | **commission handling** — we take no commission |
| the **prepay path** (the DuitNow QR / the online payment) | a **delivery schedule/dispatch optimiser** |
| **cash on delivery** (the driver collects — the tenant's choice) | **anything that makes the tenant manage OUR way** |

**The principle restated** — *the tenant manages its drivers by whatever means it likes*
: WhatsApp, a spreadsheet, its own app, a group chat — **we only need to carry the
driver's name and phone on the order** → the customer can call them.

### 3.1 The switch — where it lives, and when it may be ON

**The rule (Kens, Nov 14, 2026) — one sentence, and one correction:**

> the allow-delivery checkbox does not show in the **Order** tab → it belongs to the
> new **Delivery** tab → **and the delivery is OFF until the tenant has subscribed the
> feature and turned it on.**

**The correction — subscribing the feature is not turning the delivery on.** There are
**two gates**, and they sit at two different levels:

| the gate | what it decides | the level | the live truth |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the plan feature** (`delivery`) | does the tenant have the delivery module at all | the **tenant** | the feature row decides it → the Delivery tab's `feature` string is compared **exactly** against the plan's tab names (`Settings.jsx:25` → `useFeatures.jsx:45-47` → `admin.py:1052` returns the row's `tab_name` as the name) |
| **the outlet flag** (`outlet_settings.allow_delivery`) | does this outlet take delivery orders | the **outlet** | `models.py:456` (`default=False`) → off in every preset (`business_presets.py:41`) → the safe fallback is off (`order.py:681`) |

→ **subscribing gives the tenant the TAB, not the delivery** → the flag stays off at
every outlet → **the tenant turns it on, per outlet, inside the Delivery tab** → → a
tenant with three outlets can deliver at one and not at the others → → and because the
checkbox lives in the tab the feature gate controls, **a tenant without the feature
never sees the tab and can never turn the flag on.**

**The flag is not a decoration — three live things read it:**
- `StaffOrder.jsx:775` / `:785` / `:1313` — the staff screen's **Delivery** order-type
  button is gated on it → without it, every outlet would offer delivery;
- `outlet_settings.py:335` — it builds `enabled_types`, the set that decides **which
  order types the `payment_timing` map may name** → the delivery's payment entry only
  exists while delivery is on → **and §4.2's customer choice sits on it**;
- `order.py:709` — the customer app reads it to decide whether to offer the delivery
  type (`customer-app/js/app.js:1367`).

**And the Order tab has a SECOND delivery control — the payment-timing row.**
`Settings.jsx:115-120` builds the per-type list and `:1257` renders it, and the
delivery row is included when `allow_delivery === true` → **so moving only the
checkbox would split the delivery's money settings across two tabs** → **the delivery's
payment-timing row moves to the Delivery tab too** — or the Order tab's row is dropped
and the Delivery tab owns the delivery's money settings (§4.2).

**The default is OFF in the backend — and ON in the POS's own fallback.** The model
(`models.py:456`), the presets (`business_presets.py:41`) and the safe fallback
(`order.py:681`) all say **False**, but the POS's own settings fallback says
**`allow_delivery: true`** (`useSettings.js:46`) → the staff screen shows **Delivery**
before the settings have arrived → **it becomes `false`** → the house rule stands: a
missing row must never sell a service the outlet did not ask for.

**And the flag outlives the subscription.** Nothing resets it on a plan change — there
is no `allow_delivery` anywhere in `website_api/` → **and the two paths that answer
the flag are not plan-gated**: the staff screen reads the outlet's settings
(`StaffOrder.jsx:775`), and the customer app reads the shared settings endpoint
(`order.py:655` — `require_shared_permission`, not `require_plan_feature`) → → **so a
downgraded tenant keeps selling delivery** → → **the effective rule is the feature AND
the flag** → the delivery storefront answers **"is this outlet delivering?"** only when
both are true.

→ **the QR is drawn by the SERVER** — the front end has **no QR library**
(`frontend/package.json` — no qrcode) while the backend has **`qrcode==7.4.2`** and
**`Pillow==10.2.0`** (`backend/requirements.txt:7-8`) → so the QR is a **PNG from a
route**, exactly the payment QR's shape (`/api/pwa/orders/{id}/pay-qr`,
`/api/orders/{id}/pay-online/qr`) → **`GET /api/delivery/outlets/{outlet_id}/qr`** → the
tab shows the image, the URL, a copy button and a download → **and the QR encodes the
tenant's own storefront** (`https://order.ideazes.com/d/<slug>`).

→ **and the QR can carry the tenant's LOGO in the middle** — `Pillow` is installed
(`backend/requirements.txt:8`) → `qrcode`'s PIL factory draws an image QR → **a QR
that goes out to the tenant's own customers on WhatsApp must look like the TENANT, not
like us** → **one flag on the same route** (`?branded=1`).

→ **and the QR is SHARED, not only printed** — the tab's **share row**: **copy the
link**, **WhatsApp** (a plain `wa.me` deep link — there is **no WhatsApp SDK or helper
in the codebase** → nothing to integrate → the link opens WhatsApp with the text
pre-filled), and **download the PNG** → **the tenant's own customer list is the fastest
way to fill the delivery app, and this is the button that does it** (§5).

**The setup flow** (the tenant's side):
1. **Settings → Delivery** → turn it on (the switch moves here);
2. **the fees** (the flat fee, the minimum, the free threshold) and the radius;
3. **the QR** → the slug is generated from the outlet's name if it is blank → the
   QR and the URL are on the tab → **print it on the menu card** → **and share it**
   (copy / WhatsApp / download) → the tenant sends it to its own customers → they scan
   it → they land on the tenant's branded app → **they must register** (§5) → they
   become the tenant's members → they can order;
4. **the customer registration switch** — the delivery app **requires a registered
   member** (§5) → but registration is refused while the outlet's
   `customer_registration_enabled` is off (`models.py:483`; the refusal —
   `loyalty.py:335-343`) → **so the Delivery tab shows this switch, and the delivery
   cannot go live without it** → **it is the one setting the tenant must turn on
   before the QR goes out**;
5. **Delivery Management → the drivers** → add the tenant's drivers (a name and a
   phone);
6. **done** — **the delivery is live**.

## 4. The order flow

**The rule that decides the whole design — the shared order machine does not change.**
The core keeps exactly the five statuses it has today:

```
pending → confirmed → preparing → ready → served
                          └──── the shared machine — UNCHANGED ────┘
```

**The delivery is a SECOND, PARALLEL leg on the order** — it never touches the
shared statuses:

```
unassigned → assigned → picked_up → delivered
└──────── the delivery leg — a table, NOT the core statuses ────────┘
```

→ **the handover IS the shared `served` step** — the core moves `ready → served`
exactly when the leg reaches `delivered` → **and the word on the button becomes
"Delivered"** (the same wording trick the house already uses for "Collected" —
`businessType.js:38`).

> **Why the leg and not two new statuses** — this is the answer to "propose the flow
> that does not go against the current ordering flow":

| If we added `out_for_delivery` and `delivered` to `orders.status` | What would break |
|---|---|
| **the live-order rule** | `isLiveOrder()` (`paymentModes.js:47`) — a **prepay** order is live only in `pending`/`confirmed`/`preparing`/`ready` → **the order would vanish from the OrderQueue while the driver is on the road** |
| **the finished-order rule** | `is_order_finished()` (`payment_modes.py:159`) — finished means `completed` **or** `served` + paid → **a third end state needs a third rule, and the revenue reports would silently miss the delivery orders** |
| **the shared flow array** | `OrderQueue.jsx:135` — the hardcoded `flow` → the button would never appear |
| **the cancellation list** | `cancellable_statuses` (`order.py:1287`, `1359`) and the customer app's own cancel (`pwa.py:618`) → literal lists that stop at `preparing` → a new status would need a third edit |
| **the reports** | `reports.py:145` filters on `payment_status == "paid"` → the money still lands, but the status vocabulary forks |

→ **the leg keeps the shared machine, the money rules and the reports exactly as they
are** → **the delivery reuses `served` as the handover** → **no status vocabulary
forks, no rule is rewritten, and the revenue path is untouched.**

**The leg's statuses and the staff's buttons** — the button depends on the **pair**
(core status, leg status) → so the queue's next-step helper must read the **order**,
not just the status (`getNextStatus(current)` → `getNextStatus(order)`,
`OrderQueue.jsx:134`):

| The core status | The leg | The button | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pending` | `unassigned` | **Confirm** | the shared step (unchanged) |
| `confirmed` | `unassigned` | **Assign driver** | the leg → `assigned` (a name + a phone from the tenant's roster) |
| `confirmed` / `preparing` | `assigned` | **Change driver** | the leg stays `assigned`; the name/phone change |
| `preparing` | `assigned` | **Start preparing** | the shared step (unchanged) |
| `ready` | `assigned` | **Out for delivery** | the leg → `picked_up` — the driver has the food |
| `ready` | `picked_up` | **Delivered** | the leg → `delivered` **and** the core → `served` — the handover |
| `served` | `delivered` | — | done (the money write may still be pending) |

→ **the three rules of the leg** — the driver may be **assigned** from `confirmed`
onward (a driver can be told in advance) · the leg may only go **`picked_up`** from
`ready` (the food must be bagged) · and the core may only go to **`served`** from
`picked_up` (the food must be on the road).

→ **and the leg must be validated on the SERVER — because the backend has no status
whitelist** — `OrderStatusUpdate.status` is a bare `str` (`order.py:340`) and the
status route has **no whitelist** (`order.py:949`) → **any client can write any
status string** → **so the hidden buttons are NOT the guard**:
- a delivery order may only move to **`served`** when its leg is **`picked_up`** —
  checked **server-side**, in the same write as the leg;
- the leg may only move **forward**, one step at a time
  (`unassigned → assigned → picked_up → delivered`), never backwards;
- the **other order types keep today's behaviour exactly** — the shared status route
  is untouched for them.
→ **so the leg is the one place where a new server-side rule must be written** — the
cost of the leg, paid once, in one route.

**The cancellation rule stays exactly as it is** — pending / confirmed / preparing
(`cancellable_statuses` — `order.py:1287` and `1359`; the customer app's own cancel
— `pwa.py:618`) → **a delivery whose leg is `assigned` but not yet `picked_up` sits
at `ready` → it is not cancellable** → **that is the existing rule, unchanged** → and
it is the right one (the kitchen has already cooked it).

**The kitchen** — a delivery ticket is a kitchen ticket **until the driver takes it**:
- the kitchen's **status filter** (`Kitchen.jsx:121` — the `pending`/`confirmed`/
  `preparing`/`ready` list) would keep the ticket in the ready column for the whole
  drive (and the ready tickets stay until they are dismissed — `Kitchen.jsx:268`)
  → **so the kitchen drops the delivery ticket when the leg reaches `picked_up`** →
  the order payload must carry the leg (a `delivery` object) → the kitchen filters
  on it;
- the kitchen's **type chip** (`Kitchen.jsx:362`, `Kitchen.jsx:459`, `Kitchen.jsx:900`
  — all three are `dine_in ? … : takeaway`) → **a third arm → "Delivery"**;
- → **v1.5 corrected a source line** — v1.4 cited `Kitchen.jsx:266` for the status
  filter; the real filter is **line 121** (line 268 is the "ready stays" rule).

**The queue already knows the delivery** — `OrderQueue.jsx:199` already filters the
delivery orders (`o.order_type === 'delivery' && isLiveOrder(o)`) and the type label
at `OrderQueue.jsx:959` already has a delivery arm → **the queue's job is only the
buttons and the leg chip.**

**The customer's status page** — the PWA shows the **merged** timeline:
- the shared steps (Placed → Confirmed → Preparing → Ready);
- the leg's steps (Driver assigned → Out for delivery → Delivered) — **only when the
  leg is not `unassigned`**;
- the driver's name and phone — **always** (the WhatsApp call);
- the ETA — the estimate, refreshed when the driver is assigned.

**The money** — the rules are the existing ones:
- **prepaid** — the DuitNow QR or the online payment → **the kitchen waits**
  (`order-flow-standards.mdx` §2.4) → the order stays `pending` until the money
  lands (`status_after_payment()` → `confirmed`, `payment_modes.py:142`) → **no ghost
  delivery**;
- **cash on delivery** — the driver collects → the staff records it at the handover →
  the `Payment` row with `payment_method = 'cash'` → the postpay write lands the order
  on `completed` (`payment_modes.py:142`);
- → **and a delivery prepay is `prepay_online`** (the money comes through the
  gateway, not at a counter) → **so it needs a connected payment account**
  (`outlet_settings.py:317` — the `LOCKED_MODES` check, `payment_modes.py:47`) **and a
  Malaysian outlet** (`ONLINE_COUNTRIES` — `payment_modes.py:73`; the check itself is
  `country_supports_online()` — `payment_modes.py:96`) → **otherwise the
  delivery order is `postpay`** (cash on delivery) → **which is the default anyway**.

### 4.1 The COD question — a delivery has TWO endings, and they can happen in either order

**What Kens asked:** *we allow prepay AND COD — do we need a new order status flow?*
**The answer: the shared statuses stay exactly as they are, but the delivery needs ONE
new rule — and the live code proves why.**

**Why the shared rule breaks on a delivery.** The shared machine encodes one rule
(`status_after_payment` — `payment_modes.py:142`): **the money lands last → the order
is `completed`**. That is true at a counter and true at a table. It is **not** true
for a delivery, because **the handover and the settlement are two different events
that can happen in either order**:

- the driver hands the food over and the customer pays the driver **at the door** →
  the handover and the money are the same moment;
- the driver takes the cash and the **cash reaches the shop later** → the handover is
  first, the money second;
- a tenant that takes the money at the counter and delivers later → the money is first.

**The two facts, and who owns each:**

| the fact | where it lives | who decides it |
|---|---|---|
| **the handover happened** | `delivery_orders.status = 'delivered'` | the driver, or the staff |
| **the money settled** | `orders.payment_status = 'paid'` | the staff |

→ **the order is finished when BOTH are true** → **and they may arrive in either
order** → **the two orders converge on the same end state** → **the tenant's process
never has to be taught.**

**The trap the live code has today — this is the finding that makes the rule
necessary.** `status_after_payment` writes `completed` for ANY postpay order
(`payment_modes.py:142`), so:

| the state | the core status | `payment_status` | `is_order_finished` says | what goes wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **the money first, the food still on the road** | `ready` | `paid` | **finished** (the write set `completed`) | the order **leaves the live queue before the customer has the food** — and the POS then **cannot fix it** |
| the handover first, the cash not counted yet | `served` | `pending` | not finished (correct) | — |
| both done | `completed` | `paid` | finished | — |

→ **the first row is the trap** → **and the front end makes it worse**:
`isLiveOrder` (`paymentModes.js:47`) says a postpay order is live only while the money
is missing → **the order vanishes from the queue** → and `canAdvanceOrder`
(`paymentModes.js:62`) says a postpay order may be advanced only while the money is
missing → **once the cash is in, the status button is gone** → **the shop can no
longer see, or move, an order whose driver is still on the road.**

**The rule — one helper, two writers, order-independent:**

```
the delivery order's core status is decided by TWO facts:
    the leg      (unassigned → assigned → picked_up → delivered)
    the money    (payment_status: pending → paid)

  the payment write (the money lands):
      if the leg is already "delivered"  ->  the core goes to "completed"
      else                               ->  the core does NOT move
                                           (the money is recorded; the handover is pending)

  the leg write (the handover happens):
      the core goes to "served"
      if the money is already in          ->  the core goes to "completed"
```

→ **the two writes are the same two facts read in a different order** → **the order
ends at `completed` either way.**

→ **and the two modes land on the two end states the shared machine already knows:**
a **prepay** delivery ends at **`served`** (the money is already in — the prepay branch
of `is_order_finished` — `payment_modes.py:159`) and a **COD** delivery ends at
**`completed`** (the postpay branch) → **no new core status, no new end state, and the
reports are untouched** (they filter on `payment_status == 'paid'` — `reports.py:145`).

→ **the three code touch points — and only three:**
1. **the backend** — a delivery-aware settlement helper (next to `payment_modes.py`) that
   BOTH the payment write (`order_service.py:791`) and the leg write call;
2. **`isLiveOrder`** (`paymentModes.js:47`) — a delivery order is live while **the leg
   is not delivered OR the money is missing** (today: a postpay order is live only
   while unpaid → the early-cash order disappears);
3. **`canAdvanceOrder`** (`paymentModes.js:62`) — **the leg's buttons are not gated by
   the money** (today: a postpay order advances only while it is unpaid) → the kitchen's
   steps stay gated (a prepay order waits for its money) → **the driver's steps do
   not**.

→ **the other order types never reach it** — the rule reads the order's own
`order_type` → **the dine-in and takeaway orders keep today's behaviour exactly.**

### 4.2 Prepay AND COD together — the mode becomes the customer's choice

**What the tenant can set today.** The timing is a map on the outlet's settings, **one
mode per order type** (`resolve_mode` — `payment_modes.py:182` → the map's `delivery`
entry) → so today an outlet is **either** all-prepay **or** all-COD for delivery.

**What Kens asks for.** The customer picks at the checkout: pay online, or pay the
driver.

→ **the order's mode is already a per-order snapshot** (`orders.payment_mode` —
`models.py:255`; stamped at creation — `order_service.py:551`) → **the choice needs NO
new column** → **what changes is where the mode comes from for a delivery order.**

**The proposal — three pieces:**
1. **the outlet's delivery settings gain the ALLOWED modes** (`payment_modes` in the
   JSON column — §3) → **the tenant still decides what is allowed** → **the customer
   picks among what is allowed**;
2. **the delivery PWA's checkout shows the choice** → the customer picks → the server
   resolves the order's mode → **the client's choice is validated against the outlet's
   allow-list** (the shape of the whole design: the client never decides the money);
3. **the lock on `prepay_online` becomes PER ORDER TYPE** — this is the live finding
   that makes the whole thing possible.

**The lock is the blocker, and it is global today.** `prepay_online` is refused for the
WHOLE map when the outlet has no connected payment account or is outside Malaysia
(`normalize_map` — `payment_modes.py:238`; the connected-account check —
`outlet_settings.py:317`; the country rule — `ONLINE_COUNTRIES` — `payment_modes.py:73`)
→ **and the reason it prints is about the in-venue app**: *"the customer app has no
checkout"* (`LOCKED_MODES` — `payment_modes.py:47`).

→ **that reason is FALSE for the delivery app** — the delivery PWA HAS a checkout →
→ **and it is TRUE for the in-venue app, which still has none** → **one global lock
cannot be right for both** → **the lock becomes per order type**: `delivery` may take
`prepay_online` when the outlet has a connected account and is in Malaysia; `dine_in`
and `takeaway` stay locked → **and the tenant's other outlets keep their own country
rule** (the rule stays per OUTLET — `ONLINE_COUNTRIES`).

→ **the front end already has the same shape waiting**: the POS's own settings screen
offers `prepay_online` behind the connected-account switch (`Settings.jsx:1274`) →
→ **the Delivery tab needs the same treatment, per order type.**

**The delivery fee** — the **server** computes it (the client never sends a price) and
it reaches the order's total → **the reports pick it up unchanged** (they filter on
`payment_status == "paid"` — `reports.py:145`).

## 6. The architecture — one API, two PWAs

| Surface | Serves | Status |
|---|---|---|
| the **in-venue PWA** (the customer app) | the dine-in + the takeaway (**unchanged**) | exists |
| the **delivery PWA** (new) | the delivery | new |
| the **POS staff app** | the counter, the kitchen, the driver assignment | exists |

**The shared core — never duplicated:**
- the menu, the prices, the tax, the currency;
- the **orders** — the same order table (the `order_type = 'delivery'`);
- the **outlets** (the tenant, the slug);
- the **payments** (the `Payment` table — the prepay, the cash);
- the **kitchen** (the delivery order arrives in the same kitchen);
- the **reports** (the delivery revenue — the takings per outlet already exist).

→ **the delivery PWA is a second *client* of the same API** — **a fork here would
duplicate the whole system**.

**The four audit gates** (the standing rule) — **the delivery PWA ships the public
customer key** → **every new route it calls needs all four**:
1. **who may call it?** → the PWA's key is public → the staff routes → **403** → the
   delivery PWA needs its **own public routes** (the storefront, the cart, the order
   , the payment, the status) — with the **ownership proof** (the
   `dining_customer_id` / the order token);
2. **whose data?** → the tenant comes from the **session/slug** (never from a client
   parameter);
3. **did they buy it?** → the tenant's plan must carry the delivery feature → **but the
   gate as it stands would REFUSE on day one — the review found it** — the gate compares
   the feature **name** against the plan's list (`auth.py:681`) and **raises 403**
   ("This feature is not included in your plan: Delivery") → **and the plan's list
   comes from the feature ROWS** → **so the route must not go live before the rows do
   ** (§9);
   → **the two readers, and they differ** — the **live plan** (the cache, refreshed on
   demand — `auth.py:662`) and the **session snapshot** (stamped at login —
   `auth.py:320`) → **both carry the tab name** → so the rows must set
   `tab_name` to the string the gate compares with;
   → **and the gate is a browser-session gate ONLY** — a server-to-server caller
   (`key_type != "user"` — `auth.py:652`) **returns unchecked** → so the **PWA's
   public routes are NOT plan-gated by this dependency** → **the delivery PWA must
   check the plan itself** — this was a **real gap in this design**, and **v1.5
   closes it**:
   → **the fix is a server-side check, not a client-side one** → `plan_features.py`
   already reads the tenant's plan features → **add a `plan_has_feature()` helper**
   (the customer id and the tab name) → the delivery routes call it → **the
   comparison is against the plan's own list, read on the server** → **the client is
   never trusted to declare what the tenant bought** → and it is the **same list**
   the staff app reads →
   **one truth, two readers**.
   → **the string it compares is the tab name** (`admin.py:1052` hands the reader the
   `tab_name` under `name`) → so the delivery rows' tab names — **"Delivery"** and
   **"Delivery Management"** — are the gate keys.
4. **does the UI agree?** → the tenant's plan, the nav, the PWA's visibility.

**And the PWA's public key is NOT a staff credential** — the PWA's order routes must
prove ownership (the order token — **a new field**, or the existing
`dining_customer_id`).

## 11. The map, the routing and the ETA — the provider decision

**The needs — and they are different problems:**

| The need | The call | The provider |
|---|---|---|
| **the address → the coordinates** | one per order | **Geoapify** (already integrated) |
| **the coordinates → a place** (the address check) | one per order | **Geoapify** (already integrated) |
| **is this address inside our coverage?** | a travel-time area | **Geoapify Isochrone API** (Pro+) |
| **the route + the ETA** | one per order | **Mapbox Directions** |
| **the map the customer looks at** | the tiles | **Mapbox GL JS** |
| **the customer's MAP, the route and the ETA** | one route request per order | **Mapbox** — the tiles + Directions (**on every plan** — §11.1) |
| **the live driver MARKER** | the driver's own phone | **the driver link** (**Enterprise** — §11.1) |
| **the driver's raw GPS → a road** | the map matching | **Mapbox Map Matching** |
| **the driver → the customer** (the turn-by-turn) | **nothing** | the driver uses their own phone |

**The recommendation — a hybrid, and the reason for each part:**
- **Geoapify stays the geocoder** → it is **already integrated in two places**
  (`outlet_settings.py:359-362` — the outlet's own geocode; `competitors.py:707`,
  `731`, `763`, `778` — the reverse geocode, the search and the places) → **the
  marginal cost is zero for a small tenant** (the free plan: **3,000 credits a day**
  — a tenant doing 500 orders a day spends 500) → **and its Isochrone API answers the
  coverage question better than a circle on a map** (a 15-minute travel area, not a
  5 km radius) → **it is the one place where the existing integration pays off**;
- **Mapbox is added for the route, the ETA and the map** → the thing Geoapify's
  routing cannot do is the **live traffic** (its routing is open-data → **the ETA is
  optimistic exactly at the peak hours, when the customer is watching**) → Mapbox
  Directions is **$2 per 1,000 requests** → and the map itself is inside the free
  tier (**100,000 map loads a month**);
- **no Google** → the Routes API is the best traffic data in the market, but it is
  billed **per kilometre**, it comes with a heavier commercial setup and display
  constraints, and its place search we already have → **it is a "later, at scale"
  answer, not a "now" answer**;
- **no HERE** → the strongest commercial-fleet routing in the market, at roughly
  **$3 per 1,000** → our drivers are cars and motorbikes, not trucks → **overkill**;
- **no self-hosted OSRM (yet)** → it is free, but it has **no traffic** → the ETA is
  wrong at the peak → and it is infrastructure we run → **worth revisiting when the
  volume makes the per-call cost visible** (at 100k orders a month, Mapbox Directions
  is about $200 a month for the whole platform).

**The cost, at 500 orders a day** (one tenant):
- the geocodes: 500 a day → **inside the free Geoapify plan** (3,000 credits a day);
- the routes: 500 a day = 15,000 a month → **about $30 a month** (Mapbox Directions,
  $2/1,000);
- the map loads: the customer's tracking page → **inside the free tier**;
- → **the live tracking costs about the price of a coffee per order** → **and it is
  the Enterprise tier's whole reason to exist.**

### 11.1 The customer's map and ETA are on EVERY plan — the MARKER is the paid tier

**Kens's rule:** *the delivery PWA must have a map and live tracking, so the customer
knows when the driver will arrive.* **Split that into the two things it really is,
because they cost different things:**

| the thing | does it need the driver's phone? | what it costs | the tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the map, the route and the ETA** | **no** | one Mapbox Directions request per order (~$2 per 1,000 → ~$30 a month at 500 orders a day) | **every plan** — including the free Starter |
| **the live driver MARKER** (the dot moving on the map) | **yes** | the driver's phone + the pings + the privacy work | **Enterprise** (`delivery_tracking`) |

→ **the ETA without GPS is honest, not fake** → the route is the **outlet → customer**
route (`Mapbox Directions`) → the driver starts at the outlet, so the route is accurate
**the moment the driver is assigned** → and from `picked_up` the ETA counts down by the
elapsed time → **the customer sees a shrinking arrival window on every plan.**

→ **so the free tier gets a map, a route and a real ETA** → **and the paid tier sells
the DOT**, not the map → **and this is what keeps the pitch honest**: we do not sell
"tracking" as a premium we cannot deliver without the driver's cooperation.

→ **and it corrects the commercial line in §1** → it is no longer "no GPS" → it is
**"no driver GPS on the free tier"** → **the map is the adoption hook, not the upsell.**

→ **the cost is the same Mapbox bill as before** — the difference is that it is now
**spread across every tenant** → at 500 orders a day per tenant it is ~$30 a month →
**so the free tier is not free for us at scale** → **which is a pricing decision, not a
design one** (§8 Q13).

### 11.2 The live tracking needs a location source — and that is the real question
There
is no GPS without somebody reporting it:
1. **the driver link** (my pick) — a **one-page web page** the driver opens on their
   own phone: no install, no account, three taps (Start / Arrived / Delivered) and
   it posts the location while the order is on the road → **it is not a driver app**
   (nothing to install, nothing to publish) → **the tenant's drivers must agree to
   use it** → **and it is the SAME link that carries the leg's buttons** (§12) → **one
   link, two jobs — and the POS still works with no link at all**;
2. **the tenant's own tracker** — a tenant that already runs a fleet system posts the
   position to a webhook → **we integrate, we do not replace it**;
3. **no tracking** — the core: the status timeline, the driver's name and phone, the
   ETA → **the WhatsApp model** → **and it is what the free tier gets**.

→ **the honest risk is not technical** — a tenant whose drivers will not tap anything
**cannot be tracked**, whatever provider we buy → **so the tracking tier sells a
workflow, not a map** → **the map is the cheap part.**

**The privacy — this is not a small footnote.** The driver's location and the
customer's address are **personal data** (the Malaysia PDPA; the GDPR for the tenants
that operate in the EU):
1. **the customer's address** → it lives in the leg and the order → it is **not** in
   the customer-facing tracking response **after the handover**;
2. **the driver's location** → the pings are **dropped 24 hours after the delivery**
   (the `delivery_pings` table is pruned by the nightly job) → **the pings are never
   part of the order record** (they are a separate table with their own life);
3. **the access** → the tenant's staff see **their own** orders only (the tenant
   scope on every route) → the customer sees **their own order only** (the order
   token / `dining_customer_id`);
4. **the driver** → the driver is tracked too → **the tenant must tell its own
   drivers** (the driver link says so) → **the driver's consent is the tenant's to
   obtain, not ours**;
5. **the map tiles** → the provider's terms require the attribution → the attribution
   goes on the tracking page.

**And the key** — the existing code falls back to a **hardcoded Geoapify key**
(`outlet_settings.py:359`, `competitors.py:707` and `763` — the literal is in the
source and it is **not repeated here**) → **that fallback must not survive** → the key
comes from the environment (a SecretRef) → **and the code must fail loudly when it is
missing** → a silent fallback to a shared key is how a bill arrives from nowhere.

## 12. The driver's write — the POS, the link, and what neither may do

**The question:** *how does the driver mark the delivery successful — through our
frontend POS, or another tool?*

**The answer: both — with one rule about what each of them may write.**

**The split — the leg is the driver's, the money is the staff's:**

| the write | who may make it | why |
|---|---|---|
| the leg (`assigned` → `picked_up` → `delivered`) | **the POS staff** *and* **the driver link** | it is the physical truth of the delivery — only the driver and the shop know it |
| the handover (`leg = 'delivered'` → the core moves) | **the server**, in the same write | the server owns the shared machine — it is the only place the status can be trusted |
| **the money** (`payment_status = 'paid'`) | **the POS staff only** | it is the tenant's money → the write already sits behind `staff:orders:write` (`order.py:1013`) → **a driver must never be able to mark money in** |
| the cash **claim** ("I took RM 12.50") | the POS staff **and** the driver link | a claim is a fact about the handover, not about the ledger → it sits on the leg → the staff reconcile it |

→ **so the driver can say "delivered, I took RM 12.50"** → **and the staff still write
the money** → **the tenant's cash flow is unchanged** → **and a COD delivery's two
endings still land in either order** (§4.1).

**Surface A — the POS (every plan, no new build).** The **Delivery Management** screen
(§10.2) carries the leg's buttons: **Assign driver** → **Out for delivery** →
**Delivered** → the same buttons, the same queue, the same server rules as today →
**a tenant whose driver is also its staff needs nothing else** → **and this is the
surface that still works when the driver will not tap anything** (the WhatsApp model).

**Surface B — the driver link (the second surface).** A **one-page web page**, not an
app: the driver opens a link on their own phone and taps three buttons → **nothing to
install, nothing to publish, no app store** → **and it is the same link that carries
the live pings** (§11.2) → **one link, two jobs.**

→ **the link's token is per order, scoped, signed and expiring** — a new claim set
next to the guest token (`guest_session.py:51` — the pattern: a signed token that
names what it may do) → **never the PWA's public key** → **a leaked link moves ONE
order's leg, and it expires.**

→ **what the link may NOT do** (the boundary, stated once):
- **it may not write money** — no `payment_status`, no payment row;
- **it may not write the core status directly** — the server moves the core in the same
  write as the leg (that is where the shared machine's rules live);
- **it may not touch the items, the prices, the fee or the address** — the order's money
  was settled at the checkout;
- **it may not cancel** — a cancellation is a staff decision (and the existing rule
  stops at `preparing` anyway — `order.py:1287`).

**Surface C — what we do NOT build.** A driver **app** (§2's boundary) → the link is
the app.

**The live cost of Surface B.** The link only works if the driver opens it → **the
honest risk is the driver's cooperation, not the provider** (§11.2) → → **so Surface A
must be complete on its own** → **it is not a fallback: it is the primary, and the link
is the convenience.**
