## 1. The short answer

**One codebase, one deployment, one API — and every tenant gets its own branded
delivery PWA.**

**The principle that decides every scope question:**
> **The tenant owns its delivery operation. We own the commerce.**
> The tenant sets its own charges, allocates its own drivers, manages them its own way
> — **we do not care how**.

**The commercial line** — Grab charges the customer and the merchant a commission and
carries the courier network → **our pitch is the same job at a fraction of the cost:
no commission, no driver marketplace, no GPS** — and the tenant's own drivers
are a **feature for them** (they know their customers).

**And the plan gating (the question) — mapped onto our REAL plans:**

| Plan | The shape | The delivery features |
|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | 1 outlet · 3 staff | **Delivery** — the order type, the storefront, the flat fee, the driver assignment |
| **Basic** | 3 outlets · 10 staff | **Delivery** (+ the outlets) |
| **Pro** | 5 outlets · 25 staff | **Delivery** + **Delivery Zones** (the postcode pricing) |
| **Enterprise** | unlimited | **Delivery** + **Delivery Zones** + **Delivery Scheduling** + **Delivery Analytics** |

> **The prices are NOT in this document — they are data.** The catalogue lives in the
> **website API** (`plan_prices` — per currency, per billing cycle, with the
> promotions and their end dates) and it **changes** → **a design document must
> never quote a price**. The **source of truth** is
> `GET /api/plans/published`.
>
> **The snapshot (for the reasoning below only — Nov 5 2026):** the **Starter is
> free** (USD 0 / MYR 0) · Basic USD 10 / MYR 40 · Pro USD 20 / MYR 80
> · Enterprise USD 50 / MYR 200 — **so the delivery on the Starter costs us
> nothing and the free plan is the hook**.

**The split — what is in the core ("Delivery")**
- the delivery **order type** — just another order type (the address, the fee,
  the driver);
- the **white-label storefront** — the branding fields **already exist**
  (`restaurant_name`, `restaurant_logo`, `brand_color`) → it costs us almost nothing
  → **charging for it would fight the adoption**;
- the tenant's **own fee settings** — the whole value is **"no commission"** →
  charging for the fee settings would contradict the pitch;
- the **driver assignment** — it is two fields.

**What is paid (the ops depth)**
- **Delivery Zones** — the postcode pricing → **Pro**;
- **Delivery Scheduling** — the promised windows → **Enterprise**;
- **Delivery Analytics** — the delivery revenue, the fee per order → **Enterprise**.

**The adoption argument — the decisive one** — a tenant that switches to us **for the
delivery** but has to pay for it loses the switch → **the basics must be free**
→ **the paid tier is the sophistication** (the zones, the scheduling, the
analytics).

**And the Starter argument — strengthened by the free Starter** — the delivery
costs us **almost nothing on Starter** (one JSON column, the storefront reads the
existing menu) → and the Starter is **free** → so the delivery is a
**customer-acquisition feature** **and a viral loop**: the tenant's own
customers order delivery → the tenant sees the orders arriving → the upgrade →
**the zones, the scheduling and the analytics are the upgrade path** — **the
delivery is the Starter's hook**.

## 2. What we build and what we do NOT

| We build | We do NOT build |
|---|---|
| the **white-label storefront PWA** (the tenant's branding, the menu, the cart, the checkout, the order status) | a **marketplace** — the customers are the tenant's own |
| the **delivery order type** in the core POS | a **driver app** — the tenant's own drivers |
| the **fee settings** (the flat fee, the minimum, the free-delivery threshold, the zones) | **GPS / live tracking** — that is Grab's cost centre |
| the **driver assignment** (a name + a phone on the order) | a **courier matching engine** |
| the **status flow** (out for delivery → delivered) | **commission handling** — we take no commission |
| the **prepay path** (the DuitNow QR / the online payment) | a **delivery schedule/dispatch optimiser** |
| **cash on delivery** (the driver collects — the tenant's choice) | **anything that makes the tenant manage OUR way** |

**The principle restated** — *the tenant manages its drivers by whatever means it likes*
: WhatsApp, a spreadsheet, its own app, a group chat — **we only need to carry the
driver's name and phone on the order** → the customer can call them.

## 3. The tenant setup — "easy setup"

**The white-label identity — the fields already exist:**

| Field | Exists? |
|---|---|
| `restaurant_name` | **yes** (`models.py:370`) |
| `restaurant_logo` | **yes** (`models.py:371`) |
| `brand_color` | **yes** (`models.py:374`) |
| `operating_hours` | **yes** (`models.py:381`) |
| **the delivery SWITCH** (`allow_delivery`) | **yes — it EXISTS** (`models.py:447` · the settings screen `Settings.jsx:1203` · the customer app reads it `order.py:702` · the presets `business_presets.py`) |
| **the delivery ADDRESS** (`orders.delivery_address`) | **yes — it EXISTS** (`models.py:280` · carried through the write path `order_service.py:494`) |
| **the storefront slug** (the PWA's URL) | **new** — one field |

→ **the storefront setup is ~2 new fields** (the slug + the delivery settings)
.

> **The review found the switch already exists** — `outlet_settings.allow_delivery`
> (`models.py:447`) is **the delivery on/off the tenant already owns**. It has a
> settings screen (`Settings.jsx:1203`), it reaches the **customer app**
> (`order.py:702` — the guest app's outlet answer already carries it) and it is
> **off in every preset** (`business_presets.py` — the delivery is off until a
> tenant deliberately switches it on).
> → **so the JSON column must NOT carry a second switch** — `delivery_settings`
> holds the **fee**, the **minimum**, the **free threshold**, the **zones** and the
> **ETA** — and **`allow_delivery` stays the one switch** (the code reads "null"
> as "no fee, no zones" — **not** as "delivery off" — that is `allow_delivery`'s
> job).
> → **and the order type already exists too** — `orders.order_type` takes
> **"delivery"** (`order.py:262`) and `ORDER_TYPES` already lists it
> (`payment_modes.py:36`) → **Phase 1 is therefore SMALLER than this document assumed**
> — what is missing is the **fee** and the **driver**, not the order type.
>
> **But the payment-timing MAP is not the same thing as `ORDER_TYPES` — the review found it
> ** — `ORDER_TYPES` is the list of order types the map may **name**; the map itself
> is **per outlet** (the live rows hold `{"dine_in": ..., "takeaway": ...}` — **no
> "delivery" key**) → `resolve_mode()` (`payment_modes.py:182`) falls back to
> **postpay** for a delivery order until the tenant sets one → **so a delivery order
> today is cash-on-delivery** — which is this document's own default.
> → **and the tenant can set one** — `normalize_map()` (`payment_modes.py:238`)
> writes a mode only for a type the outlet **serves** → **so the delivery mode becomes
> settable the moment `allow_delivery` is on**. (The house already anticipated
> this — `merge_map()` (`payment_modes.py:207`) says in its own docstring that the
> presets must not wipe *"the delivery entry"*.)

**The delivery settings — new** (one JSON column, the house pattern):

```json
// OutletSettings.delivery_settings
{
  "fee": 5.00,              // the flat delivery fee
  "free_above": 100.00,     // free above this order value
  "minimum_order": 30.00,   // the minimum order value
  "eta_minutes": 30,
  "eta_max_minutes": 45,
  "prepaid_only": false,    // false = cash on delivery is allowed
  "zones": [                // the postcode pricing
    { "name": "SS17", "postcodes": ["43000"], "fee": 5.00 }
  ]
}
```

→ **one nullable column** → **no backfill**.

> **But the column does need the ALTER — the review found it** — `create_all()`
> (`main.py:44`) creates a **missing table** — it **never adds a column to an existing
> one** → the house mechanism for that is `_ensure_added_columns()`
> (`main.py:47` — it runs on every start and only alters when the column is really
> missing) → **so the delivery settings column goes there**, the same way
> `payment_timing` did (`main.py:111-117`) → **idempotent, reversible, and
> it leaves the live rows alone**.
>
> **And "null" means "no fee, no zones" — NOT "delivery off"** — the off switch is
> `allow_delivery` and it already exists (`models.py:447`) → **the JSON column
> must not carry a second switch** — the same rule the review found in §3.

**The setup flow** (the tenant's side):
1. **Settings → Delivery** → turn it on;
2. **the fees** (the flat fee, the minimum, the free threshold);
3. **the zones** (optional — the postcodes and their fee);
4. **the storefront URL** (the slug) → the PWA;
5. **done** — **no driver setup** (the tenant's problem).

## 4. The order flow

**The statuses** — the shared machine **plus TWO delivery-specific steps**:

```
pending → confirmed → preparing → ready → out_for_delivery → delivered
                                └── the shared machine ✓ ──┘  └── the delivery only ✓ ✓
```

- **the last step's wording** — "Delivered" for the delivery (the translation trick
  — the same as "Collected");
- → **two new status values** (`out_for_delivery` and `delivered`) — and they apply
  **only to the delivery type** — the dine-in and the takeaway keep their shared
  machine untouched;
- → **and "delivered" must resolve to the existing end state** — **"completed"**
  (the payment drives the order there) → **or the revenue reports would miss every
  delivery order** (`is_order_finished()` — §4);
- → **the cancellation rule** — pending / confirmed / preparing (unchanged).

> **The review found the status flow is a HARDCODED array — and three of them** — the new
> state is not one change, it is **three**:
> - the **staff flow** — `OrderQueue.jsx:135` → `const flow = ['pending', 'confirmed',
>   'preparing', 'ready', 'served']` → **`out_for_delivery` is not in it** → the
>   button would never appear;
> - the **kitchen's filter** — `Kitchen.jsx:266` → it keeps only `pending` / `confirmed` /
>   `preparing` / `ready` → **a delivery ticket drops out of the kitchen when it goes out
>   ** (that is right — the food has left — but it must be a decision, not an
>   accident);
> - the **kitchen's type chip** — `Kitchen.jsx:900` → `order.order_type === 'dine_in' ? … :
>   …` → **a delivery ticket would be labelled "Takeaway"** → the chip needs a
>   third arm.
>
> → **and the backend accepts any status string** — `OrderStatusUpdate.status` is a
> bare `str` (`order.py:340`) and the route has **no whitelist**
> (`order.py:942`) → **so "out_for_delivery" would be storable today** — **but the
> finished-order rule would not know it** (see the money note below).
>
> → **and the cancellation list is a literal too** — `cancellable_statuses`
> (`order.py:1233` and `1305`) and the customer app's own cancel (`pwa.py:618`)
> both stop at **preparing** → **so "out for delivery" is NOT cancellable** — which
> is the right rule (the food is already on the road).

**The driver's place in the flow**
1. the order is placed (prepaid or cash on delivery);
2. the kitchen prepares (the same kitchen);
3. **the staff assigns the driver** (a name + a phone — from the tenant's own list
   ) → **the assignment is on the order**;
4. **the staff taps "out for delivery"** when the driver takes it;
5. **the customer sees the driver's name and phone** in their PWA → **a WhatsApp call
   ** — the whole "tracking" for the price of a phone number;
6. **"delivered"** → the cash is collected if needed → the payment is recorded.

**The money**
- **prepaid** — the DuitNow QR or the online payment → **the kitchen waits**
  (the rule from `order-flow-standards.mdx` §2.4) → **no ghost delivery**;
- **cash on delivery** — the driver collects → the staff records it at the handover
  → the `Payment` row with `payment_method = 'cash'`.

> **The review found the online payment is CLOSED outside Malaysia — it is not a free choice
> ** — `ONLINE_COUNTRIES` is `("MY",)` (`payment_modes.py:73`) and
> `country_supports_online()` (`payment_modes.py:96`) is the check → **an outlet in
> another country can never offer it** (the rule is per **outlet**, not per tenant
> — the same tenant may own a Malaysian outlet and another elsewhere).
> → **and the "pay online" mode is LOCKED for a second reason** — `LOCKED_MODES`
> (`payment_modes.py:47`) refuses `prepay_online` unless the tenant has a **connected
> payment account** (`outlet_settings.py:317`) → **so "prepaid" is per tenant**
> , not a property of the delivery.
>
> **What the payment write does to the STATUS — the one to watch** — `status_after_payment()`
> (`payment_modes.py:142`) sends a **postpay** order to **"completed"** and a
> **prepay** order to **"confirmed"** → **so the money write lands the order on a
> status the delivery flow does not own** → **the delivery needs its own hand-off
> between the kitchen and the driver** (the status the kitchen leaves at **"ready"**
> and the driver takes from there).
>
> **And the finished-order rule** — `is_order_finished()` (`payment_modes.py:159`)
> counts an order as finished when it is **"completed"** or **"served" + paid** →
> **a delivery order that ends at "delivered" would never be counted** → the revenue
> reports would **miss the delivery revenue** → **so "delivered" must resolve to the
> existing end state** (the payment drives it there).

## 5. The customer app — the white-label PWA

**A separate entry point** — **not** a flag inside the existing app (that file is
already large) → **a new `delivery-app/`** sharing the API client and the i18n
— **one codebase, one deployment**.

**The pages**
1. **the storefront** — the tenant's logo, the brand colour, the categories, the
   menu, the hours, **the delivery fee + the minimum** (shown before the
   checkout — the customer must see the fee);
2. **the cart** — the same rules as the in-venue PWA;
3. **the checkout** — the name, the phone, the **address**, the **postcode** (the
   zone → the fee), the payment;
4. **the order status** — placed → confirmed → preparing → out for delivery →
   delivered **+ the driver's name and phone**;
5. **the order history** (the customer's own — the `dining_customer_id` already
   exists).

**The white-label rule** — **the tenant's branding comes from the outlet settings**
(the logo, the colour, the name — **already stored**) → **no per-tenant build,
no per-tenant deployment**.

## 6. The architecture — one API, two PWAs

| Surface | Serves | Status |
|---|---|---|
| the **in-venue PWA** (the customer app) | the dine-in + the takeaway (**unchanged**) | exists |
| the **delivery PWA** (new) | the delivery | new |
| the **POS staff app** | the counter, the kitchen, the driver assignment | exists |

**The shared core — never duplicated:**
- the menu, the prices, the tax, the currency;
- the **orders** — the same order table (the `order_type = 'delivery'`);
- the **outlets** (the tenant, the slug);
- the **payments** (the `Payment` table — the prepay, the cash);
- the **kitchen** (the delivery order arrives in the same kitchen);
- the **reports** (the delivery revenue — the takings per outlet already exist).

→ **the delivery PWA is a second *client* of the same API** — **a fork here would
duplicate the whole system**.

**The four audit gates** (the standing rule) — **the delivery PWA ships the public
customer key** → **every new route it calls needs all four**:
1. **who may call it?** → the PWA's key is public → the staff routes → **403** → the
   delivery PWA needs its **own public routes** (the storefront, the cart, the order
   , the payment, the status) — with the **ownership proof** (the
   `dining_customer_id` / the order token);
2. **whose data?** → the tenant comes from the **session/slug** (never from a client
   parameter);
3. **did they buy it?** → the tenant's plan must carry the delivery feature → **but the
   gate as it stands would REFUSE on day one — the review found it** — the gate compares
   the feature **name** against the plan's list (`auth.py:684`) and **raises 403**
   ("This feature is not included in your plan: Delivery") → **and the plan's list
   comes from the feature ROWS** → **so the route must not go live before the rows do
   ** (§9);
   → **the two readers, and they differ** — the **live plan** (the cache, refreshed on
   demand — `auth.py:662`) and the **session snapshot** (stamped at login —
   `auth.py:320`) → **both carry the tab name** → so the rows must set
   `tab_name` to the string the gate compares with;
   → **and the gate is a browser-session gate ONLY** — a server-to-server caller
   (`key_type != "user"` — `auth.py:652`) **returns unchecked** → so the **PWA's
   public routes are NOT plan-gated by this dependency** → **the delivery PWA must
   check the plan itself** (the tenant's own feature list) — this is a **real gap in
   this design**.
4. **does the UI agree?** → the tenant's plan, the nav, the PWA's visibility.

**And the PWA's public key is NOT a staff credential** — the PWA's order routes must
prove ownership (the order token — **a new field**, or the existing
`dining_customer_id`).

## 7. The phases

| Phase | What | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **0 — the plan rows** | the four `features` rows (sort orders 39-42) + their `plan_features` rows — **BEFORE any route declares the gate** (§9) | small |
| **1 — the core** | the `delivery_settings` (one nullable column **+ the idempotent ALTER**) · the delivery order type (the address, the fee) · the fee in the order's total | medium |
| **2 — the staff side** | the delivery order in the OrderQueue · the **driver assignment** (the name + the phone) · the **status buttons** (out for delivery → delivered — **the hardcoded flow array and the kitchen's type chip**) · the kitchen's ticket shows the delivery | medium |
| **3 — the storefront** | the delivery PWA (the branding, the menu, the cart, the checkout, the zones) + the public routes (the four gates — **including the plan check the PWA must do itself**) | **large** |
| **4 — the money** | the prepay (the DuitNow QR — **Malaysia only**) + cash on delivery + the payment records | medium |
| **5 — the paid tier** | the zones (the postcode pricing) · the scheduling · the delivery analytics | medium |

**The order matters** — the core → the staff side → the storefront → the money:
**the POS must be able to deliver before the customer can order delivery**.

## 8. The open questions

1. **Cash on delivery — the default?** — my pick: **allowed** (the tenant's driver
   collects) but **the tenant can require prepay** (`prepaid_only`) — my
   default: **allowed** (the Malaysian market expects it).
2. **The zones — by postcode or by area name?** — my pick: **postcodes** (the
   customer types one → the fee) — **no map**.
3. **Does the delivery order carry a separate fee line?** — my pick: **yes** (a
   delivery-fee line item) → the invoice and the e-Invoice stay clean.
4. **Do the tenants' customers get an account?** — the `dining_customer_id` already
   exists → **reuse it** (the PWA's login).
5. **Is the delivery PWA its own domain or a path?** — my pick: **a path**
   (`/d/<slug>`) → **one deployment, one certificate** — a custom domain later
   .
6. **Does the delivery order need its own kitchen screen?** — my pick: **no** — the same
   kitchen (a restaurant's delivery food is made in the same kitchen).
7. **Who checks the plan on the PWA's own routes?** — **the review raised this** — the
   `require_plan_feature()` gate is a **browser-session gate** → a server-to-server
   caller is **returned unchecked** → **the delivery PWA's public routes are NOT gated by
   it** → my pick: **the PWA checks the tenant's feature list itself** (the
   same list the staff app reads) → **so a tenant without the delivery feature cannot order
   delivery**.

## 9. The review — what the live code says

**Every claim in this document was re-checked against the live backend, the live frontend,
the live website API and the live database (Nov 12, 2026)**.

**The short verdict** — **the shape of this design is right** (one codebase, the
tenant owns the drivers, the fee belongs to the tenant, no GPS, no commission)
→ **and most of the DATA it needs already exists** → **but six things in this document
would have failed on day one** → they are corrected above and listed here.

### 9.1 What already exists — the delivery primitives are NOT new

| The thing | Where | The truth |
|---|---|---|
| **the delivery SWITCH** | `outlet_settings.allow_delivery` (`models.py:447`) | **exists** — settings screen (`Settings.jsx:1203`), the customer app reads it (`order.py:702`), off in every preset (`business_presets.py`) |

| the STOREFRONT | — | **new** — no slug, no delivery app |

### 9.2 The six things that would have failed

6. **The status flow lives in a HARDCODED array** — `OrderQueue.jsx:135` → **and the kitchen's

### 9.3 What the review did NOT change
