## 1. The short answer

**One codebase, one deployment, one API — and every tenant gets its own branded
delivery PWA.**

**The principle that decides every scope question:**
> **The tenant owns its delivery operation. We own the commerce.**
> The tenant sets its own charges, allocates its own drivers, manages them its own way
> — **we do not care how**.

**The commercial line** — Grab charges the customer and the merchant a commission and
carries the courier network → **our pitch is the same job at a fraction of the cost:
no commission and no driver marketplace** — and the tenant's own drivers are a
**feature for them** (they know their customers).
→ **and the customer's WHOLE delivery app — the menu, the cart, the checkout, the
kitchen status, the delivery status, the map and the live tracking — is ONE
entitlement** (§5) → **so the thing we sell is the tenant's own operational depth**
(the zones, the analytics), **never the customer's experience** (§11.1).
→ **and since v1.9 that module sits on Enterprise** → **so the delivery is sold the
way Inventory is sold** — to the tenant that is already running a real operation.

**And the plan gating (the question) — mapped onto our REAL plans:**

**And the plan gating (the question) — mapped onto our REAL plans — and v1.9 settled
it: the delivery is ONE module on ONE plan.**

**The rule (Kens, Nov 14, 2026):** *the delivery app must have the full features, and
we should assign the delivery feature to the Enterprise plan like the inventory
features. Then only the customers of a tenant that subscribes Enterprise AND allows
delivery at that outlet can access the delivery app.*

**Two rules, and they answer different questions:**
1. **the plan decides WHO gets the delivery** → **one plan (Enterprise) carries the

   the FULL app** — the menu, the cart, the checkout (pay online or cash on delivery),
   the order submit, the kitchen status, the delivery status, the map and the live
   tracking (§5.2, §5.3).

## 2. What we build and what we do NOT

| We build | We do NOT build |
|---|---|
| the **white-label storefront PWA** (the tenant's branding, the menu, the cart, the checkout, the kitchen status, the delivery status, the map, the live tracking) | a **marketplace** — the customers are the tenant's own |
| the **delivery order type** in the core POS | a **driver APP** — the tenant's own drivers (the delivery ships a **one-page driver LINK**, not an app — §11.2, §12) |
| the **fee settings** (the flat fee, the minimum, the free-delivery threshold, the radius) | **a tiered customer app** — the delivery PWA has **ONE code path**: the map, the route, the ETA, the live marker and the checkout are the same for every entitled tenant, and since v1.9 only ONE plan carries the delivery (§5.3, §11.1) |
| the **driver assignment** (a name + a phone on the order) | a **courier matching engine** |
| the **status flow** — a **parallel leg** on the order (assigned → out for delivery → delivered) **plus one delivery-aware settlement rule** (the handover and the money are two facts — §4.1), **the shared machine untouched** | **commission handling** — we take no commission |
| the **prepay path** (the DuitNow QR / the online payment) | a **delivery schedule/dispatch optimiser** |
| **cash on delivery** (the driver collects — the tenant's choice) | **anything that makes the tenant manage OUR way** |

**The principle restated** — *the tenant manages its drivers by whatever means it likes*
: WhatsApp, a spreadsheet, its own app, a group chat — **we only need to carry the
driver's name and phone on the order** → the customer can call them.

## 4. The order flow

**The rule that decides the whole design — the shared order machine does not change.**
The core keeps exactly the five statuses it has today:

```

```

**The delivery is a SECOND, PARALLEL leg on the order** — it never touches the
shared statuses:

```

└──────── the delivery leg — a table, NOT the core statuses ────────┘
```

> **Why the leg and not two new statuses** — this is the answer to "propose the flow
> that does not go against the current ordering flow":

| If we added `out_for_delivery` and `delivered` to `orders.status` | What would break |
|---|---|
| **the live-order rule** | `isLiveOrder()` (`paymentModes.js:47`) — a **prepay** order is live only in `pending`/`confirmed`/`preparing`/`ready` → **the order would vanish from the OrderQueue while the driver is on the road** |
| **the finished-order rule** | `is_order_finished()` (`payment_modes.py:159`) — finished means `completed` **or** `served` + paid → **a third end state needs a third rule, and the revenue reports would silently miss the delivery orders** |

| **the cancellation list** | `cancellable_statuses` (`order.py:1287`, `1359`) and the customer app's own cancel (`pwa.py:618`) → literal lists that stop at `preparing` → a new status would need a third edit |
| **the reports** | `reports.py:145` filters on `payment_status == "paid"` → the money still lands, but the status vocabulary forks |

### 4.1 The COD question — a delivery has TWO endings, and they can happen in either order

**What Kens asked:** *we allow prepay AND COD — do we need a new order status flow?*
**The answer: the shared statuses stay exactly as they are, but the delivery needs ONE
new rule — and the live code proves why.**

**Why the shared rule breaks on a delivery.** The shared machine encodes one rule
(`status_after_payment` — `payment_modes.py:142`): **the money lands last → the order
is `completed`**. That is true at a counter and true at a table. It is **not** true
for a delivery, because **the handover and the settlement are two different events
that can happen in either order**:

- the driver hands the food over and the customer pays the driver **at the door** →
  the handover and the money are the same moment;
- the driver takes the cash and the **cash reaches the shop later** → the handover is
  first, the money second;
- a tenant that takes the money at the counter and delivers later → the money is first.

**The two facts, and who owns each:**

| the fact | where it lives | who decides it |
|---|---|---|
| **the handover happened** | `delivery_orders.status = 'delivered'` | the driver, or the staff |
| **the money settled** | `orders.payment_status = 'paid'` | the staff |

→ **the order is finished when BOTH are true** → **and they may arrive in either
order** → **the two orders converge on the same end state** → **the tenant's process
never has to be taught.**

**The trap the live code has today — this is the finding that makes the rule
necessary.** `status_after_payment` writes `completed` for ANY postpay order
(`payment_modes.py:142`), so:

| the state | the core status | `payment_status` | `is_order_finished` says | what goes wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **the money first, the food still on the road** | `ready` | `paid` | **finished** (the write set `completed`) | the order **leaves the live queue before the customer has the food** — and the POS then **cannot fix it** |
| the handover first, the cash not counted yet | `served` | `pending` | not finished (correct) | — |
| both done | `completed` | `paid` | finished | — |

→ **the first row is the trap** → **and the front end makes it worse**:
`isLiveOrder` (`paymentModes.js:47`) says a postpay order is live only while the money
is missing → **the order vanishes from the queue** → and `canAdvanceOrder`
(`paymentModes.js:62`) says a postpay order may be advanced only while the money is
missing → **once the cash is in, the status button is gone** → **the shop can no
longer see, or move, an order whose driver is still on the road.**

**The rule — one helper, two writers, order-independent:**

```
the delivery order's core status is decided by TWO facts:
    the leg      (unassigned → assigned → picked_up → delivered)
    the money    (payment_status: pending → paid)

  the payment write (the money lands):
      if the leg is already "delivered"  ->  the core goes to "completed"
      else                               ->  the core does NOT move
                                           (the money is recorded; the handover is pending)

  the leg write (the handover happens):
      the core goes to "served"
      if the money is already in          ->  the core goes to "completed"
```

→ **the two writes are the same two facts read in a different order** → **the order
ends at `completed` either way.**

→ **and the two modes land on the two end states the shared machine already knows:**
a **prepay** delivery ends at **`served`** (the money is already in — the prepay branch
of `is_order_finished` — `payment_modes.py:159`) and a **COD** delivery ends at
**`completed`** (the postpay branch) → **no new core status, no new end state, and the
reports are untouched** (they filter on `payment_status == 'paid'` — `reports.py:145`).

→ **the three code touch points — and only three:**
1. **the backend** — a delivery-aware settlement helper (next to `payment_modes.py`) that
   BOTH the payment write (`order_service.py:791`) and the leg write call;
2. **`isLiveOrder`** (`paymentModes.js:47`) — a delivery order is live while **the leg
   is not delivered OR the money is missing** (today: a postpay order is live only
   while unpaid → the early-cash order disappears);
3. **`canAdvanceOrder`** (`paymentModes.js:62`) — **the leg's buttons are not gated by
   the money** (today: a postpay order advances only while it is unpaid) → the kitchen's
   steps stay gated (a prepay order waits for its money) → **the driver's steps do
   not**.

→ **the other order types never reach it** — the rule reads the order's own
`order_type` → **the dine-in and takeaway orders keep today's behaviour exactly.**

### 4.2 Prepay AND COD together — the mode becomes the customer's choice

**What the tenant can set today.** The timing is a map on the outlet's settings, **one
mode per order type** (`resolve_mode` — `payment_modes.py:182` → the map's `delivery`
entry) → so today an outlet is **either** all-prepay **or** all-COD for delivery.

**What Kens asks for.** The customer picks at the checkout: pay online, or pay the
driver.

→ **the order's mode is already a per-order snapshot** (`orders.payment_mode` —
`models.py:255`; stamped at creation — `order_service.py:551`) → **the choice needs NO
new column** → **what changes is where the mode comes from for a delivery order.**

**The proposal — three pieces:**
1. **the outlet's delivery settings gain the ALLOWED modes** (`payment_modes` in the
   JSON column — §3) → **the tenant still decides what is allowed** → **the customer
   picks among what is allowed**;
2. **the delivery PWA's checkout shows the choice** → the customer picks → the server
   resolves the order's mode → **the client's choice is validated against the outlet's
   allow-list** (the shape of the whole design: the client never decides the money)
   → **and the choice is never a plan thing** — every entitled tenant's customer picks
   among the outlet's allowed modes the same way (§5.2);
3. **the lock on `prepay_online` becomes PER ORDER TYPE** — this is the live finding
   that makes the whole thing possible.

**The lock is the blocker, and it is global today.** `prepay_online` is refused for the
WHOLE map when the outlet has no connected payment account or is outside Malaysia
(`normalize_map` — `payment_modes.py:238`; the connected-account check —
`outlet_settings.py:317`; the country rule — `ONLINE_COUNTRIES` — `payment_modes.py:73`)
→ **and the reason it prints is about the in-venue app**: *"the customer app has no
checkout"* (`LOCKED_MODES` — `payment_modes.py:47`).

→ **that reason is FALSE for the delivery app** — the delivery PWA HAS a checkout →
→ **and it is TRUE for the in-venue app, which still has none** → **one global lock
cannot be right for both** → **the lock becomes per order type**: `delivery` may take
`prepay_online` when the outlet has a connected account and is in Malaysia; `dine_in`
and `takeaway` stay locked → **and the tenant's other outlets keep their own country
rule** (the rule stays per OUTLET — `ONLINE_COUNTRIES`).

→ **the front end already has the same shape waiting**: the POS's own settings screen
offers `prepay_online` behind the connected-account switch (`Settings.jsx:1274`) →
→ **the Delivery tab needs the same treatment, per order type.**

**The delivery fee** — the **server** computes it (the client never sends a price) and
it reaches the order's total → **the reports pick it up unchanged** (they filter on
`payment_status == "paid"` — `reports.py:145`).

### 4.3 The two doors — the counter and the PWA

**Kens's question:** *so we allow the customer to order as delivery through the
counter, or using the delivery PWA — is it?*

→ **BOTH. And they produce the SAME order.**

| | **the counter (the staff screen)** | **the delivery PWA** |
|---|---|---|
| **who orders** | the customer is on the phone or at the counter; **the staff enters it** | **the customer** orders for themselves |
| **what creates the order** | the staff order screen (`StaffOrder.jsx`) → the **same** `OrderCreate` schema (`order.py:262` — `order_type: str = "dine_in"  # dine_in, takeaway, delivery`) → **the delivery is a first-class order type in the SAME route** | the PWA's own order route → **the same schema** |
| **the order's shape** | `order_type = 'delivery'` + the address + the fee + the leg row | **identical** |
| **the money** | the staff records it → **and the mode is DERIVED today** (`StaffOrder.jsx:769` — `modeFor(settings?.payment_timing, orderType)`) → **so the counter needs its own chooser** (§4.2) | **the customer chooses**: pay online, or cash on delivery (§4.2) |
| **who sees the tracking** | the tenant, on the **Delivery Management** screen | the customer, on the **order status + the map** (§5.2) |
| **the customer member** | **optional** — `dining_customer_id` is **nullable** (`models.py:224`) | **mandatory** — registration is required (§5) |

→ **so it is ONE delivery order type with two doors in** → → **the kitchen, the leg,
the driver, the settlement rule and the reports are the same either way** → → **and
that is why the WhatsApp model works**: a tenant whose customers order by WhatsApp
runs the **counter** path; a tenant whose customers scan the QR runs the **PWA** path
→ **the tenant can run one, the other, or both** (§11.2).

→ **and the ONE difference that matters — the customer's side** → **the PWA order
carries a member** (`dining_customer_id`) → **so the customer gets the map, the live
tracking, the ETA and the history** → → **a counter order has no member**
(`models.py:224` — nullable) → → **so the customer gets NOTHING on their phone** — no
map, no tracking, no history → **the tenant tracks it on the Delivery Management
screen** → → **v1.12 CLOSES THIS: the member is REQUIRED on a delivery** →
**the rule is below** → **and it is one field**: the order already accepts
`dining_customer_id` (`order.py:262`) → **the staff link the member** → **and the
customer gets the tracking link too**.

#### 4.3.1 THE RULE — a delivery order must belong to a customer of the tenant

**Kens's decision (v1.12):** *"1 rules for delivery type must be customer belong to
tenant's customer."* → **stated as an invariant the server enforces:**

> **A delivery order MUST carry a `dining_customer_id` whose member belongs to the
> tenant's own restaurant group. A delivery order with no member is REFUSED. A
> delivery order whose member belongs to another tenant is REFUSED.**

**and the live code gives that sentence three enforceable clauses:**

| # | the clause | the live proof |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **the tenant scope is `customer_id` — NEVER the phone** | the member table is `dining_customers` (`models/loyalty.py:47`) → `customer_id` = "Restaurant group" (`models/loyalty.py:55` — `nullable=False`) → and the unique index is **`(customer_id, phone)`** (`models/loyalty.py:51` — *"Unique constraint: phone must be unique per restaurant (customer_id)"*) → **so the SAME phone number exists in two different tenants** → **so a phone lookup alone could attach ANOTHER tenant's member** → **the member picker at the counter searches scoped by the outlet's `customer_id`** |
| **2** | **the walk-in member is EXCLUDED** | the house excludes it by phone — `AND phone != 'WALKIN'` (`loyalty.py:814-816` — *"the house convention used in vouchers.py and ai/__init__.py"* rather than a hardcoded id) → **and a walk-in has no phone to receive the tracking link and no login** → **so a delivery can never ride the walk-in member** → **the delivery's member search skips it, and the server rejects it** |
| **3** | **the check is the SPLIT-BILL precedent, verbatim** | `order.py:1104-1113` — *"The member has to belong to the same restaurant group as the order — a payer from another tenant would burn another restaurant's points"* → and the refusal is **HTTP 404, "This member does not belong to the restaurant"** → **so the delivery reuses the house's own check and its own wording** |

**What the rule changes:**
- **the delivery becomes the ONE order type where a member is REQUIRED** — dine-in and
  takeaway stay optional (`models.py:224` stays nullable) → **so the POS's own
  validation must branch on `order_type`**: a delivery with no member is refused;
- **the counter's staff screen gains a member step** — the phone lookup, scoped by the
  outlet's `customer_id` (§4.3.1 clause 1), **excluding the walk-in** (clause 2) → **and
  the found member's phone is pre-filled into the address block** → **so the customer
  is identified before the order is submitted**;
- **and now EVERY delivery has a customer side** → **the map, the live tracking, the
  ETA and the history are guaranteed on every delivery order** → → **not a maybe for
  the PWA orders and a maybe for the counter orders — always** → **which is what makes
  the tracking worth building** → **and it gives the PWA order and the counter order
  the SAME customer experience**, which was the whole point of the two doors (§4.3);
- **and the loyalty story closes too**: the delivery's points now land on a real
  member of the tenant — **the same reason the split-bill path guards the payer**
  (`order.py:1104-1113`).

**and the one caveat, stated honestly:** this makes the counter delivery **slower to
enter** — the staff must identify the customer → → **and that is a cost the tenant
accepts**, because the alternative is a delivery the customer can never track.

→ **and the entitlement guards BOTH doors** — the PWA's login gate (§5.1) **and the
counter's Delivery button** (§9.11) → → **one entitlement, two doors, one order**
(§6).

## 5. The customer app — the white-label PWA

**A separate entry point** — **not** a flag inside the existing app (that file is
already large) → **a new `delivery-app/`** sharing the API client and the i18n
— **one codebase, one deployment**.

→ **the customer's nine screens are listed in §3.2 (table B)** — this section is the

### 5.1 The one gate — the delivery app's login

**Kens's rule (Nov 14, 2026):** *we only check, while the customer logs into the
delivery app, whether this tenant has subscribed the delivery features. If the tenant
did not subscribe, the customer login shows the message "sorry this tenant didn't
allow the delivery".*

→ **so the check happens ONCE, at the login, and it is the ONLY plan check in the
whole app** → **everything behind it is the full experience** → **and the plan never
appears again** (§5.3).

**The check is server-side — and it is NOT the existing `require_plan_feature()`.** The
live code says why: `require_plan_feature()` compares the feature name against the
session's stamped list and raises 403 (`auth.py:681`) → **but it returns UNCHECKED for
every caller whose key is not a browser session** (`auth.py:652` — the `key_type !=
"user"` early return) → **and the delivery PWA calls with the public customer key**
(`customer-app/js/app.js:11` — `customer_app_key`) → **so that dependency would wave
the delivery PWA straight through** → **the delivery app needs its own explicit
check** (§6, §9.8).

**What it reads — two facts, both already in the house:**
1. **the entitlement** — the tenant's feature list, read from the website API
   (`plan_features.fetch_plan_features()` → `plan_cache.get_features()`) → **the string
   is the tab name** (`admin.py:1052` hands the reader the `tab_name` under `name`) →
   **the check is `"Delivery" in the list`** → **and the reader is the same one the
   staff app reads** → **one truth, two readers** → **and since v1.9 the row is on
   Enterprise only → so this ONE check IS the Enterprise check** → **a tenant that
   downgrades loses the entitlement, and the login gate refuses it** → **the stale
   `allow_delivery` flags no longer leak delivery** (§3.1, §8 Q17);
2. **the subscription** — the tenant's subscription state
   (`subscription_state.fetch_subscription_state()` →
   `subscription_cache.get_state()`) → **and the house's own decision**
   (`subscription_gate.check_subscription_access()`) → **because the features endpoint
   does NOT check the subscription's status** → it reads the plan's `plan_features`
   rows for the tenant's first subscription row → **so a CANCELLED tenant still shows
   its plan's features as enabled** → **the entitlement alone is not enough** → **the
   gate needs both facts.**

→ **and the per-tenant override counts** — the website API's customer-features
endpoint applies the tenant's own `CustomerFeatureOverride` rows on top of the plan
→ **so a tenant whose admin switched the delivery off is refused even though its plan
carries the row** → **that is the "this tenant didn't allow the delivery" case a plan
can never express.**

**Where the check runs — three places, one helper:**
1. **the login** (`POST /api/delivery/{slug}/auth/login`) → **the customer sees the
   message here**;
2. **the registration** (`POST /api/delivery/{slug}/auth/register`) → **the same
   check** → a customer cannot register against a tenant that does not deliver;
3. **the order create** → **re-checked** → **and this one is not optional**: the guest
   token lives **30 days** (`guest_session.py` — `GUEST_TOKEN_TTL_DAYS = 30`) →
   **so a customer who logged in before the tenant lapsed would otherwise keep
   ordering for a month** → **the login gate is the UX; the order gate is the truth.**
4. **the COUNTER** → **the staff screen's Delivery button asks the SAME feature list**
   (§9.11) → **and the order create re-checks** → → **so the entitlement guards BOTH
   doors** — the PWA and the counter (§4.3).

→ **and the outlet's own switch is the third thing** — the tenant must have allowed
delivery **at that outlet** (`outlet_settings.allow_delivery`) → **so a customer may
log in to a tenant that has the delivery but reach an outlet that does not take
delivery orders** → **that is the `outlet_not_delivering` message** (§3.1) → **the two
levels stay distinct**: the **Enterprise plan** says the tenant HAS the delivery; the
**outlet flag** says THIS branch takes delivery orders.

**The messages** — the server returns a **machine code** and the app renders the
**text** (the PWA is multi-lingual → the sentence belongs in the app's i18n):

| the fact | the code | what the customer sees |
|---|---|---|
| the tenant has no delivery entitlement | `delivery_not_allowed` | **"Sorry, this tenant didn't allow the delivery."** |
| the tenant's subscription is cancelled / expired / overdue | `delivery_not_subscribed` | **"Sorry, this tenant didn't allow the delivery."** (Kens's one message) |
| the outlet's `allow_delivery` is off | `outlet_not_delivering` | **"This branch is not accepting delivery orders right now."** (the outlet's own switch — §3.1) |
| the tenant IS entitled | — | the app opens → the full journey (§5.2) |

**And a technical failure is fail-OPEN, like the rest of the house** — if the website
API cannot be read, the plan cache keeps the **stale** list (the house rule: *a stale
plan is better than locking a paying customer out* — `plan_cache.py`) → and only a cold
cache with an unreachable API gives `None` → **then the storefront opens and logs
loudly** → **the order-create check is the backstop** (above).

### 5.2 The journey — the full app, in order

**One code path. Every step is the same for every entitled tenant.**

|---|---|---|---|

**And the order history** — the customer's own — the `dining_customer_id` already

### 5.3 One code path — the rule that makes it simple

**The delivery app has no plan branches.** Every entitled tenant's customer gets the
same screens, the same fields, the same choices. The plan is asked **once**, at the
login (§5.1), and never again.

→ **and since v1.9 that is no longer a rule we have to hold — it is a FACT**: only
ONE plan carries the delivery → **there is exactly ONE configuration of the delivery
app in the world** → **nothing to compare, nothing to branch on** → → **and the rule
survives for the future**: if a second plan ever carries the delivery, the customer

**So the tenant's CONFIGURATION — not the tenant's plan — decides what the customer
sees:**
- the **fee** comes from the outlet's delivery settings (§3) → **every tenant that has
  the delivery runs the SAME app**; they just price it differently;
- the **postcode zones** change the tenant's FEE MODEL — the customer still
  sees one "delivery fee" line either way → **no extra screen, no extra field, no
  locked anything**;
- the **analytics** (Enterprise) are the tenant's own reports → **they are not in the
  customer's app at all.**

→ **and that is the test: if a tenant's plan can change a customer-facing screen, the
design is wrong** → **the plan may only change the tenant's back office** (§1).

**The white-label rule** — **the tenant's branding comes from the outlet settings**
(the logo, the colour, the name — **already stored**) → **no per-tenant build,
no per-tenant deployment**.

**And the delivery app REQUIRES a registered member of the tenant.** Kens's rule: *the
customer must register and belong to the tenant customer before they can order* →
→ **so the delivery checkout is gated on the member's token** (the guest token —
`guest_session.py:51`) → **and the order carries `dining_customer_id`** (the column
exists — `models.py:224`) → **so the points follow the right member and the customer
gets an order history.**

→ **and the server must check the member belongs to the tenant** — the order create
takes a `dining_customer_id` (`order.py:262` — the `OrderCreate` schema) → **the same
check the split-bill path already does** (the payer must belong to the order's
restaurant group — `order.py:1104-1113`) → **the delivery order create copies it** →
**no member of another tenant can ever be attached to a delivery order.**

→ **what this changes for the tenant:** the tenant's existing walk-in customers become
**members** (with the tenant's loyalty) → **and that is exactly why the tenant shares
the QR with them** (§3) → **the QR is the onboarding funnel, not a poster.**

→ **and one setting decides whether the tenant can take delivery orders at all** —
registration is refused while the outlet's `customer_registration_enabled` is off
(`models.py:483`; the refusal — `loyalty.py:335-343`) → **the Delivery tab surfaces it
(§3).**

→ **and the tenant's own customers are the tenant's business, not ours** — the
customer's phone number is the tenant's to keep → **the QR is the tenant's own channel
to their own customers** → **we are the storefront, not the owner of the
relationship.**

**And the delivery prepay** — the customer can **submit and prepay** → the same
payment path as the in-venue PWA (the QR, the poll) → **but a delivery prepay is
`prepay_online`** → **so it needs a connected payment account and a Malaysian
outlet** (§4) → **otherwise the delivery is cash on delivery**.

   → **the fix is a server-side check, not a client-side one** → `plan_features.py`

   **one truth, two readers**.
   → **the string it compares is the tab name** (`admin.py:1052` hands the reader the

   → **and v1.8 moved the check to ONE place — the delivery app's LOGIN** (§5.1) →

   → **and v1.11 adds the SECOND door** — the **counter** → **the staff screen's

4. **does the UI agree?** → the tenant's plan, the nav, the PWA's visibility.

## 8. The open questions

1. **Cash on delivery — the default?** — my pick: **allowed** (the tenant's driver
   collects) but **the tenant can require prepay** (`prepaid_only`) — my
   default: **allowed** (the Malaysian market expects it).
2. **The zones — by postcode or by area name?** — **the default is a radius** (one
   number, no geocode on the hot path) and **the managed alternative is the postcodes**
   (the customer types one → the fee) → **the zones became a table** (a managed list)
   → **and both sit inside the one Enterprise module** → **so this is a choice the
   tenant makes, not a choice the plan makes.**
3. **Does the delivery order carry a separate fee line?** — my pick: **yes** (a
   delivery-fee line item) → the invoice and the e-Invoice stay clean.
4. **Do the tenants' customers get an account?** — **yes, and the v1.5 answer is
   firmer** — the `dining_customer_id` already exists → **reuse it** → and the
   sign-up **derives the tenant from the slug** (§10.3).
5. **Is the delivery PWA its own domain or a path?** — my pick: **a path**
   (`/d/<slug>`) → **one deployment, one certificate** — a custom domain later
   .
6. **Does the delivery order need its own kitchen screen?** — my pick: **no** — the same
   kitchen (a restaurant's delivery food is made in the same kitchen).
7. **Who checks the plan on the PWA's own routes?** — **the server does, and since
   v1.8 it does it in ONE place: the delivery app's LOGIN** (§5.1) → **the tenant's
   feature list** (the tab name `"Delivery"`) **AND the tenant's subscription state**
   → **NOT `require_plan_feature()`** — it returns unchecked for every non-`user`
   caller (`auth.py:652`) and the delivery PWA calls with the public customer key →
   → **a tenant without the entitlement cannot even log in** → it sees **"sorry this
   tenant didn't allow the delivery"** → **and the client is never the one to say so.**
8. **Where does the storefront slug live — the outlet or the tenant?** — **the
   outlet** → because the branding (`restaurant_name`, `restaurant_logo`,
   `brand_color`), the menu and the switch are all **per outlet**
   (`outlet_settings`) → the tenant is derived from the outlet → **one storefront
   and one QR per branch**, one tenant behind them.
9. **The driver roster — a table or two fields on the order?** — **a table**
   (`delivery_drivers`) → **Kens's comment changed this**: a list the tenant
   *maintains* cannot live in two fields → and the order keeps a **snapshot** of the
   name and the phone, so the history survives an edit.
10. **The coverage area — a radius or a polygon?** — **a radius by default** (one
    number) and **the postcode zones** as the managed alternative → a polygon needs a
    map editor and a geocoder per vertex → **not yet** → **and the zones change the
    tenant's FEE model, never a customer-facing screen** — the customer still sees one
    delivery-fee line either way (§5.3).
11. **Where does the driver's live location come from?** — **three options** (§11) →
    my pick: **the driver link** for every entitled tenant, the tenant's own tracker
    for the tenants who have one, and **no tracking** when the drivers will not tap
    anything → **the honest risk is the driver's cooperation, not the provider** →
    **and it is no longer a tier question** (§11.1).
12. **Which map and routing provider?** — **Geoapify for the geocoding (already
    integrated), Mapbox for the route, the ETA and the tiles** → §11.
13. **Is the live driver marker on the free tier?** — **the question is dead twice
    over** → **v1.8 made the whole delivery app one entitlement** (the map, the ETA and
    the live marker all ship with it) → **and v1.9 put the entitlement on
    Enterprise only** → **so there is no free tier to ask about** → **the honest risk
    stays the driver's cooperation** (§11.2).
14. **Does the driver's link write the money?** — **no** → the leg is the driver's, the
    money is the staff's (§12) → the driver may claim the cash they took, the staff
    record it.
15. **Is a COD delivery's settlement the money or the handover?** — **both, and they
    may arrive in either order** → one delivery-aware rule (§4.1) → the end state is
    the same either way.
16. **Does the customer have to register before a delivery order?** — **yes** (Kens) →
    so the order carries `dining_customer_id` → and the tenant's registration switch
    must be on (§3, §5).
17. **What happens to the delivery flag when the plan lapses?** — **the downgrade case
    is CLOSED** (Kens: **plan downgrade is not allowed today**) → **so the live case is
    the subscription LAPSING** — cancelled, expired or overdue → **the entitlement
    follows the subscription, and the login gate refuses** (§5.1) → **and nothing
    resets `allow_delivery`** (no `allow_delivery` anywhere in `website_api/`) → **but
    a tenant that has lost the entitlement cannot see the tab to switch its flag off**
    → **do we warn the tenant?** → **the clean fix is a nightly sweep** that turns the
    flag off wherever the entitlement is gone (the tenant sees it the next morning,
    and the storefront never sells a delivery it cannot deliver).
18. **A customer who logged in BEFORE the tenant lapsed?** — **the guest token lives
    30 days** (`guest_session.py` — `GUEST_TOKEN_TTL_DAYS = 30`) → **so the login gate
    alone is a 30-day hole** → **the order-create route re-checks the same
    entitlement** (§5.1) → **the login gate is the UX; the order gate is the truth.**
19. **The delivery is Enterprise-only — so what IS the Starter's hook, and is the
    delivery affordable at the Enterprise price?** — **a commercial question, not a
    design one** → **the Starter loses the delivery's viral loop** (the tenant's own
    customers order → the tenant sees the orders → the upgrade) → **and the Starter
    needs a different hook** → **and the delivery's own running cost (the Mapbox
    bill — ~$30 a month at 500 orders a day) now lands on the plan that is priced to
    carry it** → **which is the first time the delivery's economics actually line up
    with its price** (§1, §11).
20. **The zones and the analytics now sit on Enterprise with everything else — do they
    still earn their own rows?** — **the house says yes** (Inventory has eight rows,
    all Enterprise — one per surface) → **and they keep the door open**: if a cheaper
    plan ever carries the delivery, the zones and the analytics are the rows that
    differentiate it → **but until then they are permission rows, not upsells** →
    **keep them, or collapse the module to one row?** (§9.9).
21. **Which plan carries the delivery — and can we change it later?** — **the assignment
    is DATA** (`plan_features`), so **yes, we can change it at any time** and nothing in
    the code changes → **the default is one plan (Enterprise — the Inventory
    precedent)** → **the rule is that no code may ask "is this tenant on plan X?" for
    the delivery** — it asks the plan list for the **tab name** (§5.1) → → **so the
    flexibility is free, as long as we keep asking the feature list** (§9.10).
22. **A counter-entered delivery — does the customer get the map and the live
    tracking?** → **ANSWERED in v1.12 — YES, and the member is REQUIRED.** Kens's
    decision: *"1 rules for delivery type must be customer belong to tenant's
    customer."* → **a delivery order MUST carry a `dining_customer_id` whose member
    belongs to the tenant's restaurant group** → **and the live code gives that
    sentence three clauses**: the scope is **`customer_id`, never the phone** (the
    unique index is `(customer_id, phone)` — `models/loyalty.py:51` — so the same
    phone exists in two tenants) → the **walk-in is excluded** (`phone != 'WALKIN'` —
    `loyalty.py:814-816`) → and the check is the **split-bill precedent**
    (`order.py:1104-1113` — 404 *"This member does not belong to the restaurant"*) →
    **so the delivery becomes the one order type where a member is mandatory** →
    **and every delivery therefore has the map, the live tracking, the ETA and the
    history** (§4.3.1, §9.12)
23. **At the counter, who picks the payment mode?** — **today nobody picks**: the staff
    screen **derives** it (`StaffOrder.jsx:769` — `modeFor(settings?.payment_timing,
    orderType)`) → **so a counter delivery is always the outlet's single configured
    mode** → **but the PWA lets the customer choose** among the outlet's allowed modes
    (§4.2) → → **the same allow-list, two choosers** → **the staff get a chooser for
    the delivery type too, or the counter stays on the outlet's single mode?**
    (§4.2, §4.3)

  entitlement check runs here too** (§5.1) → **a customer cannot register against a
  tenant that does not deliver**;
- `POST /api/delivery/{slug}/auth/login` → the same resolution → **the entitlement

> **the review found a real trap here** — the loyalty register derives the tenant

→ **after the login** — the customer is a `customers` row of that tenant → the order

### 10.4 The status flow, in one picture

```
the customer places the order ──────────────────────────────► pending
the staff confirms ──────────────────────────────────────────► confirmed
the staff starts the kitchen ────────────────────────────────► preparing
the kitchen bags it ─────────────────────────────────────────► ready
the staff assigns a driver (the leg) ────────────────────────► leg: assigned
the driver takes it (the leg) ───────────────────────────────► leg: picked_up
the driver hands it over (the leg) ──────────────────────────► leg: delivered
the core status moves with the handover ─────────────────────► served
the money is recorded (the postpay write) ───────────────────► completed
```

→ **one line of the core machine changed** (`ready → served` is the handover) →
**and the leg is four values in a table the POS does not own.**

## 11. The map, the routing and the ETA — the provider decision

**The needs — and they are different problems:**

| The need | The call | The provider |
|---|---|---|
| **the address → the coordinates** | one per order | **Geoapify** (already integrated) |
| **the coordinates → a place** (the address check) | one per order | **Geoapify** (already integrated) |
| **is this address inside our coverage?** | a travel-time area | **Geoapify Isochrone API** (Enterprise) |
| **the route + the ETA** | one per order | **Mapbox Directions** |
| **the map the customer looks at** | the tiles | **Mapbox GL JS** |
| **the customer's MAP, the route and the ETA** | one route request per order | **Mapbox** — the tiles + Directions (**with the entitlement — §11.1**) |
| **the live driver MARKER** | the driver's own phone | **the driver link** (**with the entitlement — it is no longer a tier — §11.1**) |
| **the driver's raw GPS → a road** | the map matching | **Mapbox Map Matching** |
| **the driver → the customer** (the turn-by-turn) | **nothing** | the driver uses their own phone |

**The recommendation — a hybrid, and the reason for each part:**
- **Geoapify stays the geocoder** → it is **already integrated in two places**
  (`outlet_settings.py:359-362` — the outlet's own geocode; `competitors.py:707`,
  `731`, `763`, `778` — the reverse geocode, the search and the places) → **the
  marginal cost is zero for a small tenant** (the free plan: **3,000 credits a day**
  — a tenant doing 500 orders a day spends 500) → **and its Isochrone API answers the
  coverage question better than a circle on a map** (a 15-minute travel area, not a
  5 km radius) → **it is the one place where the existing integration pays off**;
- **Mapbox is added for the route, the ETA and the map** → the thing Geoapify's
  routing cannot do is the **live traffic** (its routing is open-data → **the ETA is
  optimistic exactly at the peak hours, when the customer is watching**) → Mapbox
  Directions is **$2 per 1,000 requests** → and the map itself is inside the free
  tier (**100,000 map loads a month**);
- **no Google** → the Routes API is the best traffic data in the market, but it is
  billed **per kilometre**, it comes with a heavier commercial setup and display
  constraints, and its place search we already have → **it is a "later, at scale"
  answer, not a "now" answer**;
- **no HERE** → the strongest commercial-fleet routing in the market, at roughly
  **$3 per 1,000** → our drivers are cars and motorbikes, not trucks → **overkill**;
- **no self-hosted OSRM (yet)** → it is free, but it has **no traffic** → the ETA is
  wrong at the peak → and it is infrastructure we run → **worth revisiting when the
  volume makes the per-call cost visible** (at 100k orders a month, Mapbox Directions
  is about $200 a month for the whole platform).

**The cost, at 500 orders a day** (one tenant):
- the geocodes: 500 a day → **inside the free Geoapify plan** (3,000 credits a day);
- the routes: 500 a day = 15,000 a month → **about $30 a month** (Mapbox Directions,
  $2/1,000);
- the map loads: the customer's tracking page → **inside the free tier**;
- → **the live tracking costs about the price of a coffee per order** → **and since
  v1.9 only ENTERPRISE tenants carry it** → **so the bill lands on the plan that is
  priced to carry it** (~$30 a month at 500 orders a day) → **and the free Starter no
  longer carries the map bill at all** → **which closes the cost question v1.8
  opened** (§8 Q19).

### 11.1 The map AND the live tracking are CORE — one entitlement, one experience

**Kens's rule (Nov 14, 2026):** *the delivery app must contain the full features …
and the whole time the order is in the delivery status, the customer must see the
full map and the live tracking.*

→ **so the split v1.6 drew (the map/ETA free, the MARKER paid) is gone** → **the map,
the route, the ETA and the live driver marker all ship with the delivery
entitlement** → **on every Enterprise tenant that has it** → **because a
customer-facing screen may not be tiered** (§5.3).

| the thing | does it need the driver's phone? | what it costs | the tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the map, the route and the ETA** | **no** | one Mapbox Directions request per order (~$2 per 1,000 → ~$30 a month at 500 orders a day) | **the entitlement** — every Enterprise tenant with the delivery on |
| **the live driver MARKER** (the dot moving on the map) | **yes** | the driver's phone + the pings + the privacy work | **the entitlement** — the same row, no extra tier |

→ **the ETA without GPS is honest, not fake** → the route is the **outlet → customer**
route (`Mapbox Directions`) → the driver starts at the outlet, so the route is accurate
**the moment the driver is assigned** → and from `picked_up` the ETA counts down by the
elapsed time → **the customer sees a shrinking arrival window.**

→ **and the marker is honest too** — it moves only while the driver is sharing a
location (§11.2) → **a tenant whose drivers will not tap anything gets the map and
the ETA, and no dot** → **that is a cooperation problem, not a plan problem.**

→ **and it corrects the commercial line in §1** → it is no longer "no driver GPS on
the free tier" → **it is "no commission and no driver marketplace"** → **the delivery
app is the adoption hook, and the tenant's own depth is the upsell.**

→ **the cost is the same Mapbox bill as before** — the difference is that it is now
**spread across every tenant** → at 500 orders a day per tenant it is ~$30 a month →
**so the free Starter carries it too** → **which is a pricing decision, not a design
one** (§8 Q19).

### 11.2 The live tracking needs a location source — and that is the real question
There
is no GPS without somebody reporting it:
1. **the driver link** (my pick) — a **one-page web page** the driver opens on their
   own phone: no install, no account, three taps (Start / Arrived / Delivered) and
   it posts the location while the order is on the road → **it is not a driver app**
   (nothing to install, nothing to publish) → **the tenant's drivers must agree to
   use it** → **and it is the SAME link that carries the leg's buttons** (§12) → **one
   link, two jobs — and the POS still works with no link at all**;
2. **the tenant's own tracker** — a tenant that already runs a fleet system posts the
   position to a webhook → **we integrate, we do not replace it**;
3. **no tracking** — the status timeline, the driver's name and phone, the ETA →
   **the WhatsApp model** → **and it is what every tenant falls back to when its
   drivers will not tap anything**.

→ **the honest risk is not technical** — a tenant whose drivers will not tap anything
**cannot be tracked**, whatever provider we buy → **so the tracking is a workflow,
not a map** → **the map is the cheap part.**

**The privacy — this is not a small footnote.** The driver's location and the
customer's address are **personal data** (the Malaysia PDPA; the GDPR for the tenants
that operate in the EU):
1. **the customer's address** → it lives in the leg and the order → it is **not** in
   the customer-facing tracking response **after the handover**;
2. **the driver's location** → the pings are **dropped 24 hours after the delivery**
   (the `delivery_pings` table is pruned by the nightly job) → **the pings are never
   part of the order record** (they are a separate table with their own life);
3. **the access** → the tenant's staff see **their own** orders only (the tenant
   scope on every route) → the customer sees **their own order only** (the order
   token / `dining_customer_id`);
4. **the driver** → the driver is tracked too → **the tenant must tell its own
   drivers** (the driver link says so) → **the driver's consent is the tenant's to
   obtain, not ours**;
5. **the map tiles** → the provider's terms require the attribution → the attribution
   goes on the tracking page.

**And the key** — the existing code falls back to a **hardcoded Geoapify key**
(`outlet_settings.py:359`, `competitors.py:707` and `763` — the literal is in the
source and it is **not repeated here**) → **that fallback must not survive** → the key
comes from the environment (a SecretRef) → **and the code must fail loudly when it is
missing** → a silent fallback to a shared key is how a bill arrives from nowhere.

## 12. The driver's write — the POS, the link, and what neither may do

**The question:** *how does the driver mark the delivery successful — through our
frontend POS, or another tool?*

**The answer: both — with one rule about what each of them may write.**

**The split — the leg is the driver's, the money is the staff's:**

| the write | who may make it | why |
|---|---|---|
| the leg (`assigned` → `picked_up` → `delivered`) | **the POS staff** *and* **the driver link** | it is the physical truth of the delivery — only the driver and the shop know it |
| the handover (`leg = 'delivered'` → the core moves) | **the server**, in the same write | the server owns the shared machine — it is the only place the status can be trusted |
| **the money** (`payment_status = 'paid'`) | **the POS staff only** | it is the tenant's money → the write already sits behind `staff:orders:write` (`order.py:1013`) → **a driver must never be able to mark money in** |
| the cash **claim** ("I took RM 12.50") | the POS staff **and** the driver link | a claim is a fact about the handover, not about the ledger → it sits on the leg → the staff reconcile it |

→ **so the driver can say "delivered, I took RM 12.50"** → **and the staff still write
the money** → **the tenant's cash flow is unchanged** → **and a COD delivery's two
endings still land in either order** (§4.1).

**Surface A — the POS (every Enterprise tenant that has the delivery, no new build).** The **Delivery Management** screen
(§10.2) carries the leg's buttons: **Assign driver** → **Out for delivery** →
**Delivered** → the same buttons, the same queue, the same server rules as today →
**a tenant whose driver is also its staff needs nothing else** → **and this is the
surface that still works when the driver will not tap anything** (the WhatsApp model).

**Surface B — the driver link (the second surface).** A **one-page web page**, not an
app: the driver opens a link on their own phone and taps three buttons → **nothing to
install, nothing to publish, no app store** → **and it is the same link that carries
the live pings** (§11.2) → **one link, two jobs.**

→ **the link's token is per order, scoped, signed and expiring** — a new claim set
next to the guest token (`guest_session.py:51` — the pattern: a signed token that
names what it may do) → **never the PWA's public key** → **a leaked link moves ONE
order's leg, and it expires.**

→ **what the link may NOT do** (the boundary, stated once):
- **it may not write money** — no `payment_status`, no payment row;
- **it may not write the core status directly** — the server moves the core in the same
  write as the leg (that is where the shared machine's rules live);
- **it may not touch the items, the prices, the fee or the address** — the order's money
  was settled at the checkout;
- **it may not cancel** — a cancellation is a staff decision (and the existing rule
  stops at `preparing` anyway — `order.py:1287`).

**Surface C — what we do NOT build.** A driver **app** (§2's boundary) → the link is
the app.

**The live cost of Surface B.** The link only works if the driver opens it → **the
honest risk is the driver's cooperation, not the provider** (§11.2) → → **so Surface A
must be complete on its own** → **it is not a fallback: it is the primary, and the link
is the convenience.**
