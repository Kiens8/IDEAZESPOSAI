# Subscription Lifecycle and Access

**Date:** November 2, 2026  
**Status:** Proposed — awaiting approval  
**Scope:** the website API (the billing system) and the POS login gate  
**Files:** `website_api/app/routes/subscription.py`, `website_api/app/routes/admin.py`, `website_api/scripts/send_notifications.py`, `backend/app/routes/auth.py`

---

## Overview

A restaurant may use the POS when its **subscription says it may**. The plan
lives in the website API (the billing system's database); the POS backend asks
it at every login and refuses the login when the answer is no.

The intent is simple and correct:

> **When the paid period ends and the subscription is not renewed, the user
> must not be able to sign in to the POS again.**

The renewal design says the same thing about the cancellation, in one line:

> *"Cancellation: status → 'cancelled' immediately (**access until period
> end**)"*

So the design is already right — **the POS login gate is what does not
implement it.** This document describes the current behaviour, the
disagreements between the billing system and the POS, and the model that
fixes them.

**The grace period is a setting, not a constant** (Kens, November 2): it is
the admin app's `payment_due_days` — 14 today, changeable by the admin, read
live by the code that needs it. Nothing in this document introduces a second
grace.

## How it works today

### The stored state

`subscriptions` (the website API's database):

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `status` | `active` · `trial` · `cancelled` · `expired` |
| `payment_status` | `not_required` · `pending` · `paid` · `failed` |
| `current_period_end` | when the paid period ends |
| `trial_ends_at` | when the trial ends |
| `cancelled_at` | when the customer cancelled |
| `payment_due_date` | when a "pay later" invoice is due |

`invoices` carry their own `status` (`pending` / `paid` / `forgiven`) and
`due_date`.

### The daily cron (09:00, `send_notifications.py`)

Runs in this order:

1. `mark_zero_invoices_paid` — a $0 invoice (a trial, a free plan) counts as paid
2. `process_trial_end` — a trial that ended gets its first paid invoice; **the status stays `trial` until it is paid**; the invoice is due at `trial_ends_at + grace`
3. `generate_renewal_invoices` — an **active** subscription whose `current_period_end` has passed gets its next invoice (due at `period_end + grace`, the grace comes from the setting `payment_due_days`, 14 by default) and the period rolls forward
4. `check_expired_subscriptions` — a pending invoice past its due date → the invoice is forgiven and the subscription becomes **`expired`**
5. `check_cancelled_subscriptions` — a cancelled subscription past its period end → **it only prints a line**
6. the notices (the trial-ending notices, the billing reminders, the final reminders)

### The login gate (the POS backend, `backend/app/routes/auth.py`)

At login the POS backend calls the website API's
`GET /api/admin/customer/{id}/subscription` and applies:

| # | Condition | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | no subscription, or `status == "none"` | **blocked** — "No active subscription found" |
| 2 | `status == "cancelled"` | **blocked** — "Your subscription has been cancelled" |
| 3 | `status == "expired"` | **blocked** — "Your subscription has expired" |
| 4 | `payment_status == "overdue"` (a pending invoice past its due date) | **blocked** — "Account suspended due to overdue payment" |
| 5 | a `trial` whose `trial_ends_at` has passed | **blocked** — "Your trial period has ended" |
| 6 | a `pending` payment past its due date | **blocked** — "Payment is overdue" |
| 7 | a trial ending within 3 days, a payment due soon | a **warning**, the login goes through |
| — | the website API cannot be reached | **allowed with a warning** (fail-open — availability wins) ✓ |

So the end-of-period path today is:

```
period ends → the renewal invoice is created (14-day grace)
             → the subscription stays "active" and the login works
             → the invoice is unpaid after the grace
             → the cron sets "expired"
             → the login is blocked  ✓
```

**That part is right.** Both live tenants are healthy: Demo until 2027-09-17,
AES until 2026-11-30 (its renewal invoice lands on the 30th, the grace runs to
December 14).

## The four disagreements

### 1. A cancellation cuts the access off immediately, not at the end of the period

`POST /cancel` sets `status = "cancelled"` the moment the customer clicks.
The login gate blocks on `cancelled` → **instant lockout**. But the route's
own docstring, its response body and its cancellation email all say:

> "Subscription cancelled. Access continues until period end."

The two halves of the product disagree with each other. A customer who
cancels on day 20 of a 30-day period loses the ten days they have already
paid for.

### 2. The trial: the POS matches the design, the code does not

The renewal design says:

> *"On trial end: status stays 'trial', first paid invoice created. **Invoice
> due immediately.**"*

The login gate does exactly that — condition 5 blocks any trial whose
`trial_ends_at` has passed ✓ — so **the POS is right here**.

The mismatch is inside the billing code: `process_trial_end()` creates the
first paid invoice with `due_date = period_start + grace` (14 days by the
setting), while the design says the invoice is **due immediately**. So the
customer is billed with 14 days to pay but locked out of the POS on the
trial-end day.

This one is a **decision, not a bug in the POS**: either the trial invoice is
due immediately (the design, and then the trial block at the POS is right) or
the trial gets the same grace as everything else (and then the login gate has
to honour it). The proposal follows the design — due immediately — and keeps
the gate as it is.

### 3. The status is only checked at login

The subscription is read once, when the user signs in. An open session keeps
working for up to **12 hours** after a cancellation or an expiry.

This is the same weakness the plan features had until November 2 (see
`ai-feature-gating.mdx`): the entitlement was a snapshot. The features now
come from a pushed cache — the subscription status still does not.

### 4. A cancelled subscription never becomes "expired"

`check_cancelled_subscriptions` finds the cancelled subscriptions past their
period end and **only prints a line**. The status stays `cancelled` forever,
so the "expired" reporting and the admin's own state never see those tenants.

### Also worth knowing: cancelling does not cancel the Stripe subscription

`POST /cancel` never calls Stripe. The webhook *does* handle
`customer.subscription.deleted` ✓ — but nothing ever cancels the Stripe
subscription, so the event never fires and **the customer keeps being
charged** after cancelling in the app. That is a money bug rather than an
access bug, but it belongs to the same feature.

## The proposed model

### One place decides access

A single function computes the tenant's **effective access** from the stored
state. The login gate asks it; the cron asks it; the admin panel can show it.
Nobody decides on their own.

```python
def effective_access(subscription, invoices, now) -> Access:
    """active | trial | grace | blocked - and the reason why."""
```

The rules:

| Stored state | Effective access | Why |
|---|---|---|
| no subscription | **blocked** | they never subscribed |
| `expired` | **blocked** | the grace ran out |
| `cancelled`, `current_period_end > now` | **active** + a warning ("your plan ends on the 30th") | they paid for the period |
| `cancelled`, `current_period_end <= now` | **blocked** | the period they paid for is over |
| `trial`, `trial_ends_at > now` | **trial** (+ a warning near the end) | the trial is running |
| `trial`, `trial_ends_at <= now`, the first invoice not yet due | **blocked** (the design: the trial invoice is due immediately) — or **grace**, if the trial is given the admin's grace | the decision in finding 2 |
| `active`, a pending invoice not yet due | **active** + a warning | the renewal is pending |
| `active`, a pending invoice past its due date | **grace** until the grace ends, then **blocked** | the grace is `payment_due_days` |
| `active`, everything paid | **active** | ✓ |

**One grace rule for everything, and it is the admin's setting.** The grace
period is `payment_due_days` from the admin app's settings (14 today) — read
live, never hardcoded. The renewal grace and the trial grace are the same
value, measured from the moment the period ends. Today the POS honours it for
the renewal and not for the trial.

### The cancellation records the intent, the state stays honest

No schema change is needed:

- the cancellation keeps writing `status = "cancelled"` and `cancelled_at` ✓
- the **effective** access reads `current_period_end` → so the access lasts
  until the period ends ✓
- the cron, after the period end, flips the cancelled subscriptions to
  **`expired`** ✓ (the missing step, and it makes the reporting correct)

The Stripe subscription is cancelled with `at_period_end=True` ✓ → the
customer is not charged again, and the billing stops at the same moment the
access stops.

### The status is checked live, like the features

The same pushed-cache pattern as the plan features:

- the website API pushes `POST /internal/subscription-changed/{customer_id}`
  after every state change (cancel, resume, upgrade, the Stripe webhook, the
  cron)
- the POS backend keeps a small cache of the subscription state per tenant
- the login gate reads the cache ✓ → an open session is affected within
  seconds, not within 12 hours

The login gate stays the place where the decision is **enforced** — the cache
only tells it what the truth is now.

| Phase | Work | Gate to the next phase |

| 5 | the pushed subscription cache in the POS backend | an open session is affected within seconds ✓ |

Each phase is independently revertable.

## The verification

### The login matrix

| Tenant state | The login |
|---|---|
| an active subscription, everything paid | **allowed** ✓ |
| an active subscription, the renewal invoice pending, inside the grace | **allowed** + a warning ✓ |
| an active subscription, the invoice past its due date | **allowed** during the grace, **blocked** after it ✓ |
| a cancelled subscription, before the period end | **allowed** + a warning ("your plan ends on the 30th") ✓ |
| a cancelled subscription, after the period end | **blocked** ✓ |
| a trial, before it ends | **allowed** + a warning near the end ✓ |
| a trial, after it ends, the first invoice not yet due | **allowed** (the grace) ✓ |
| a trial, after it ends, the invoice past its due date | **blocked** ✓ |
| an expired subscription | **blocked** ✓ |
| no subscription | **blocked** ✓ |
| the website API unreachable | **allowed** + a warning ✓ (fail-open) |

### The regression

- the login matrix, exercised against the two live tenants and a test tenant
- the cron, run by hand: `python3 scripts/send_notifications.py --renewal`,
  `--trial-end`, `--check-expired` → the states end where the table says
- the POS route sweep → `routes with problems: 0 / 24`
- the browser check per role → no console errors

## The risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| A customer loses access earlier than intended | the effective access is **never stricter** than the stored state today — every change makes it *more* permissive for the cancellation and trial cases |
| The grace becomes a free window | one rule, measured from the moment the period ends, and the trial case now matches the billing system's own design |
| The live cache locks someone out on a technicality | the cache falls back to the last known state, and the login gate keeps its fail-open behaviour when the website API is unreachable |
| The Stripe change double-charges or stops the billing wrongly | the cancellation is `at_period_end=True` — the billing stops exactly when the access ends ✓ |

## Open decisions

1. **The trial invoice** — due immediately (the renewal design) or with the
   admin's grace (`payment_due_days`)? The proposal: **due immediately**,
   which keeps the login gate as it is today.
2. **A cancelled tenant that resumes** — the resume route restores `active`
   but not `current_period_end` (which may already be in the past). The
   proposal: the resume sets a fresh period from today and clears
   `cancelled_at`.

**Already decided**

- **The grace period** — the admin app's setting `payment_due_days` (14
  today), read live. One value for the trial and the renewal ✓
- **The warning period** — the admin app's setting `overdue_warning_days`
  (7 today), the same window the billing reminders already use ✓
- **The cancellation** — the status changes at once, the **access lasts until
  the period end** ✓ (the renewal design says so; the POS gate does not)
