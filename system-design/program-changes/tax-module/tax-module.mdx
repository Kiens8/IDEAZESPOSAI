# SaaS Tax Module — the shared contract

> **Date:** November 27, 2026
> **Status:** Proposal — not implemented
> **Author:** Lumi

This folder is the proposal for the **SaaS subscription tax module**: the service tax
(SST) that we charge on our own subscriptions, plus the service period that every
invoice has to state.

It is deliberately **three separate proposals**, because the three surfaces change for
different reasons and must not be shipped as one undifferentiated "tax module":

| Document | Surface | What changes there |
|---|---|---|
| [website-api-changes](./website-api-changes) | `website_api` (FastAPI) | the data model, the tax engine, the invoice factory, the API contracts |
| [admin-app-changes](./admin-app-changes) | `admin` (React) | the money views, the invoice list, the reports, the tax settings |
| [site-app-portal-changes](./site-app-portal-changes) | `site` (Next.js) | the prices, the checkout, the portal billing, the printable invoice, the three locales |

This document holds only what the three surfaces **share**: the vocabulary, the tax
treatment state machine, the service-period rule, the threshold monitor, the rollout,
and the questions that a Malaysian accountant has to answer before any of it is built.

---

## 1. Why this is a design proposal and not a task

Because the tax treatment is not yet decided. Three things are genuinely open and they
are **not ours to decide**:

1. **Are we a registered person, and from when?**
   SST registration is required when the value of taxable services supplied exceeds
   **RM500,000 in any 12-month period**. Below that we must not charge SST to anyone.
   The registration date decides which invoices carry tax and which do not.
2. **Is the Cambodian tenant's subscription an export of services, and is it exempt?**
   This changes *what we charge* (nothing vs 8%) **and** *whether that revenue counts
   toward the RM500,000 threshold* — the exemption question decides the threshold
   question.
3. **Are our prices tax-inclusive or tax-exclusive?**
   This changes every number the customer sees, the promo logic, and the refund math.

So the module is designed so that these are **data with an effective date and a named
source**, entered by an administrator or an accountant — never hard-coded, never guessed
at request time. The engine reads a treatment and applies it; the treatment comes from
data.

---

## 2. The vocabulary

| Term | Meaning in this design |
|---|---|
| **Taxable service** | a supply of IT/SAAS services that is subject to SST once we are registered |
| **Net (subtotal)** | the price of the service **before** service tax |
| **Tax** | the SST charged on that net amount |
| **Gross (total)** | net + tax — **this is what the customer pays** |
| **Tax treatment** | the rule that decided how this particular invoice is taxed |
| **Service period** | the dates the service is provided for — the period the money is *for*; snapshot onto the invoice as `period_start` / `period_end` |
| **Tax liability** | the tax we collected and owe to the government — **not revenue** |
| **Revenue** | the net amount — what the subscription actually earned |

The last two are the whole reason this module exists, and they are the reason the
reporting change in [admin-app-changes](./admin-app-changes) is not optional.

---

## 3. The tax treatment state machine

A treatment is **resolved per invoice**, from the customer and the date, and then
**frozen onto that invoice**. A later change of rate, of registration, or of the
customer's status must never rewrite a historical invoice — the invoice keeps the
treatment that was applied to it.

```
                       ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
                       │  not_registered                          │
                       │  (value of taxable services <= RM500,000)│
                       │  → tax 0, no SST on any invoice          │
                       └───────────────┬──────────────────────────┘
                                       │ registration effective (a date)
                                       ▼
        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │  registered                                                 │
        │                                                             │
        │  customer is in MY ──→ taxable ──→ charge SST at the rate   │
        │  customer is outside MY ─┬─→ export_exempt (if determined) │
        │                          └─→ export_taxable ──→ charge SST  │
        └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The treatment codes

| Code | Meaning | Tax |
|---|---|---|
| `not_registered` | we are not a registered person; charging SST would be unlawful | 0 |
| `domestic_taxable` | the customer is in Malaysia; the supply is a taxable service | rate × net |
| `export_taxable` | the customer is outside Malaysia and the supply is **not** exempt | rate × net |
| `export_exempt` | the customer is outside Malaysia and the supply is an exempt export | 0 |
| `export_pending` | the customer is outside Malaysia and the export determination is **not yet made** | 0, and flagged |

`export_pending` is the important one. It is what the Cambodian tenant gets while the
determination is outstanding. It charges nothing (the safe direction), it is visible
**only to the admin** as a badge, and it is deliberately *not* shown to the customer —
we do not display our own uncertainty on a customer invoice.

`export_pending` also **does not count toward the RM500,000 threshold** — an exempt or
undetermined export is excluded from taxable services supplied in Malaysia. That is why
the threshold monitor must be able to exclude it, and why the export determination is
worth chasing before we approach the threshold, not after.

### Where a treatment comes from

The chain, in order:

1. `tax_registration_status` + `tax_registration_effective_date` (settings) — are we
   registered, and since when.
2. The **rate in force** on the invoice date (`tax_rates` table, effective-dated).
3. The **customer's country** (`website_customers.country`) — the same claim the
   payment-method gate already uses.
4. The **customer's export status** — an admin-entered determination, because the
   export question is a *per-customer factual* question, not something the server can
   derive from a country field.

> **Note:** `website_customers.country` is a claim in the profile. The payment methods
> already treat it that way (`services/payment_methods.country_code`, and
> `subscription.py:335` refuses MYR to a non-MY customer). The export determination must
> be **confirmed by an administrator**, never inferred from the country field at request
> time.

## 4. The rate is data

A rate is a row, not a constant:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `code` | e.g. `SST_IT_8` |
| `rate` | e.g. `0.08` |
| `effective_from` / `effective_to` | the window the rate applies to |
| `source` | the named authority for the rate (the gazette, the accountant's memo) |
| `note` | free text — what the determination actually said |

The invoice stores a **snapshot** of the rate it used (`tax_rate`, `tax_treatment`), so
the rate table can change and the history still explains itself. This mirrors the rule
already in the codebase for money and promotions: the *price* is per currency and
effective-dated, and the value carries its own provenance.

**The rate is not 8% by default.** 8% is the standard rate for IT services from
1 March 2024 — but it is entered, with a source, by an administrator.

## 5. The service period

**Every invoice states the service period it is for.** Two dates: the start and the end.

This is what makes the invoice *accountable* rather than merely *paid*. It is also what
the accounting service needs to recognise revenue over the period instead of on the
cash date — the deferred-revenue model in
[accounting/internal-tenant](../../accounting/internal-tenant) depends on the
service period being present.

The rule is a refusal: **an invoice that cannot state its service period is not
issued.** There is no "period unknown" invoice. That refusal is what keeps the
accounting reconciliation honest later.

### 5.1 Why the invoice has to carry its own period

The period lives on the subscription (`current_period_start` / `current_period_end`),
**and the renewal job advances it at the moment it issues the invoice**. So the
subscription's period is never the period of the invoice you are reading — after the
next renewal, the subscription points at a later period, and a connector that reads it
when it sees an old invoice recognises that invoice's revenue **in the wrong period**
(and for an annual plan, a year wrong).

So the period is **snapshotted onto the invoice**, as `period_start` and `period_end`.

> **The column names are `period_start` and `period_end`** — the same two columns that
> [accounting/purchase-path](../../accounting/purchase-path) already specifies on
> `invoices`, and the same ones the accounting program's Phase 0b work adds. The two
> programs must land **one** pair of columns. An earlier draft of this proposal used
> `service_period_start` / `service_period_end`; that was a mistake and it is
> corrected here.

### 5.2 The convention

`period_start` is **inclusive**, `period_end` is **exclusive** — matching the
subscription's own convention, so the two chain without an off-by-one and a renewal
invoice's `period_start` is the previous invoice's `period_end`.

The **display** is separate from the storage: a customer reads "1 Nov – 30 Nov", which
is `period_start` through `period_end` minus one day. And a service period is a range
of **calendar days**, not a moment — it must never be shifted by a timezone. The
rendering rule is spelled out in
[site-app-portal-changes §6.1](./site-app-portal-changes#61-the-dates--sitesrclibdatetimets).

### 5.3 Capture before you advance

The rule that makes the snapshot correct:

> **Capture the period from the values the code has already computed, at the moment it
> computes them — never by re-reading the subscription afterwards.**

This matters most in the renewal cron, which is where the period is advanced. The
trial-end invoice states the first **paid** period (beginning at `trial_ends_at`), and
the renewal invoice states the period **being invoiced** — captured before the
subscription moves on.

The period is captured at each of the **eleven invoice-creation sites** — see
[website-api-changes §4](./website-api-changes#4-the-eleven-invoice-sites) for the
table of which period each one uses, and
[§5](./website-api-changes#5-the-service-period) for the rule each one follows.

### 5.4 What the period is not

The period is **not** the invoice date, and it is **not** the subscription's current
period. Three different facts, and the invoice carries the third one:

| Fact | What it is | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| the invoice date | when the invoice was written | `invoice.created_at` |
| the subscription's current period | the period the subscription is *in now* | `subscription.current_period_start` / `_end` |
| **the invoice's service period** | the period **this money bought** | `invoice.period_start` / `_end` |

## 6. The RM500,000 threshold monitor

A monitor, not a gate. It watches the rolling 12-month value of taxable services and
warns **well before** the threshold, because the consequence of crossing it unprepared
is that we have been charging (or not charging) the wrong thing for months.

Three rules make the monitor trustworthy:

1. **Only taxable supplies count.** Revenue from `export_exempt` and `export_pending`
   is excluded — an exempt export is not a taxable service supplied in Malaysia.
2. **The money is never added across currencies.** The threshold is in ringgit, and the
   book is in USD and MYR. The monitor converts with a **dated FX rate table with a
   named source**, exactly the discipline in
   [multi-currency-money](../../multi-currency-money). The MYR-equivalent total is
   an **estimate**, and it says so.
3. **The monitor is not the ledger.** It is a warning light, not an accounting record.
   The authoritative figure is the accounting service's, and the two are reconciled —
   which is precisely what the internal tenant exists for.

The monitor reports three bands: below 70% (quiet), 70–100% (notice), above 100%
(over the threshold — registration is required, and the treatment table flips to
`registered`).

## 7. Inclusive or exclusive pricing

Two defensible designs. We recommend **exclusive** (net), and this is the decision we
want confirmed.

| | Exclusive (recommended) | Inclusive |
|---|---|---|
| Plan prices are | the **net** amount | the **gross** amount |
| The customer sees | `RM 100.00` + `+ SST` | `RM 108.00` `incl. SST` |
| The gross charged | net + tax | the price as listed |
| Net on the invoice | the price as listed | price ÷ 1.08 |
| Promo comparison | stored promo price = net | stored promo price = gross |
| Refund math | tax refunded with the service | tax already inside the number |
| Changing the rate later | the price list stays valid | **every price list is wrong** |

Exclusive wins because it survives a rate change and because the stored prices keep
meaning "the price of the service". Inclusive wins for the customer only in that one
number is shown — at the cost of a price list that must be rewritten whenever the rate
moves.

**Under exclusive pricing, the tax is added at the invoice, and the price list is
annotated, not changed.** The customer-facing price pages must then say that the price
excludes SST — which is a **display** obligation, and it lands in the site portal
document.

> **The registration status decides when this matters.** While `not_registered`, there
> is no tax and no annotation — saying "excl. SST" when we charge none is noise.

---

## 8. What the three surfaces share

These three rules must hold in every surface, or the three surfaces will disagree:

1. **The gross is what is paid.** `invoices.amount` keeps its current meaning: the
   amount that is charged. The tax is *added on top*, in new columns. Nothing that
   touches payment changes meaning.
2. **The net is the revenue; the tax is a liability.** Reporting splits them. The
   customer-facing gross is neither.
3. **Every money figure keeps its currency.** Tax is charged and reported in the
   invoice's own currency. Nothing crosses a currency boundary except through the
   dated FX table.

## 9. Rollout

Each step is independently shippable, and each one can be stopped at.

| Step | What ships | Flag | Effect on the customer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | schema + engine, everything dormant | `tax_module_enabled = off` | none |
| **1** | **shadow** — compute the tax, store it, show it to nobody | `tax_mode = shadow` | none |
| **2** | admin display — the admin sees the tax, the customer does not | `tax_mode = admin_only` | none |
| **3** | customer display — the tax line appears on the invoice, still charged as before | `tax_mode = display` | the invoice shows the tax line |
| **4** | **charging** — the gross is raised, the tax is collected | `tax_mode = charge` | the customer pays more |

Steps 0–2 are safe to run against production: no invoice amount changes. Step 3 changes
what is *printed*; step 4 changes what is *charged*.

The flag lives with the other settings (`settings` table), so the admin can flip it
without a deploy — and because `tax_mode` is a **value with an effective date**, the
rollout itself is auditable: "from the 1st of December we were in shadow mode, from the
15th we were charging."

## 10. Acceptance criteria (all three surfaces)

- [ ] No invoice amount changes while `tax_mode` is `shadow` or `admin_only`.
- [ ] Every invoice issued after the change carries a service period; the factory
      refuses to issue one that cannot.
- [ ] The service period is the period the money bought — not the invoice date, and
      not the subscription's current period.
- [ ] A renewal invoice states the period **being invoiced**; a trial-end invoice
      states the first **paid** period, beginning at `trial_ends_at`; a proration
      invoice states the **prorated segment**, not the whole period.
- [ ] A service period renders as calendar dates and is never shifted by a timezone.
- [ ] `invoices.amount` still equals the amount the payment provider charged, for
      every historical and every new invoice.
- [ ] The admin revenue report shows **net revenue** and **tax liability** as two
      different numbers, per currency.
- [ ] A MY customer sees the tax line; a customer under `export_exempt` or
      `export_pending` does not.
- [ ] The promo email still recognises a promotional price — the promo comparison uses
      the **net**, not the gross.
- [ ] The threshold monitor reports a MYR-equivalent taxable-services figure, states
      that it is an estimate, and excludes exempt/pending exports.
- [ ] The site portal prints the tax line in **en**, **km** and **zh**.
- [ ] The accounting connector's ledger events carry net, tax, gross and the service
      period, so the books can be fed without a second interpretation.

## 11. The decisions that need a professional answer

These are the questions I could not answer from the code, and I should not have answered
them from the code at all.

**For the accountant / customs authority:**

1. The legal entity's SST registration status, and whether registration has been applied
   for or is likely on the RM500,000 measure.
2. Whether the Cambodian tenant's subscription is an **export of services**, and if so
   whether the exemption conditions are met (the "outside scope" condition).
3. Whether our prices should be presented tax-inclusive or tax-exclusive, and whether
   the customer-facing price pages must carry the SST annotation.
4. The applicable service-tax rate for our supply, and its effective date — with the
   gazette reference for the source field.
5. Whether the tax on a **USD** invoice to a Malaysian customer is charged in USD
   (our default) or in MYR, and how the MYR liability is determined.
6. The **imported-services self-assessment** on our own vendor spend — which vendors
   supply us services from outside Malaysia, and whether the reverse charge applies to
   them. This is a *vendor* question, and it is a separate workstream from this module:
   it does not touch the invoice.
7. Whether an invoice must carry the SST registration number, and what other
   particulars SST requires on a tax invoice.

**For Kens (business decisions, not tax advice):**

8. Whether we register voluntarily before the threshold — if registration is cheap and
   we are heading for it, registering early is usually simpler than a mid-year
   effective date.
9. Whether the tax is charged in the invoice currency or in MYR (item 5 above is the
   tax view of this).
