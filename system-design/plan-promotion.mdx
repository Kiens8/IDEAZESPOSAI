# Plan Promotion System

IDEAZES POS AI can sell a plan at a **promotional price**. The promotion is an offer we make to
a **new customer** — it is not a change to the plan's price.

:::info
**A promotion applies to every payment method**

DuitNow, Touch 'n Go, Stripe and Pay Later. **Stripe is just a payment method** — a customer in
Malaysia and a customer in the United States must both be able to buy a promoted plan. The
promotion price is therefore stored **per currency**, and the Stripe renewal has to switch the
price back on its own.

**Existing customers are never affected.** A promotion that is published today reaches only
the subscriptions that start today.
:::

---

## Core Concepts

### Key Rule

**A promotion is the price of a NEW subscription's FIRST billing period. From the next cycle the
customer pays the regular price — automatically, with nobody touching anything.**

### What "first period" means

| The customer subscribes | The promotion covers | The next invoice |
|---|---|---|
| **Monthly** | the first **month** (Day 0 → Day 30) | Day 30 → the **regular** monthly price |
| **Annual** | the first **year** | the end of the year → the **regular** annual price |

So yes — **the next cycle goes back to the normal price.** The promotion is not a permanent
discount that you have to remember to switch off, and it never becomes a surprise on a
renewal invoice.

### The three prices

| Price | Where it lives | Who it applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **The regular price** | `plan_prices.price_monthly` / `price_annual` | every customer, every cycle |
| **The promotion price** | `plan_prices.promo_price_monthly` / `promo_price_annual` (nullable) | a **new** subscription's first period |
| **The snapshot** | `subscriptions.promo_price` + `subscriptions.promo_cycles_left` | the customer who was actually sold it |

`promo_cycles_left` = **the upcoming PAID invoices the promotion still covers**. A normal signup
pays its first invoice right away, so the promotion is used up immediately (`0`); a trial pays
its first invoice at the end of the trial, so its promotion is still waiting (`1`).

### Why the snapshot exists

The renewal cron re-prices **from the plan** on every cycle — today the price a customer paid is
not stored anywhere (`Subscription` has no price column).

That means a naive promotion — "if the plan has a promotion price, use it" — would **silently
re-price every existing subscriber** the moment you published the promotion, and it would repeat
**every month, forever** until you cleared the field.

The snapshot fixes both:

1. **Only new subscriptions get the promotion** (the customers who signed up before it was
   published keep the regular price).
2. **The promotion ends by itself** (the counter runs out at the first renewal).

### The confirmation email

The first paid invoice of a subscription sends the **subscription confirmation email**
(`app/services/subscription_email.py`). When the invoice was made out at the plan's
promotion price, the email carries:

- the amount they paid,
- the regular price it discounts (struck through),
- how much they saved (the amount and the %),
- when the offer ends.

The promotion is recognised **by the price** - the invoice amount equals the plan's
promotion price for that currency. That works for every payment method (HitPay, Pay
Later, Stripe - where the promotion was a one-time coupon). The invoice is the truth
about what they paid.

### The Stripe coupon - the API moved (Nov 3, 2026)

The Stripe API changed under us and **both changes failed silently** (the code catches the
exception and carries on) - so a Stripe customer was charged the **REGULAR price** while the
site advertised the promotion:

| What | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| The coupon's amount | `amount=` | `amount_off=` (the Basil API renamed it) |
| The discount | `subscription_data={'discounts': [...]}` | `discounts=[...]` **on the session** |

Both go through one helper: `website_api/app/services/stripe_coupon.py`
(`create_one_time_coupon` ✓) - it tries `amount_off` ✓ and falls back to `amount` ✓ for an
older API version ✓.

**The lesson:** a Stripe call that fails inside a `try/except` is a *money* bug, not a
cosmetic one. The helper is verified against the **real Stripe test API** ✓ (the SDK here
is 15.6.1 ✓, the key is a test key ✓).

### The grandfathering rule

> A promotion published today applies to subscriptions that **start** today.
> A subscription that is already running keeps the regular price at its next renewal.

The regular **price change** keeps its current behaviour — if you raise a plan's regular price,
every subscriber sees the new price at their next renewal. Only the **promotion** is
grandfathered.

## Data Model

### `plan_prices` — the promotion price (per currency)

| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `promo_price_monthly` | `Float`, nullable | the promoted monthly price |
| `promo_price_annual` | `Float`, nullable | the promoted annual price |

`NULL` = **no promotion** (both of them, or one of them — a plan can promote only its monthly
price if it wants to).

The promotion lives on the **price row**, not on the plan, because the prices are per currency:
the site's price switch shows `plan.prices[MYR]` next to `plan.prices[USD]`, so a promotion
must exist in the same currency as the price it discounts. Otherwise the card would show a USD
promotion next to a MYR price.

### `subscription_plans` — the label and the end date

| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `promo_price_monthly` | `Float`, nullable | the plan's own currency (the fallback when the plan has no `plan_prices` row) |
| `promo_price_annual` | `Float`, nullable | idem |
| `promo_label` | `String`, nullable | **the admin's own text** — the ribbon on the card ("Launch Offer", "Founding Customer") |
| `promo_ends_at` | `Date`, nullable | optional — "the offer ends on" |

### `subscriptions` — the snapshot

| Column | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `promo_price` | `Float`, nullable | the amount this customer was sold |
| `promo_cycles_left` | `Integer`, nullable | the upcoming PAID invoices still covered (0 after a normal signup, 1 for a trial) |
| `promo_currency` | `String`, nullable | the currency the promotion was in (a plan price change must not reinterpret it) |

### Nothing is backfilled

The migration adds **nullable** columns only. `NULL` means "no promotion", so **no existing
customer or invoice is touched** by the migration.

## The Flow

### 1. The admin publishes a promotion

`admin/src/pages/PlanManagement.jsx` — the create / edit plan modal:

- **Promotion price (monthly)** — blank = none
- **Promotion price (annual)** — blank = none
- **Promotion label** — your own text, shown on the ribbon (blank = the automatic "SAVE 33%")
- **Promotion ends on** — optional

The same two promotion fields exist in the **per-currency price editor**, because the promotion
price has to exist in every currency the plan is sold in.

> **The plan modal's promotion is the plan's OWN currency, and it is mirrored into that
> currency's price row.** The pricing cards read the `plan_prices` row (the prices are per
> currency), so a promotion that lives only on the plan would never reach the card. The
> backend writes it into the row of `plan.currency_code` when the plan is saved.

The plan list shows a **PROMO** chip, so you can see at a glance which plans are on promotion.

### 2. The site shows it

`site/src/app/[locale]/page.tsx` — the pricing cards:

- the **regular price struck through**, the **promotion price** next to it (the bigger number);
- **"Save 33%"** (computed) next to it;
- the **offer end date** when the admin set one ("Offer ends 30 Nov 2026");
- a **diagonal corner ribbon across the top-left corner** of the card (the classic corner
  banner: a red/orange gradient band with the fold at the corner) carrying the admin's label
  (or "SAVE 33%" when there is no label). The card's content moves down and right so the ribbon
  never covers the plan name;
- the whole thing reads from `plan.prices[currency]` → the API returns the promotion price in
  the same dictionary as the regular price.

The signup page and the portal's billing page show the same treatment, plus a line that says
what happens next: *"Your first invoice is RM60. From 4 Dec the regular price is RM80."*

### 3. The customer subscribes

The **server** decides the price — the browser never sends an amount:

```python
amount, currency = resolve_price_safe(db, plan, cycle, method, chosen_currency)
promo_amount, promo_currency = resolve_promo_price_safe(db, plan, cycle, method, chosen_currency)
if promo_amount is not None:
    amount = promo_amount              # the first period is the promotion
    sub.promo_price = promo_amount
    sub.promo_cycles_left = 1
    sub.promo_currency = promo_currency
```

The invoice is created at the promotion price, and the subscription keeps the snapshot.

### 4. The renewal — the regular price

At the first renewal the cron creates the next invoice:

```python
if sub.promo_cycles_left and sub.promo_cycles_left > 0:
    amount = sub.promo_price          # the promotion still covers an invoice
    sub.promo_cycles_left -= 1
else:
    amount = the regular price        # today's behaviour, unchanged
```

**The invoice at the end of the promotion period is the regular price.** The customer sees the
promotion on the first invoice only.

### 5. Stripe — the one-time coupon

Stripe bills automatically, so the cron does **not** create a local invoice for a Stripe
subscription — Stripe charges what its Checkout Session says.

The Stripe price stays the **regular** price and the promotion is applied as a **one-time
coupon** (`duration: 'once'`), which Stripe applies to the **first invoice only**:

| Moment | What Stripe charges | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The signup | the **promotion** price | the regular price minus the one-time coupon |
| The first renewal | the **regular** price | the coupon is spent |

This is the clean way to do it: **no price swap, no race** at the period boundary (the swap
approach would have to beat Stripe's own renewal invoice), and no dependency on the webhook or
the cron being timely.

```python
# the difference in the checkout's own currency
discount = round((regular_amount - promo_amount) * 100)
coupon = stripe.Coupon.create(
    amount=discount,
    currency=(resolved_currency or 'USD').lower(),
    duration='once',
    name=f"{plan.name} promotion",
)
stripe.checkout.Session.create(
    ...,
    subscription_data={'discounts': [{'coupon': coupon.id}]},
)
```

If the coupon cannot be created, the customer pays the **regular** price (never a wrong price)
and the failure is logged.

**The multi-currency case**: the Checkout Session uses the currency the customer chose
(`price_data.currency`) and the coupon is created in **that** currency ✓ — the promotion price
and the regular price are both resolved in it ✓.

### 6. What if the customer upgrades during the promotion?

The proration uses the subscriber's **effective** price:

```
the difference = (the target plan's REGULAR price − the subscriber's EFFECTIVE price) × days_left / days_total
```

- The subscriber's effective price = their promotion price while `promo_cycles_left > 0`,
  otherwise the regular price.
- The target plan's price = its **regular** price. **A mid-cycle upgrade does not receive a new
  promotion** — the promotion is an offer for a new subscription, not a discount on the
  difference.
- The promotion period itself is untouched by the upgrade: the counter keeps counting down, and
  the renewal after it is the regular price of the **new** plan.

## The Decision Table

### What a renewal invoice costs

| The subscription | The renewal amount |
|---|---|
| No promotion snapshot | the **regular** price (today's behaviour) |
| A snapshot with `promo_cycles_left > 0` | the **promotion** price, and the counter goes down |
| A snapshot with `promo_cycles_left = 0` | the **regular** price |
| A Stripe subscription | Stripe's own billing — the coupon covered the first invoice |

### What a new subscription costs

| The plan | The first invoice | The next invoice |
|---|---|---|
| No promotion | the regular price | the regular price |
| A promotion (monthly) | the promotion price | the regular price (the next month) |
| A promotion (annual) | the promotion price | the regular price (the next year) |

### What the access does

**Nothing changes.** The renewal system owns the access — the grace period, the statuses, the
blocking. A promotion is only a price; it never touches the access rules.

## Scenarios

### Scenario 1: New subscription - monthly - promotion - paid on time

```
Day 0:  The customer subscribes (Pro, MYR 80/month, the promotion is MYR 60).
        The invoice is created at MYR 60, the subscription snapshots
        promo_price = 60, promo_cycles_left = 1.
        The period is Day 0 → Day 30.
Day 7:  The warning email (the renewal system's own schedule).
Day 14: The invoice is due.
Day 14: The customer pays MYR 60 → paid → the access is Full.
Day 30: The renewal invoice is created at **MYR 80** (the regular price).
        promo_cycles_left becomes 0.
```

**Result:** one month at MYR 60, then the regular price.

### Scenario 2: New subscription - annual - promotion - paid on time

The same flow with the annual cycle: the first invoice is the annual promotion price, and the
invoice at the end of the year is the regular annual price.

### Scenario 3: The renewal after the promotion

The invoice at the end of the promotion period is the **regular** price. The customer is never
surprised: the portal and the billing page say what the next invoice will be.

### Scenario 4: A new subscription while the promotion is running

The plan has a promotion price → the new customer's first invoice is at the promotion price ✓.
The promotion applies to everybody who subscribes while it is published.

### Scenario 5: A subscription that started BEFORE the promotion

```
Before the promotion:  the customer subscribed at the regular MYR 80.
Today:                 the admin publishes the promotion (MYR 60).
The customer's next renewal invoice: **MYR 80** (the regular price).
```

**The grandfathering rule.** The running subscription has no snapshot → the cron uses the plan's
regular price ✓. Nothing changes for the customer, and nothing has to be migrated.

### Scenario 6: An upgrade mid-cycle from a promotion subscriber

```
Pro (MYR 80) with the promotion (MYR 60), 10 of 30 days left.
The customer upgrades to Enterprise (MYR 200).
The difference = (200 − 60) × 10 / 30 = **MYR 46.67**
```

The old price is the subscriber's **effective** price (the promotion), not the regular price —
otherwise the customer would be charged the difference against a price they never agreed to pay.

### Scenario 7: An upgrade mid-cycle TO a promoted plan

The target plan has a promotion (say RM150 instead of RM200) → the **difference is still
computed from the regular prices**: (200 − 80) × days/total. The promotion is an offer for a new
subscription; it does not discount the difference of a mid-cycle upgrade. The customer's renewal
at the end of the cycle is the **regular** price of the new plan.

### Scenario 8: A Stripe subscription with a promotion

```
Day 0:  The Checkout Session is created with the REGULAR price and a
        one-time coupon for the difference. The customer pays the
        promotion price. The subscription snapshots promo_price and
        promo_cycles_left = 0.
Day 30: The coupon is spent → the next Stripe invoice is the regular price.
```

No price swap, no race at the period end ✓.

### Scenario 9: The promotion is removed while a customer is in their promotion period

The customer's snapshot holds: their first invoice is at the promotion price (already paid) and
their next invoice is the regular price — the same result either way ✓. Removing a promotion
never changes an invoice that has already been issued.

### Scenario 10: The promotion has no label

The ribbon falls back to the automatic **"SAVE 33%"** (computed from the two prices).

### Scenario 11: A trial that converts into a promotion

The trial's first **paid** invoice (at the end of the trial) is the promotion price — the trial
itself is free, so the promotion starts with the first period they actually pay for.

### Scenario 12: A free plan

A free plan (Starter, RM0) cannot have a promotion — the validation rejects it.

## Validation Rules

| Rule | The message |
|---|---|
| The promotion must be greater than 0 | "The promotion price must be greater than zero" |
| The promotion must be **lower** than the regular price | "The promotion price must be lower than the regular price" |
| The annual promotion must be lower than the annual regular price | idem |
| The promotion is only for a paid plan | "A free plan cannot have a promotion" |
| The promotion price is **per currency** | it is entered in the per-currency price editor |
| Blank = no promotion | `NULL` |

---

## What Does NOT Change

- **The renewal system** — the access, the grace period, the statuses, the blocking. Untouched.
  A promotion is only a price.
- **The regular price change** — it still reaches every subscriber at their next renewal ✓.
- **The proration formula** — the same shape, only the old price is the effective price ✓.
- **The invoice statuses** — `pending`, `paid`, `failed`, `forgiven`, `refunded`, `cancelled` ✓.
- **The currency rules** — DuitNow and Touch 'n Go settle in MYR, Stripe keeps the customer's
  chosen currency ✓.
